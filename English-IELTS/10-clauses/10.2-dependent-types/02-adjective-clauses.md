# Adjective Clauses (Relative Clauses)

**Adjective clauses, also known as relative clauses, are a type of dependent clause that function as adjectives within a sentence. They provide additional information about a noun or pronoun, helping to describe or identify it more specifically.**

Here are some further details about adjective clauses:

## 1. Introduction

- Adjective clauses are introduced by **relative pronouns** or **relative adverbs**.
- The choice of relative pronoun or adverb depends on the role and function of the clause within the sentence.

### Relative Pronouns

| Pronoun | Refers To | Example |
|----------|------------|---------|
| **who** | People (subject) | "The person **who** called me is my friend." |
| **whom** | People (object) | "The person **whom** I met yesterday was kind." |
| **whose** | Possession | "The student **whose** project won received an award." |
| **which** | Things or animals | "The book **which** I bought is excellent." |
| **that** | People, animals, or things | "The car **that** is parked outside is mine." |

### Relative Adverbs

| Adverb | Function | Example |
|---------|----------|---------|
| **where** | Place | "This is the place **where** we met." |
| **when** | Time | "I remember the day **when** we graduated." |
| **why** | Reason | "She explained the reason **why** she left." |

### Examples

- **"The person who called me is my friend."**
- **"This is the place where we met."**
- **"I remember the year when we first met."**

---

## 2. Modifying Function

- Adjective clauses modify a noun or pronoun by providing additional information about it.
- They help describe, identify, or specify the noun or pronoun they are associated with.
- Because they function as adjectives, they answer questions such as:
  - Which one?
  - What kind?
  - Whose?

### Examples

- **"The book that she is reading is interesting."**
  - Adjective clause: *"that she is reading"*
  - Modifies: *book*

- **"I met the person who won the competition."**
  - Adjective clause: *"who won the competition"*
  - Modifies: *person*

- **"The house that overlooks the lake is beautiful."**
  - Adjective clause: *"that overlooks the lake"*
  - Modifies: *house*

---

## 3. Placement

- Adjective clauses usually appear immediately after the noun or pronoun they modify.
- Their placement helps clearly connect the description to the correct noun.

### Examples

- **"The boy who is playing soccer is my neighbor."**
- **"The woman whose car was stolen contacted the police."**
- **"The building that was recently renovated looks amazing."**

### Important Rule

The adjective clause should be placed as close as possible to the noun it modifies to avoid confusion.

---

## 4. Restrictive Adjective Clauses

- Restrictive adjective clauses provide **essential information** needed to identify the noun.
- They are **not separated by commas**.
- Removing them changes the meaning of the sentence.

### Examples

- **"The car that is parked outside is mine."**
- **"The students who studied hard passed the exam."**
- **"The restaurant that serves Italian food is always busy."**

### Analysis

Sentence:

> "The students who studied hard passed the exam."

Adjective clause:

- *"who studied hard"*

Function:

- Identifies which students passed the exam.

If removed:

> "The students passed the exam."

The meaning changes because the specific group is no longer identified.

---

## 5. Nonrestrictive Adjective Clauses

- Nonrestrictive adjective clauses provide **additional, nonessential information**.
- They are **set off by commas**.
- Removing them does not change the basic meaning of the sentence.

### Examples

- **"My brother, who is a doctor, lives in London."**
- **"Paris, which is the capital of France, attracts millions of tourists."**
- **"Maria, whose research won an award, will present at the conference."**

### Analysis

Sentence:

> "My brother, who is a doctor, lives in London."

Adjective clause:

- *"who is a doctor"*

Function:

- Adds extra information about the brother.

If removed:

> "My brother lives in London."

The core meaning remains unchanged.

---

## 6. Restrictive vs. Nonrestrictive Clauses

| Feature | Restrictive Clause | Nonrestrictive Clause |
|----------|-------------------|-----------------------|
| Importance | Essential information | Additional information |
| Commas | No commas | Uses commas |
| Effect if Removed | Meaning changes | Main meaning remains |
| Purpose | Identifies the noun | Describes the noun |
| Example | *"The employee who arrived late apologized."* | *"John, who arrived late, apologized."* |

---

## 7. Relative Pronouns as Subjects and Objects

### Subject Relative Pronoun

The relative pronoun acts as the subject of the adjective clause.

#### Example

> "The woman who teaches mathematics is my aunt."

- Relative pronoun: *who*
- Subject of the clause: *who teaches mathematics*

---

### Object Relative Pronoun

The relative pronoun acts as the object of the verb.

#### Example

> "The woman whom I met yesterday is my aunt."

- Relative pronoun: *whom*
- Object of: *met*

---

### Omitted Relative Pronouns

When the relative pronoun functions as an object, it can often be omitted in restrictive clauses.

#### Examples

- **"The book that I bought is excellent."**
- **"The book I bought is excellent."**

Both sentences are grammatically correct.

---

## 8. Common Errors

### Incorrect Relative Pronoun

❌ **"The book who is on the table is mine."**

✅ **"The book that is on the table is mine."**

✅ **"The book which is on the table is mine."**

---

### Incorrect Comma Usage

❌ **"The students, who studied hard, passed the exam."**

This suggests that all students studied hard and passed.

✅ **"The students who studied hard passed the exam."**

Only the students who studied hard passed.

---

### Misplaced Clause

❌ **"She spoke with the teacher in the hallway who teaches biology."**

This could imply that the hallway teaches biology.

✅ **"She spoke with the teacher who teaches biology in the hallway."**

Or:

✅ **"In the hallway, she spoke with the teacher who teaches biology."**

---

## 9. Benefits of Using Adjective Clauses

Adjective clauses help writers:

- Add detailed information efficiently
- Avoid repetitive sentences
- Create more sophisticated sentence structures
- Clarify and identify specific nouns
- Improve sentence flow and readability

### Example

Instead of:

> "I met a scientist. The scientist won a Nobel Prize."

You can write:

> "I met a scientist who won a Nobel Prize."

---

## Summary

**Adjective clauses** (or **relative clauses**) are dependent clauses that function as adjectives by modifying nouns or pronouns. They are introduced by **relative pronouns** (*who, whom, whose, which, that*) or **relative adverbs** (*where, when, why*). Adjective clauses can be **restrictive**, providing essential identifying information without commas, or **nonrestrictive**, adding extra descriptive information and set off by commas. Mastering adjective clauses allows writers to create clearer, more precise, and more sophisticated sentences.
