# Noun Clauses

**Noun clauses are a type of dependent clause that function as nouns within a sentence. They can serve various roles such as the subject, object, or complement of a verb or preposition.**

Here are some key details about noun clauses:

## 1. Introduction

- Noun clauses are introduced by words or phrases such as:
  - **that**
  - **whether**
  - **if**
  - **who**
  - **whom**
  - **whose**
  - **what**
  - **which**
  - **where**
  - **when**
  - **how**
  - **why**

- The introductory word establishes the purpose or nature of the clause.

### Examples

- **That**
  > "I know that he is coming."

- **Whether**
  > "She asked whether it was true."

- **If**
  > "I wonder if he will be there."

- **What**
  > "What she said surprised everyone."

- **Why**
  > "I understand why she left."

---

## 2. Function

- Noun clauses function as nouns within a sentence.
- They can serve as:
  - Subject
  - Direct object
  - Indirect object
  - Subject complement
  - Object complement
  - Object of a preposition

- A noun clause can often replace:
  - A single noun
  - A noun phrase
  - An entire idea

### Examples

| Function | Example |
|-----------|---------|
| Subject | **"What she said surprised me."** |
| Direct Object | **"I know what she said."** |
| Subject Complement | **"The truth is that he lied."** |
| Object of a Preposition | **"We talked about what happened."** |

---

## 3. Noun Clauses as Subjects

A noun clause may function as the subject of a sentence.

### Examples

- **"What she said surprised me."**
- **"Whoever arrives first will open the door."**
- **"Why he left remains a mystery."**

### Analysis

Sentence:

> "What she said surprised me."

Subject:

- *"What she said"*

Verb:

- *surprised*

Object:

- *me*

---

## 4. Noun Clauses as Objects

Noun clauses frequently function as direct objects.

### Examples

- **"I know what she said."**
- **"They discovered where the treasure was hidden."**
- **"She explained why she was late."**

### Analysis

Sentence:

> "I know what she said."

Verb:

- *know*

Direct object:

- *"what she said"*

---

## 5. Noun Clauses as Subject Complements

A noun clause may follow a linking verb and rename or explain the subject.

### Examples

- **"The problem is that we are running out of time."**
- **"The truth is that he lied."**
- **"My hope is that everyone succeeds."**

### Analysis

Sentence:

> "The truth is that he lied."

Subject:

- *The truth*

Linking verb:

- *is*

Subject complement:

- *"that he lied"*

---

## 6. Noun Clauses as Objects of Prepositions

A noun clause may serve as the object of a preposition.

### Examples

- **"We talked about what happened."**
- **"She is responsible for whatever occurs."**
- **"The decision depends on whether they agree."**

### Analysis

Sentence:

> "The decision depends on whether they agree."

Preposition:

- *on*

Object of the preposition:

- *"whether they agree"*

---

## 7. Types of Noun Clauses

### A. Declarative Noun Clauses

These express statements or facts.

Often introduced by:

- that

### Examples

- **"I believe that she is right."**
- **"He said that he would arrive early."**
- **"We know that the project is complete."**

---

### B. Interrogative Noun Clauses

These express indirect questions.

Often introduced by:

- whether
- if
- who
- what
- where
- when
- why
- how

### Examples

- **"He asked where she was going."**
- **"I wonder how they solved the problem."**
- **"She wants to know whether the meeting was canceled."**

### Note

Indirect questions follow statement word order.

❌ **"I wonder where is she going."**

✅ **"I wonder where she is going."**

---

### C. Exclamatory Noun Clauses

These express strong feelings, surprise, or emphasis.

### Examples

- **"It's amazing how quickly time flies."**
- **"I can't believe how beautiful the view is."**
- **"It's incredible what they accomplished."**

---

## 8. Common Introductory Words

### That-Clause

Used to report information, beliefs, statements, or facts.

#### Examples

- **"I know that he is honest."**
- **"She believes that success requires effort."**

---

### Whether/If Clause

Used for indirect yes/no questions.

#### Examples

- **"I don't know whether he will come."**
- **"She asked if the store was open."**

---

### Wh-Clauses

Introduced by:

- who
- whom
- whose
- what
- which
- where
- when
- why
- how

#### Examples

- **"What you said is important."**
- **"I know where they live."**
- **"She explained why she left."**

---

## 9. Noun Clauses vs. Other Dependent Clauses

| Feature | Noun Clause | Adjective Clause | Adverb Clause |
|----------|------------|------------------|---------------|
| Function | Noun | Adjective | Adverb |
| Modifies | Acts as a noun | Noun or pronoun | Verb, adjective, or adverb |
| Introduced By | That, what, who, whether, etc. | Who, whom, whose, which, that | Because, if, when, although, etc. |
| Example | *"What she said"* | *"who won the race"* | *"because it was raining"* |

---

## 10. Common Errors

### Sentence Fragment

❌ **"That he is coming."**

This is only a noun clause and does not form a complete sentence.

✅ **"I know that he is coming."**

---

### Incorrect Word Order

❌ **"I wonder where is the station."**

✅ **"I wonder where the station is."**

Indirect questions use normal statement word order.

---

### Unnecessary "That"

Sometimes **that** may be omitted in informal writing.

#### Examples

- **"I think that she is right."**
- **"I think she is right."**

Both forms are correct.

---

## 11. Benefits of Using Noun Clauses

Noun clauses help writers:

- Express complex ideas
- Report speech and thoughts
- Ask indirect questions
- Convey uncertainty or possibility
- Connect related ideas smoothly
- Create more sophisticated sentence structures

### Example

Instead of:

> "She said something. It surprised everyone."

You can write:

> "What she said surprised everyone."

This creates a more concise and elegant sentence.

---

## Summary

**Noun clauses** are dependent clauses that function as **nouns** within a sentence. They can serve as **subjects, objects, complements,** or **objects of prepositions**. Introduced by words such as **that, whether, if, what, who, where, when, why,** and **how**, noun clauses allow speakers and writers to express complex ideas, report information, ask indirect questions, and connect thoughts more effectively. Mastering noun clauses is an important step toward creating clear, sophisticated, and grammatically accurate sentences.
