# Relative Clauses (Adjective Clauses)

**Relative clauses, also known as adjective clauses, are a type of dependent clause that provide additional information about a noun or pronoun. They function as adjectives within a sentence, modifying the noun or pronoun they are associated with.**

Here are some key details about relative clauses:

## 1. Introduction

- Relative clauses are introduced by **relative pronouns** or **relative adverbs**.
- The choice of relative pronoun or adverb depends on the role and function of the clause within the sentence.

### Common Relative Pronouns

| Relative Pronoun | Refers To | Example |
|------------------|------------|---------|
| **who** | People (subject) | "The person **who** called me is my friend." |
| **whom** | People (object) | "The person **whom** I met was kind." |
| **whose** | Possession | "The student **whose** project won received a prize." |
| **which** | Animals or things | "The book **which** I bought is excellent." |
| **that** | People, animals, or things (restrictive clauses) | "The car **that** is parked outside is mine." |

### Common Relative Adverbs

| Relative Adverb | Function | Example |
|-----------------|----------|---------|
| **where** | Place | "This is the place **where** we met." |
| **when** | Time | "I remember the day **when** we graduated." |
| **why** | Reason | "She explained the reason **why** she left." |

### Examples

- **"The person who called me is my friend."**
- **"This is the place where we met."**
- **"I remember the year when we first traveled abroad."**

---

## 2. Function

- Relative clauses provide additional information about the noun or pronoun they modify.
- They function as **adjectives**, helping describe or identify the noun more precisely.

### Examples

- **"The book that she is reading is interesting."**
  - Relative clause: *"that she is reading"*
  - Modifies: *book*

- **"I met the person who won the competition."**
  - Relative clause: *"who won the competition"*
  - Modifies: *person*

- **"The house which overlooks the ocean is expensive."**
  - Relative clause: *"which overlooks the ocean"*
  - Modifies: *house*

---

## 3. Types of Relative Clauses

### A. Restrictive Relative Clauses

- Restrictive relative clauses provide **essential information**.
- They identify exactly which person, place, thing, or idea is being discussed.
- They are **not separated by commas**.

### Examples

- **"The car that is parked outside is mine."**
- **"The student who earned the highest score received a scholarship."**
- **"The restaurant that serves Italian food is very popular."**

### Key Characteristic

If the clause is removed, important identifying information is lost.

#### Example

- Original:
  > "The students who studied passed the exam."

- Without the clause:
  > "The students passed the exam."

The meaning changes because the clause identifies a specific group of students.

---

### B. Nonrestrictive Relative Clauses

- Nonrestrictive relative clauses provide **additional, nonessential information**.
- They add descriptive details but do not identify the noun.
- They are **set off by commas**.

### Examples

- **"My brother, who is a doctor, lives in London."**
- **"The Eiffel Tower, which is one of the most famous landmarks in the world, attracts millions of visitors."**
- **"Maria, whose research won an award, will present her findings tomorrow."**

### Key Characteristic

The sentence remains meaningful if the clause is removed.

#### Example

- Original:
  > "My brother, who is a doctor, lives in London."

- Without the clause:
  > "My brother lives in London."

The essential meaning remains unchanged.

---

## 4. Placement

Relative clauses are usually placed immediately after the noun or pronoun they modify.

### Examples

- **"The boy who is playing soccer is my neighbor."**
- **"The woman whose car was stolen contacted the police."**
- **"The house that was recently renovated looks beautiful."**

### End-of-Sentence Position

Relative clauses often appear near the end of a sentence because they follow the noun they modify.

#### Example

> "I bought a new car, which is blue."

Relative clause:

- *"which is blue"*

Modifies:

- *car*

---

## 5. Relative Pronouns as Subjects and Objects

### As the Subject of the Relative Clause

> "The woman who teaches English is my aunt."

- Relative pronoun: *who*
- Subject of the clause: *who teaches English*

### As the Object of the Relative Clause

> "The woman whom I met yesterday is my aunt."

- Relative pronoun: *whom*
- Object of the verb: *met*

### Omitted Relative Pronouns

In restrictive clauses, the relative pronoun can sometimes be omitted when it functions as an object.

#### Examples

- **"The book that I bought is excellent."**
- **"The book I bought is excellent."**

Both are correct.

---

## 6. Relative Clauses with Prepositions

A relative clause may contain a preposition.

### Formal Style

> "The person to whom I spoke was helpful."

### Informal Style

> "The person whom I spoke to was helpful."

### Most Common Everyday Form

> "The person I spoke to was helpful."

---

## 7. Common Errors

### Incorrect Comma Usage

❌ **"The students, who studied hard, passed the exam."**

This suggests that **all students** studied hard and passed.

### Correct Restrictive Form

✅ **"The students who studied hard passed the exam."**

Only the students who studied hard passed.

---

### Incorrect Relative Pronoun Choice

❌ **"The book who is on the table is mine."**

### Correct

✅ **"The book that is on the table is mine."**

✅ **"The book which is on the table is mine."**

---

## 8. Relative Clauses vs. Other Dependent Clauses

| Feature | Relative Clause | Noun Clause | Adverb Clause |
|----------|----------------|-------------|---------------|
| Function | Adjective | Noun | Adverb |
| Modifies | Noun or pronoun | Functions as a noun | Modifies a verb, adjective, or adverb |
| Introduced By | Relative pronouns/adverbs | That, what, whether, etc. | Because, if, when, although, etc. |
| Example | *"The book that I bought"* | *"What she said"* | *"Because it was raining"* |

---

## Summary

**Relative clauses** (or **adjective clauses**) are dependent clauses that modify a noun or pronoun by providing additional information. They are introduced by **relative pronouns** (*who, whom, whose, which, that*) or **relative adverbs** (*where, when, why*). Relative clauses may be **restrictive**, providing essential identifying information without commas, or **nonrestrictive**, adding extra information and set off by commas. Mastering relative clauses helps create more precise, descriptive, and sophisticated sentences.
