# Dependent Clauses

**A dependent clause, also known as a subordinate clause, is a type of clause that cannot stand alone as a complete sentence. It relies on an independent clause to convey its meaning and function within a sentence.**

Here are some key details about dependent clauses:

## 1. Relationship to Independent Clauses

- Dependent clauses cannot stand alone as complete sentences because they do not express a complete thought.
- They rely on an independent clause to provide the necessary information for their meaning.

### Examples

- **"Because it was raining"** *(dependent clause)*
- **"After she finished her work"** *(dependent clause)*
- **"Although he was tired"** *(dependent clause)*

### Complete Sentences

To form a complete sentence, a dependent clause must be connected to an independent clause.

#### Examples

- **"Because it was raining, we stayed indoors."**
- **"After she finished her work, she went for a walk."**
- **"Although he was tired, he continued working."**

---

## 2. Types of Dependent Clauses

Dependent clauses can function as **nouns**, **adjectives**, or **adverbs** within a sentence.

### A. Noun Clauses

- Noun clauses function as nouns within a sentence.
- They can act as subjects, objects, complements, or objects of prepositions.

#### Examples

- **"What she said surprised everyone."**
  - Noun clause: *"What she said"*
  - Function: Subject

- **"I know what she said."**
  - Noun clause: *"what she said"*
  - Function: Direct object

- **"The problem is that we are running out of time."**
  - Noun clause: *"that we are running out of time"*
  - Function: Subject complement

---

### B. Adjective Clauses (Relative Clauses)

- Adjective clauses modify nouns or pronouns.
- They provide additional information or description.

#### Examples

- **"The book that she is reading is fascinating."**
  - Adjective clause: *"that she is reading"*
  - Modifies: *book*

- **"The student who won the award gave a speech."**
  - Adjective clause: *"who won the award"*
  - Modifies: *student*

- **"The house which was built in 1900 has been restored."**
  - Adjective clause: *"which was built in 1900"*
  - Modifies: *house*

---

### C. Adverb Clauses

- Adverb clauses modify verbs, adjectives, or adverbs.
- They provide information about:
  - Time
  - Place
  - Reason
  - Condition
  - Purpose
  - Contrast
  - Manner

#### Examples

- **"He left when the movie ended."**
  - Adverb clause: *"when the movie ended"*
  - Function: Time

- **"We stayed inside because it was raining."**
  - Adverb clause: *"because it was raining"*
  - Function: Reason

- **"If you study hard, you will succeed."**
  - Adverb clause: *"if you study hard"*
  - Function: Condition

---

## 3. Subordinating Conjunctions

- Dependent clauses are often introduced by **subordinating conjunctions**.
- These words establish a relationship between the dependent clause and the independent clause.

### Common Subordinating Conjunctions

#### Time

- after
- before
- when
- while
- since
- until

#### Cause and Reason

- because
- since
- as

#### Condition

- if
- unless
- provided that

#### Contrast

- although
- though
- even though
- whereas

#### Purpose

- so that
- in order that

### Examples

- **"Because it was raining, we stayed indoors."**
- **"He went to the party even though he was tired."**
- **"If you call me, I will answer."**

---

## 4. Placement Within Sentences

Dependent clauses can appear at the **beginning**, **middle**, or **end** of a sentence.

### Beginning of the Sentence

When a dependent clause comes first, it is usually followed by a comma.

#### Example

> "After she finished her work, she went for a walk."

---

### End of the Sentence

When the dependent clause follows the independent clause, a comma is often unnecessary.

#### Example

> "She went for a walk after she finished her work."

---

### Middle of the Sentence

A dependent clause may also interrupt the independent clause.

#### Example

> "The project, although it was difficult, was completed successfully."

---

## 6. Common Errors

### Sentence Fragment

A dependent clause by itself is a sentence fragment.

❌ **"Because I was tired."**

This is incomplete because it does not express a complete thought.

✅ **"Because I was tired, I went to bed early."**

---

### Misplaced Clause

The clause should clearly relate to the word or idea it modifies.

❌ **"She almost missed the train because she was running."**

This may create ambiguity depending on the context.

✅ **"Because she was running late, she almost missed the train."**

---

## 7. Independent vs. Dependent Clauses

| Feature | Independent Clause | Dependent Clause |
|----------|-------------------|------------------|
| Contains a Subject and Verb | Yes | Yes |
| Expresses a Complete Thought | Yes | No |
| Can Stand Alone as a Sentence | Yes | No |
| Requires Another Clause | No | Yes |
| Example | *"She finished her work."* | *"After she finished her work"* |

---

## Summary

A **dependent clause** (or **subordinate clause**) is a group of words that contains a **subject** and a **verb** but does **not express a complete thought**. Because it cannot stand alone as a sentence, it must be connected to an **independent clause**. Dependent clauses can function as **nouns**, **adjectives**, or **adverbs**, and they are often introduced by **subordinating conjunctions** or **relative pronouns**. Understanding dependent clauses is essential for building clear, detailed, and grammatically correct **complex sentences**.
