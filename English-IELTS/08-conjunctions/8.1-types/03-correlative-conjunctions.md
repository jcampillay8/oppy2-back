# Correlative Conjunctions

**Correlative conjunctions** are pairs of words that work together to connect **words, phrases, or clauses of equal grammatical importance**.

They create balance and show the relationship between the connected elements.

Unlike coordinating conjunctions such as `and`, `but`, or `or`, correlative conjunctions always work as a **pair**.

---

# 1. Common Correlative Conjunctions

The most common pairs are:

- `either ... or`
- `neither ... nor`
- `both ... and`
- `not only ... but also`
- `whether ... or`

---

# 2. "Either ... or"

**"Either ... or"** presents a **choice or alternative between two possibilities**.

### Structure

> **Either + option 1 + or + option 2**

### Examples

> "**Either** you come with us **or** you stay here."

> "You can choose **either** coffee **or** tea."

> "We can **either** take the bus **or** walk."

### Key Idea

> **EITHER ... OR → Choice / Alternative**

---

# 3. "Neither ... nor"

**"Neither ... nor"** expresses a **negative choice involving two options**.

It means that **not one and not the other** is true.

### Structure

> **Neither + option 1 + nor + option 2**

### Examples

> "She is **neither** a singer **nor** a dancer."

> "**Neither** John **nor** Mary attended the meeting."

> "He likes **neither** coffee **nor** tea."

### Key Idea

> **NEITHER ... NOR → Negative alternatives**

---

# 4. "Both ... and"

**"Both ... and"** connects two elements and indicates that **the two are included or true**.

### Structure

> **Both + element 1 + and + element 2**

### Examples

> "She is **both** intelligent **and** talented."

> "I speak **both** English **and** Spanish."

> "**Both** the manager **and** the employees attended the meeting."

### Key Idea

> **BOTH ... AND → Two elements together**

---

# 5. "Not only ... but also"

**"Not only ... but also"** emphasizes that **two things are true**, often highlighting the second element.

### Structure

> **Not only + element 1 + but also + element 2**

### Examples

> "He is **not only** a great athlete **but also** an excellent student."

> "She is **not only** intelligent **but also** hardworking."

> "The company **not only** reduced costs **but also** increased productivity."

### Key Idea

> **NOT ONLY ... BUT ALSO → Addition + Emphasis**

---

# 6. "Whether ... or"

**"Whether ... or"** introduces **alternative possibilities or situations**.

It often expresses uncertainty or indicates that the result remains the same regardless of which possibility is true.

### Structure

> **Whether + possibility 1 + or + possibility 2**

### Examples

> "**Whether** you like it **or** not, you have to complete the assignment."

> "I don't know **whether** he will come **or** stay home."

> "**Whether** we travel by car **or** by train, the journey will take several hours."

### Key Idea

> **WHETHER ... OR → Alternative possibilities**

---

# 7. Parallel Structure

One of the most important rules when using correlative conjunctions is **parallel structure**.

The elements connected by the two conjunctions should normally have the **same grammatical form**.

### Correct

> "She likes **both reading and writing**."

Both elements use gerunds:

- `reading`
- `writing`

### Incorrect

> ❌ "She likes **both reading and to write**."

The forms are different:

- `reading` → gerund
- `to write` → infinitive

### Better

> "She likes **both reading and writing**."

Or:

> "She likes **both to read and to write**."

---

# 8. Parallel Structure with "Either ... or"

The same principle applies to `either ... or`.

### Correct

> "You can **either study or work**."

Both elements are base-form verbs:

- `study`
- `work`

### Correct

> "You can **either study at home or work at the library**."

Both elements are verb phrases.

### Incorrect

> ❌ "You can **either study at home or working at the library**."

The structures are not parallel.

### Corrected

> "You can **either study at home or work at the library**."

---

# 9. Parallel Structure with "Not Only ... But Also"

### Correct

> "She is **not only intelligent but also hardworking**."

Both elements are adjectives:

- `intelligent`
- `hardworking`

### Correct

> "He **not only studies hard but also exercises regularly**."

Both elements are verb phrases:

- `studies hard`
- `exercises regularly`

### Incorrect

> ❌ "He **not only studies hard but also regular exercise**."

The structures are different.

### Corrected

> "He **not only studies hard but also exercises regularly**."

---

# 10. Position of Correlative Conjunctions

The two parts of the conjunction should normally be placed **immediately before the elements they connect**.

### Example

> "She is **both** intelligent **and** talented."
