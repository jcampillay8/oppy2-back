# Subordinating Conjunctions vs. Conjunctive Adverbs

**Subordinating conjunctions** and **conjunctive adverbs** are both used to connect ideas and clauses, but they perform different grammatical functions.

The main difference is:

> **Subordinating conjunction → creates a dependent clause.**

> **Conjunctive adverb → connects ideas between independent clauses.**

---

# 1. Main Difference

| **Feature** | **Subordinating Conjunction** | **Conjunctive Adverb** |
|---|---|---|
| **Function** | Introduces a dependent clause | Shows the logical relationship between independent clauses |
| **Clause Type** | Creates a dependent clause | Connects independent clauses |
| **Relationship** | Creates a hierarchical relationship | Creates a transitional/logical relationship |
| **Placement** | Usually at the beginning of the dependent clause | Usually at the beginning or middle of an independent clause |
| **Punctuation** | Comma is often used when the dependent clause comes first | Usually `;` before and `,` after when connecting two independent clauses |
| **Examples** | `although`, `because`, `if`, `when`, `while` | `however`, `therefore`, `moreover`, `consequently` |

---

# 2. Subordinating Conjunctions

A **subordinating conjunction** introduces a **dependent clause**.

A dependent clause cannot normally stand alone as a complete sentence.

### Common Subordinating Conjunctions

- `although`
- `because`
- `if`
- `when`
- `while`
- `after`
- `before`
- `since`
- `unless`
- `until`
- `though`

---
