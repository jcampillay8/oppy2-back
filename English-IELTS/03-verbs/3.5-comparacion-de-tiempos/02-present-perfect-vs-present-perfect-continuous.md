# Present Perfect vs. Present Perfect Continuous

## Present Perfect Tense

**Focus:** The result, completion, experience, or present relevance of an action.

**Structure:**

Subject + have/has + past participle

**Example:**

I have visited that city before.

**Key idea:** Focus on **what has happened or what has been completed**.

---

## Present Perfect Continuous Tense

**Focus:** The duration or continuity of an action that started in the past and continues to the present or has recently stopped.

**Structure:**

Subject + have/has + been + verb-ing

**Example:**

She has been studying for three hours.

**Key idea:** Focus on **how long an action has been happening**.

---

# Key Differences

| | **Present Perfect** | **Present Perfect Continuous** |
|---|---|---|
| **Main focus** | Result or completion | Duration or continuity |
| **Action** | Often completed | Ongoing or recently completed |
| **Time** | Past action with present relevance | Action started in the past and continues toward the present |
| **Structure** | have/has + past participle | have/has + been + verb-ing |
| **Example** | I have cleaned the room. | I have been cleaning the room. |

# Compare the Meaning

### Present Perfect

> I have cleaned the room.

**Focus:** The **result**.

The room is clean now.

### Present Perfect Continuous

> I have been cleaning the room.

**Focus:** The **activity and duration**.

The emphasis is on the process of cleaning.

---

# Another Example

### Present Perfect

> She has written three reports today.

**Focus:** The number of completed reports.

### Present Perfect Continuous

> She has been writing reports all morning.

**Focus:** The activity and how long she has been doing it.

---

# Important Rule

Both tenses can describe actions that started in the past and have a connection to the present.

The difference is mainly the **focus**:

- **Present Perfect → RESULT**
- **Present Perfect Continuous → PROCESS / DURATION**

## Easy Way to Remember

**What has happened? → Present Perfect**

> I have finished my homework.

**How long has it been happening? → Present Perfect Continuous**

> I have been doing my homework for two hours.
