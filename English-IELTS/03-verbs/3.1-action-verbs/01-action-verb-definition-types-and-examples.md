# 🏃 Action Verbs — Verbos de Acción

> **Action verbs** are verbs that express a **physical or mental action**. They describe what the subject of a sentence is doing, thinking, perceiving, or experiencing.

Action verbs can be classified according to whether they require an object and according to the type of action they describe.

---

# 1. 🎯 Transitive Action Verbs

**Transitive action verbs** require a **direct object** to complete their meaning.

The action is performed **on something or someone**.

### Example

> She **ate** an apple.

- **She** → Subject
- **ate** → Transitive action verb
- **an apple** → Direct object

### Additional Examples

> He **kicked** the ball.

> I **read** a book.

> They **built** a house.

> She **wrote** a letter.

### Structure

**Subject + Transitive Verb + Direct Object**

> She **opened** the door.

> They **bought** a car.

> He **fixed** the computer.

---

# 2. 🏃 Intransitive Action Verbs

**Intransitive action verbs** do **not require a direct object** to complete their meaning.

The action does not act directly on an object.

### Example

> He **sleeps** peacefully.

There is no direct object receiving the action.

### Additional Examples

> She **ran** quickly.

> They **laughed** loudly.

> He **talks** a lot.

> The birds **flew** away.

### Structure

**Subject + Intransitive Verb**

> The baby **cried**.

> They **arrived**.

> She **laughed**.

An intransitive verb can still be followed by additional information such as an adverb or prepositional phrase:

> She **ran** quickly.

> They **arrived** at the station.

---

# 3. 💪 Physical Action Verbs

**Physical action verbs** describe actions involving **bodily movement or physical activity**.

They describe something the subject physically does.

### Example

> He **dances** gracefully.

### Additional Examples

> She **runs** daily.

> They **swim** in the lake.

> He **jumps** high.

> I **climb** mountains.

### More Examples

- pull

### Example in Context

> The workers **built** a bridge.

> She **opened** the door.

> They **carried** the boxes upstairs.

---

# 4. 🧠 Mental Action Verbs

**Mental action verbs** describe actions related to **thinking, understanding, remembering, perceiving, believing, or feeling**.

Although these actions are not necessarily physical, they describe something happening mentally.

### Example

> She **believes** in herself.

### Additional Examples

> He **thinks** deeply.

> They **remember** the event.

> She **understands** the concept.

> I **doubt** his intentions.

> She **remembers** his name.

---

# 📊 Transitive vs. Intransitive

The key difference is whether the verb requires a **direct object**.

| Type | Requires Direct Object? | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Transitive** | ✅ Yes | She **read a book**. |
| **Intransitive** | ❌ No | She **laughed**. |

### Compare

> She **opened the door**.

**Opened** → transitive  
**the door** → direct object

> She **laughed**.

**Laughed** → intransitive  
No direct object.

---

# 📊 Physical vs. Mental Action

Action verbs can also be classified according to the **type of action**.

| Type | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Physical Action** | Involves physical activity or movement | run, jump, swim, build |
| **Mental Action** | Involves thinking, perception, or mental processes | think, understand, remember, believe |

---

# 🔄 Important: A Verb Can Belong to More Than One Category

These categories are not always mutually exclusive.

For example, **write** is:

- an **action verb**
- a **physical action verb**
- usually a **transitive verb** when followed by an object

> She **wrote** a letter.

Similarly, **remember** is:

- an **action verb**
- a **mental action verb**
- often **transitive**

> I **remember** the event.

---

# 🧠 How to Identify an Action Verb

Ask yourself:

### 1. Is the subject doing or experiencing something?

If yes, the verb may be an action verb.

> She **runs** every morning.

> He **thinks** about the problem.

### 2. Is there a direct object?

If yes, the action verb may be **transitive**.

> She **bought** a car.

If there is no direct object, it may be **intransitive**.

> She **laughed**.

### 3. Is the action physical or mental?

**Physical:**

> They **swim** every weekend.

**Mental:**

> They **understand** the problem.

---

# 🎯 Key Takeaways

- **Action verbs** describe physical or mental actions.
- **Transitive action verbs** require a direct object.
- **Intransitive action verbs** do not require a direct object.
- **Physical action verbs** describe bodily movements or physical activities.
- **Mental action verbs** describe thinking, understanding, remembering, believing, or other mental processes.
- A single verb can belong to more than one category depending on how it is used.

### 💡 Quick Rule

> **ACTION VERB** → What is the subject doing or experiencing?
>
> **TRANSITIVE** → Action + direct object
>
> **INTRANSITIVE** → Action without a direct object
>
> **PHYSICAL** → Body or physical activity
>
> **MENTAL** → Thinking, perceiving, remembering, feeling, etc.
