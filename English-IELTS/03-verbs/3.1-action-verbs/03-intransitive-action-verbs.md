# 🏃 Intransitive Action Verbs

> **An intransitive action verb** is a verb that does **not require a direct object** to complete its meaning. It expresses an action or state that does not transfer to an object.

In other words, the action **does not act directly on someone or something**.

---

# 1. 🔹 Intransitive Action Verbs

Intransitive action verbs can stand alone and express a complete idea without requiring a direct object.

### Example

> She **dances** gracefully.

- **She** → Subject
- **dances** → Intransitive action verb
- **gracefully** → Adverb

The verb **dances** does not require a direct object.

### Basic Structure

**Subject + Intransitive Verb**

> He **laughed**.

> They **arrived**.

> She **smiled**.

---

# 2. 🚫 No Direct Object

Intransitive action verbs do **not have a direct object** because the action is not transferred to someone or something.

### Example

> He **laughed** loudly.

- **He** → Subject
- **laughed** → Intransitive verb
- **loudly** → Adverb

There is no direct object receiving the action.

### Compare

**Transitive:**

> He **kicked the ball**.

**the ball** → Direct object

**Intransitive:**

> He **laughed**.

No direct object.

# 5. 🔍 How to Identify an Intransitive Verb

A useful way to identify an intransitive verb is to check whether the verb requires a direct object.

Ask:

> **What?** or **Whom?**

after the verb.

### Example

> She **runs** every morning.

Ask:

> She runs **what?**

There is no direct object.

Therefore, **runs** is being used intransitively.

---

### Another Example

> They **arrived** late.

Ask:

> They arrived **what?**

The question does not make sense because **arrive** does not take a direct object in this use.

Therefore, **arrived** is intransitive.

---

# 6. 📊 Transitive vs. Intransitive

The main difference is whether the verb has or requires a **direct object**.

| Type | Direct Object | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Transitive** | ✅ Yes | She **opened the door**. |
| **Intransitive** | ❌ No | She **laughed**. |

### Transitive

> He **kicked the ball**.

**kicked** → Verb  
**the ball** → Direct object

### Intransitive

> He **laughed**.

**laughed** → Intransitive verb  
No direct object.

---

# ⚠️ Important: Extra Information Is Not Necessarily a Direct Object

An intransitive verb can still be followed by other types of information, such as **adverbs** or **prepositional phrases**.

### Example

> She **runs** every morning.

**every morning** → Adverbial expression  
**runs** → Intransitive verb

### Example

> They **arrived** at the station.

**at the station** → Prepositional phrase  
**arrived** → Intransitive verb

The presence of additional information does **not** automatically mean that the verb is transitive.

---

# 🎯 Key Takeaways

- An **intransitive action verb** does **not require a direct object**.
- The action does not transfer directly to another person or thing.
- Intransitive verbs can stand alone and express a complete idea.
- They can still be followed by **adverbs** or **prepositional phrases**.
- Examples include **run, sleep, laugh, swim, jump, arrive, fly, sing, stop,** and **smile**.

### 💡 Quick Rule

> **Subject + Intransitive Verb**

> She **laughed**.

> They **arrived**.

> He **slept**.

Or:

> **Subject + Intransitive Verb + Additional Information**

> She **runs** every morning.

> They **arrived** at the station.

> He **sings** beautifully.
