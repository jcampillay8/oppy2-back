# 🎯 Transitive Action Verbs

> **A transitive action verb** is a verb that requires a **direct object** to complete its meaning. It describes an action that is performed **on someone or something**.

The **direct object** receives the action of the verb.

---

# 1. 🔹 Transitive Action Verbs

Transitive action verbs require a **direct object** to make the meaning of the verb complete.

### Example

> She **ate** an apple.

- **She** → Subject
- **ate** → Transitive action verb
- **an apple** → Direct object

The verb **ate** describes an action performed on **an apple**.

### Basic Structure

**Subject + Transitive Verb + Direct Object**

> He **painted** a picture.

> They **built** a house.

> She **wrote** a letter.

---

# 2. 🎯 Direct Object

The **direct object** is the noun or noun phrase that **receives the action** of the verb.

### Example

> He **painted** a picture.

- **He** → Subject
- **painted** → Verb
- **a picture** → Direct object

The picture receives the action of **painting**.

### More Examples

> She **opened** the door.

> I **read** the book.

> They **bought** a car.

> We **cooked** dinner.

# 4. 👥 Direct Object and Indirect Object

Some transitive verbs can have **both a direct object and an indirect object**.

### Example

> She **gave** him a gift.

- **She** → Subject
- **gave** → Transitive verb
- **him** → Indirect object
- **a gift** → Direct object

### What is the difference?

**Direct object:**

> Receives the action directly.

**Indirect object:**

> Receives the direct object or benefits from the action.

### Another Example

> I **sent** her a postcard.

- **I** → Subject
- **sent** → Verb
- **her** → Indirect object
- **a postcard** → Direct object

# 6. 🔍 How to Identify a Transitive Verb

A useful way to identify a transitive verb is to ask:

> **What?** or **Whom?**

after the verb.

### Example

> She **bought** a car.

Ask:

> She bought **what?**

Answer:

> **A car.**

Therefore, **bought** is being used transitively.

---

### Another Example

> He **kicked** the ball.

Ask:

> He kicked **what?**

Answer:

> **The ball.**

Therefore, **kicked** is a transitive verb.

---

# ⚠️ Transitive vs. Intransitive

The key difference is whether the verb has a **direct object**.

| Type | Direct Object | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Transitive** | ✅ Required | She **opened the door**. |
| **Intransitive** | ❌ Not required | She **laughed**. |

### Transitive

> He **read the book**.

**read** → verb  
**the book** → direct object

### Intransitive

> He **laughed**.

There is no direct object.

---

# 🎯 Key Takeaways

- A **transitive action verb** requires a **direct object**.
- The **direct object receives the action** of the verb.
- Some transitive verbs can have both a **direct object** and an **indirect object**.
- To identify the direct object, ask **"what?"** or **"whom?"** after the verb.
- Transitive verbs can describe physical or mental actions.

### 💡 Quick Rule

> **Subject + Transitive Verb + Direct Object**

> She **wrote** a letter.

> They **built** a house.

> He **kicked** the ball.
