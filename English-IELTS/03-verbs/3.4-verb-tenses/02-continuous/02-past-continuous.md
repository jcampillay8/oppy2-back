# Past Continuous Tense

The **Past Continuous Tense**, also known as the **Past Progressive Tense**, is used to describe **actions that were in progress at a specific time in the past**.

It emphasizes the **ongoing or continuous nature** of an action.

## 1. Formation of the Past Continuous Tense

The Past Continuous is formed using:

**Subject + was/were + verb-ing**

### Forms of "to be"

| Subject | Auxiliary | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I | was | I **was studying**. |
| You | were | You **were studying**. |
| He | was | He **was studying**. |
| She | was | She **was studying**. |
| It | was | It **was raining**. |
| We | were | We **were studying**. |
| They | were | They **were studying**. |

### Examples

> They **were playing** soccer.

> She **was cooking** dinner.

> I **was studying** for the exam.

---

## 2. Uses of the Past Continuous Tense

### Ongoing Actions in the Past

Used to describe an action that was happening at a particular moment in the past.

> I **was studying** when she called me.

> She **was working** at 8 PM.

> They **were watching** TV last night.

### Setting the Background

The Past Continuous can provide background information or context for another past event.

> She **was cooking** dinner while he was watching TV.

> The sun **was shining**, and the birds **were singing**.

### Simultaneous Actions

Used when two or more actions were happening at the same time.

> We **were talking** and **laughing** at the same time.

> She **was studying** while her brother **was playing** video games.

### Interrupted Actions

The Past Continuous is often used for an action that was in progress when another action occurred.

The ongoing action usually uses **Past Continuous**, while the interrupting action uses **Simple Past**.

> He **was reading** a book when the phone **rang**.

> We **were watching** a movie when it **started** to rain.

> She **was writing** a letter when the power **went** out.

### Important Pattern

**Past Continuous + when + Simple Past**

> I **was sleeping** when the alarm **rang**.

The first action was already in progress. The second action interrupted or occurred during it.

---

## 3. Examples

- I **was studying** all night for the exam.
- They **were traveling** in Europe last summer.
- She **was writing** a letter when the power went out.
- We **were watching** a movie when it started to rain.
- He **was running** late for the meeting.
- She **was working** at 7 PM.
- They **were talking** when the teacher entered the room.

---

## 4. Time Expressions

The Past Continuous is commonly used with expressions that indicate a specific moment or period in the past.

### Common expressions

- at that time
- while
- when
- as
- during
- at 7 PM
- at that moment
- all morning
- all night

### Examples

> She **was cooking** dinner at 7 PM.

> I **was working** at that time.

> They **were talking** when I arrived.

> He **was sleeping** while she was studying.

---

## 5. Negative Form

To form negative sentences, add **not** after **was/were**.

**Subject + was/were + not + verb-ing**

### Examples

> He **was not listening** to music.

> They **were not studying**.

> She **was not working** at that time.

### Contractions

- was not → **wasn't**
- were not → **weren't**

Examples:

> He **wasn't listening**.

> They **weren't studying**.

---

## 6. Interrogative Form

To form questions, place **was/were** before the subject.

**Was/Were + subject + verb-ing...?**

### Examples

> **Were you playing** soccer?

> **Was she studying**?

> **Were they working** yesterday?

> **Was he sleeping** when you called?

### Short Answers

**Yes:**

> Yes, I was.

> Yes, she was.

> Yes, they were.

**No:**

> No, I wasn't.

> No, she wasn't.

> No, they weren't.

---

## 7. Past Continuous vs. Simple Past

These two tenses are often used together.

### Past Continuous

Describes an action that was **in progress**.

### Simple Past

Describes a **completed event** or an event that happened during that action.

### Compare

> I **was studying** when my friend **called**.

- **was studying** → action already in progress
- **called** → event that happened during it

Another example:

> They **were watching** TV when the lights **went** out.

- **were watching** → ongoing action
- **went out** → completed event

---

## 8. "While" vs. "When"

### While

**While** is commonly used to connect two actions that were happening at the same time.

> She **was cooking** while he **was watching** TV.

> I **was studying** while my brother **was playing**.

### When

**When** is commonly used when an event occurs during an ongoing action.

> I **was sleeping** when the phone **rang**.

> They **were eating** when the teacher **arrived**.

---

## 9. Summary

| Form | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Affirmative | Subject + was/were + verb-ing | She **was studying**. |
| Negative | Subject + was/were + not + verb-ing | She **wasn't studying**. |
| Question | Was/Were + subject + verb-ing? | **Was** she **studying**? |
| Short answer | Yes + subject + was/were | Yes, she **was**. |
| Negative answer | No + subject + wasn't/weren't | No, she **wasn't**. |

## Key Idea

> **Past Continuous = action that was in progress at a particular time in the past**

### Basic Formula

**AFFIRMATIVE:**  
`Subject + was/were + verb-ing`

**NEGATIVE:**  
`Subject + was/were + not + verb-ing`

**QUESTION:**  
`Was/Were + subject + verb-ing?`

### Example

**Affirmative:**

> I **was studying** English.

**Negative:**

> I **wasn't studying** English.

**Question:**

> **Were** you **studying** English?
