# Present Perfect Tense

The **Present Perfect Tense** is used to describe actions or events that happened in the past but have a **connection to the present**. It often focuses on the **result, experience, or relevance** of an action rather than the specific time it happened.

## 1. Formation of the Present Perfect Tense

The Present Perfect is formed with:

**Subject + have/has + past participle**

| Subject | Auxiliary | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I / You / We / They | have | I have finished |
| He / She / It | has | She has finished |

### Examples

- I **have finished** my work.
- She **has seen** that movie.
- They **have visited** London.
- He **has lost** his keys.

> **Note:** The main verb is always in the **past participle** form.

---

## 2. Usage of the Present Perfect Tense

### A. Past actions with a present result

The action happened in the past, but its result is important **now**.

- I **have lost** my keys.
- She **has broken** her phone.
- They **have finished** the project.

The exact time is not important. What matters is the **present result**.

---

### B. Life experiences

The Present Perfect is used to talk about experiences that have happened at some point in someone's life.

- I **have visited** Paris.
- She **has traveled** to several countries.
- He **has never eaten** sushi.
- **Have** you **ever seen** the Eiffel Tower?

The specific time is usually not mentioned.

---

### C. Actions that started in the past and continue into the present

The Present Perfect can describe situations that began in the past and are still true now.

- I **have lived** here for five years.
- She **has worked** at the company since 2022.
- They **have known** each other for a long time.

Common expressions:

- **for** + period of time
- **since** + starting point

Examples:

- I have studied English **for** three years.
- I have studied English **since** 2023.

---

### D. Recent actions

The Present Perfect is commonly used for actions that happened recently.

- We **have just arrived**.
- She **has recently started** a new job.
- They **have already finished** the meeting.

Common words:

- just
- recently
- already
- yet

---

## 3. Examples

- I **have visited** Paris several times.
- She **has read** that book before.
- They **have studied** English for three years.
- He **has lost** his keys.
- We **have never been** to Asia.
- She **has already finished** her homework.
- They **have just arrived** home.

---

## 4. Common Time Expressions

The Present Perfect is frequently used with:

| Expression | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **ever** | at any time | Have you ever traveled abroad? |
| **never** | at no time | I have never been to Japan. |
| **already** | before now | She has already finished. |
| **yet** | until now | Have you finished yet? |
| **just** | very recently | He has just arrived. |
| **recently** | not long ago | They have recently moved. |
| **so far** | until now | We have completed three tasks so far. |
| **since** | from a specific time | I have lived here since 2020. |
| **for** | during a period | I have worked here for five years. |

---

## 5. Negative Form

To form the negative:

**Subject + have/has + not + past participle**

### Examples

- I **have not finished** my work.
- She **has not seen** that movie.
- They **have not arrived** yet.

### Contractions

- have not → **haven't**
- has not → **hasn't**

Examples:

- I **haven't finished** yet.
- He **hasn't called** me.
- They **haven't arrived**.

---

## 6. Question Form

To form questions:

**Have/Has + subject + past participle?**

### Examples

- **Have** you **finished** your homework?
- **Has** she **seen** this movie?
- **Have** they **visited** London?
- **Has** he **completed** the project?

### Short Answers

**Have you finished?**

- Yes, I **have**.
- No, I **haven't**.

**Has she arrived?**

- Yes, she **has**.
- No, she **hasn't**.

---

## 7. Present Perfect vs. Simple Past

The key difference is whether we specify a **finished time in the past**.

### Present Perfect

The specific time is not mentioned or is not important.

- I **have visited** Paris.
- She **has seen** that movie.

### Simple Past

The action happened at a specific finished time.

- I **visited** Paris **last year**.
- She **saw** that movie **yesterday**.

> **Present Perfect:** connection with the present  
> **Simple Past:** finished action at a specific past time

---

## 8. Basic Structure

### Affirmative

**Subject + have/has + past participle**

> She **has finished** her work.

### Negative

**Subject + have/has + not + past participle**

> She **hasn't finished** her work.

### Question

**Have/Has + subject + past participle?**

> **Has** she **finished** her work?

---

## 9. Key Point

The **Present Perfect** connects the **past with the present**.

It is commonly used for:

- Past actions with a present result
- Life experiences
- Actions that started in the past and continue now
- Recent actions
- Situations where the exact past time is not important

### Formula

**HAVE / HAS + PAST PARTICIPLE**

> I **have worked**.  
> She **has studied**.  
> They **have finished**.
