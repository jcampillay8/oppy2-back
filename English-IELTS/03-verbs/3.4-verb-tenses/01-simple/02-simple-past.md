# Simple Past Tense

The **Simple Past Tense** is used to describe **completed actions or events that occurred in the past**. It indicates that the action is finished and is no longer happening.

## 1. Formation of the Simple Past Tense

### Regular Verbs

For most regular verbs, the simple past is formed by adding **-ed** to the base form of the verb.

- walk → walked
- work → worked
- play → played
- study → studied

**Example:**

> She walked to the store.

### Irregular Verbs

Irregular verbs do not follow a consistent pattern and have their own past forms.

- go → went
- eat → ate
- write → wrote
- see → saw
- buy → bought

**Example:**

> He ate lunch an hour ago.

---

## 2. Uses of the Simple Past Tense

### Past Actions or Events

Used for actions that were completed in the past.

> He ate lunch an hour ago.

> We watched a movie last night.

### Specific Time in the Past

Used when the action happened at a specific time or during a specific period in the past.

> They lived in that house for ten years.

> I visited London in 2019.

### Stories and Historical Events

Used to describe events in stories or historical situations.

> Once upon a time, there was a brave knight.

> The company opened its first office in 1995.

### Past Habits or States

Used to describe habits, activities, or states that were true in the past.

> When I was young, I played the piano.

> She lived in Chile when she was a child.

---

## 3. Examples

- They studied hard for the exam.
- She traveled to Europe last summer.
- We watched a movie last night.
- He wrote a letter to his friend.
- The team won the championship.
- I visited my grandparents last weekend.
- She worked at the company for five years.

---

## 4. Time Expressions

The Simple Past is commonly used with expressions that indicate a finished time in the past.

### Common expressions

- yesterday
- last night
- last week
- last month
- last year
- two hours ago
- three days ago
- in 1999
- when I was a child
- last weekend

**Examples:**

> I visited my grandparents **last weekend**.

> She moved to London **in 2020**.

> They finished the project **two days ago**.

---

## 5. Negative Form

To form negative sentences, use:

**Subject + did not (didn't) + base verb**

> She **did not go** to the party.

> They **didn't finish** their homework.

> He **didn't study** for the exam.

### Important

After **did/didn't**, always use the **base form** of the verb.

❌ She didn't went to school.

✅ She didn't go to school.

---

## 6. Interrogative Form

To form questions, use:

**Did + subject + base verb...?**

> Did you finish your homework?

> Did she go to the party?

> Did they watch the movie?

### Short answers

**Yes:**

> Yes, I did.

> Yes, she did.

**No:**

> No, I didn't.

> No, she didn't.

---

## 7. Simple Past with the Verb "To Be"

The verb **be** is different because it does not use **did** in affirmative, negative, or interrogative forms.

### Affirmative

- I was
- You were
- He was
- She was
- It was
- We were
- They were

**Examples:**

> I was tired yesterday.

> They were at home last night.

### Negative

- was not → wasn't
- were not → weren't

> She wasn't at school.

> They weren't ready.

### Questions

**Was/Were + subject...?**

> Was he at work?

> Were they happy?

---

## 8. Summary

| Form | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Affirmative | Subject + past verb | She **worked** yesterday. |
| Negative | Subject + didn't + base verb | She **didn't work** yesterday. |
| Question | Did + subject + base verb? | **Did** she **work** yesterday? |
| Be – affirmative | Subject + was/were | She **was** tired. |
| Be – negative | Subject + wasn't/weren't | She **wasn't** tired. |
| Be – question | Was/Were + subject? | **Was** she tired? |

### Key idea

> **Simple Past = completed action in the past**

**Affirmative:**  
> I **worked** yesterday.

**Negative:**  
> I **didn't work** yesterday.

**Question:**  
> **Did** you **work** yesterday?
