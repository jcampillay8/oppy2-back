# Simple Future Tense

The **Simple Future Tense** is used to express **actions or events that will happen in the future**. It indicates that something will occur after the present moment.

## 1. Formation of the Simple Future Tense

The Simple Future is formed using:

**Subject + will + base form of the verb**

### Examples

- I **will study**.
- She **will work**.
- They **will travel**.
- We **will meet**.

> She **will study** for the exam.

### Important

After **will**, always use the **base form** of the verb.

❌ She will studies.

✅ She will study.

---

## 2. Uses of the Simple Future Tense

### Future Actions or Events

Used to describe actions that will happen later.

> They **will go** on vacation next week.

> She **will cook** dinner tonight.

### Predictions

Used to make predictions about the future.

> I think it **will rain** tomorrow.

> The company **will grow** next year.

### Promises and Offers

Used to make promises or offer help.

> I **will help** you with your project.

> Don't worry. I **will call** you later.

### Decisions Made at the Moment of Speaking

The Simple Future with **will** is commonly used when a decision is made spontaneously.

> The phone is ringing. I **will answer** it.

> I'm thirsty. I **will get** some water.

---

## 3. "Will" vs. "Be Going To"

English commonly uses both **will** and **be going to** to talk about the future, but they can have different meanings.

### Will

Often used for:

- predictions
- promises
- offers
- spontaneous decisions

> I **will help** you.

> I think it **will rain** tomorrow.

### Be Going To

Often used for:

- plans or intentions
- predictions based on present evidence

> I **am going to study** tonight.

> Look at those clouds! It **is going to rain**.

Both forms can refer to the future, but the context determines which one is more appropriate.

## 5. Time Expressions

The Simple Future is commonly used with expressions that indicate a future time.

### Common expressions

- tomorrow
- tonight
- next week
- next month
- next year
- soon
- in the future
- in a month
- in two years
- later
- tomorrow morning
- next weekend

### Examples

> We **will meet** tomorrow.

> She **will start** the course next month.

> They **will move** to another city in two years.

---

## 6. Negative Form

To form negative sentences, use:

**Subject + will not + base verb**

The contraction of **will not** is **won't**.

### Examples

> She **will not attend** the party.

> They **won't come** tomorrow.

> I **won't forget** your advice.

### Structure

**Subject + won't + base verb**

> He **won't work** on Saturday.

---

## 7. Interrogative Form

To form questions, put **will** before the subject:

**Will + subject + base verb...?**

### Examples

> **Will you come** to the meeting?

> **Will she study** tonight?

> **Will they travel** next month?

> **Will we see** you tomorrow?

### Short Answers

**Yes:**

> Yes, I will.

> Yes, she will.

**No:**

> No, I won't.

> No, she won't.

---

## 8. The Verb "To Be" in the Simple Future

The verb **be** follows the same structure:

**Subject + will + be**

### Examples

> I **will be** there tomorrow.

> She **will be** happy.

> They **will be** late.

### Negative

> I **will not be** there.

> They **won't be** late.

### Question

> **Will** you **be** at home?

> **Will** she **be** available tomorrow?

---

## 9. Summary

| Form | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Affirmative | Subject + will + base verb | She **will work** tomorrow. |
| Negative | Subject + will not + base verb | She **will not work** tomorrow. |
| Negative contraction | Subject + won't + base verb | She **won't work** tomorrow. |
| Question | Will + subject + base verb? | **Will** she **work** tomorrow? |
| Short answer | Yes + subject + will | Yes, she **will**. |
| Short negative answer | No + subject + won't | No, she **won't**. |

## Key Idea

> **Simple Future = action or event that will happen in the future**

**Affirmative:**

> I **will study** tomorrow.

**Negative:**

> I **won't study** tomorrow.

**Question:**

> **Will** you **study** tomorrow?

### Basic Formula

**AFFIRMATIVE:**  
`Subject + will + base verb`

**NEGATIVE:**  
`Subject + will not / won't + base verb`

**QUESTION:**  
`Will + subject + base verb?`
