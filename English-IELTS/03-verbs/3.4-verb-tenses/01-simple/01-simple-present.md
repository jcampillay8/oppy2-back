# Simple Present Tense

The **Simple Present Tense** is used to express **general truths, habitual actions, repeated actions, and permanent situations**.

It is one of the most basic and frequently used verb tenses in English.

---

## 1. Formation of the Simple Present Tense

For most verbs, the Simple Present is formed using the **base form of the verb**.

### Affirmative Form

**Subject + Base Verb**

Examples:

- I work every day.
- You speak English.
- We live in Chile.
- They study at university.

### Third-Person Singular

With **he, she, and it**, regular verbs usually take **-s** or **-es**.

Examples:

- He works every day.
- She speaks English.
- It rains frequently.
- He watches television.
- She goes to school.

### Common Spelling Rules

| Base Verb | He/She/It |
|---|---|
| work | works |
| play | plays |
| read | reads |
| watch | watches |
| go | goes |
| study | studies |
| try | tries |

---

## 2. Usage of the Simple Present Tense

### General Truths and Facts

The Simple Present is used for things that are generally or universally true.

Examples:

- "The sun rises in the east."
- "Water boils at 100°C."
- "The Earth revolves around the Sun."

### Habitual or Repeated Actions

It is used to describe actions that happen regularly.

Examples:

- "She goes for a run every morning."
- "I eat breakfast every day."
- "He reads books for pleasure."

### Routines and Schedules

The Simple Present can describe routines, timetables, and scheduled events.

Examples:

- "The train departs at 8:00 AM."
- "The class starts at 9:00."
- "The bus leaves every thirty minutes."

### Permanent Situations or Characteristics

It can describe situations, states, or characteristics that are considered permanent or long-term.

Examples:

- "I live in New York City."
- "She speaks three languages."
- "He works for a technology company."

## 3. Examples of the Simple Present

- "I eat breakfast every day."
- "He reads books for pleasure."
- "They speak English fluently."
- "The flowers bloom in spring."
- "She dances beautifully."
- "We study English together."
- "My brother works in an office."

---

## 4. Adverbs and Time Expressions

The Simple Present is commonly used with **adverbs of frequency** and **time expressions** that indicate regularity.

### Adverbs of Frequency

Common examples include:

- always
- usually
- often
- sometimes
- rarely
- never

Examples:

- "She always arrives on time."
- "He usually drinks coffee in the morning."
- "They often go to the gym."
- "I sometimes work from home."
- "She never arrives late."

### Time Expressions

Common expressions include:

- every day
- every week
- every month
- once a week
- twice a month
- three times a year

Examples:

- "They go to the gym three times a week."
- "I study English every day."
- "She visits her parents once a month."

---

## 5. Negative Form

To form negative sentences, use the auxiliary verb **do + not + base verb**.

For **he, she, and it**, use **does + not + base verb**.

### Structure

**Subject + do/does + not + Base Verb**

Examples:

- I do not like coffee.
- You do not work here.
- They do not speak French.
- He does not like coffee.
- She does not work here.

### Contractions

In everyday English, contractions are very common:

- do not → **don't**
- does not → **doesn't**

Examples:

- I don't like coffee.
- They don't live here.
- He doesn't speak French.
- She doesn't work on Sundays.

> **Important:** After **doesn't**, use the base form of the verb.

- ✅ She doesn't **like** coffee.
- ❌ She doesn't **likes** coffee.

---

## 6. Interrogative Form

To form questions in the Simple Present, use **do** or **does** before the subject.

### Structure

**Do/Does + Subject + Base Verb?**

Examples:

- Do you play the guitar?
- Do they live here?
- Do we need a reservation?
- Does he work here?
- Does she speak English?

> **Important:** After **does**, use the base form of the verb.

- ✅ Does she **work** here?
- ❌ Does she **works** here?

---

## 7. Simple Present Structure

| Form | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Affirmative** | Subject + Verb | She works here. |
| **Negative** | Subject + do/does + not + Verb | She doesn't work here. |
| **Question** | Do/Does + Subject + Verb? | Does she work here? |

---

## 8. Key Points to Remember

- Use the **base form** of the verb with **I, you, we, and they**.
- Add **-s** or **-es** with **he, she, and it**.
- Use **do/does** to form negatives and questions.
- After **does/doesn't**, always use the **base form** of the verb.
- The Simple Present is commonly used for:
  - Facts
  - General truths
  - Habits
  - Routines
  - Repeated actions
  - Schedules
  - Permanent or long-term situations

---

## IELTS Tip

The Simple Present is particularly useful in IELTS when describing:

- **Facts and general information**
- **Graphs and charts**
- **Routines and habits**
- **General opinions**
- **Processes**
- **Permanent situations**

For example:

> "The graph **shows** an increase in the number of students."

> "The process **begins** when the raw materials **enter** the factory."

Using the Simple Present accurately is an important foundation for developing grammatical range and accuracy in IELTS Speaking and Writing.
