# Active Voice

The **active voice** is a grammatical structure in which the **subject performs the action** expressed by the verb.

It is one of the most common and direct ways to construct sentences in English.

---

## 1. Structure of Active Voice

The basic structure is:

**Subject + Verb + Object**

### Example

> "John wrote a letter."

- **Subject:** John
- **Verb:** wrote
- **Object:** a letter

The subject **John** performs the action of writing.

---

## 2. Characteristics of Active Voice

- The subject is the **doer** or performer of the action.
- The verb directly describes the action performed by the subject.
- The object receives the action.
- It usually follows the **Subject + Verb + Object (SVO)** structure.
- It is generally more direct, clear, and concise than passive voice.

---

## 3. Examples of Active Voice

- "She paints beautiful landscapes."
- "The company released a new product."
- "They built a bridge across the river."
- "He writes reports every week."
- "We completed the project yesterday."

### Example Analysis

**The company released a new product.**

- **Subject:** The company
- **Verb:** released
- **Object:** a new product

The company **performs** the action.

---

## 4. Active Voice in Different Tenses

Active voice can be used with different verb tenses.

| **Tense** | **Example** |
|---|---|
| Simple Present | She writes reports. |
| Present Continuous | She is writing a report. |
| Simple Past | She wrote a report. |
| Past Continuous | She was writing a report. |
| Present Perfect | She has written a report. |
| Past Perfect | She had written a report. |
| Simple Future | She will write a report. |
| Future Perfect | She will have written a report. |

The sentence remains in active voice because **the subject performs the action**.

---

## 5. Advantages of Active Voice

### Clarity

Active voice makes it clear who performs the action.

> "The engineer analyzed the data."

We immediately know that **the engineer** performed the analysis.

### Conciseness

Active sentences are often shorter.

> "The team completed the project."

Instead of:

> "The project was completed by the team."

### Directness

Active voice makes communication more direct and easier to follow.

### Responsibility

It clearly identifies who performed an action.

> "The technician repaired the machine."

The sentence clearly identifies **the technician** as the person responsible for the repair.

---

## 6. Active Voice vs. Passive Voice

### Active Voice

> "The technician repaired the machine."

**Focus:** The technician

**Structure:**

**Subject + Verb + Object**

### Passive Voice

> "The machine was repaired by the technician."

**Focus:** The machine

**Structure:**

**Subject + be + Past Participle + by + Agent**

The meaning is similar, but the **focus changes**.

---

## 7. When to Use Active Voice

Active voice is generally preferred when:

- You want to clearly identify the person performing the action.
- You want your writing to be direct.
- You want to make a sentence shorter and easier to understand.
- You are explaining a process or giving instructions.
- You are writing everyday English.

### Example

> "The analyst reviewed the security logs."

This is clear and direct because we know exactly **who reviewed the logs**.

---

## 8. Quick Rule

Remember:

> **ACTIVE = The subject does the action.**

### Formula

**Subject → Action → Object**

### Example

> "The developer created the application."

**The developer** → performs → **created**

**The application** → receives the action

---

## Summary

| **Active Voice** | **Description** |
|---|---|
| **Subject** | Performs the action |
| **Verb** | Expresses the action |
| **Object** | Receives the action |
| **Structure** | Subject + Verb + Object |
| **Main Focus** | The person or thing performing the action |
| **Main Advantage** | Clear, direct, and concise |

### Key Example

> **"The engineer analyzed the data."**

**Engineer = Doer**  
**Analyzed = Action**  
**Data = Receiver**
