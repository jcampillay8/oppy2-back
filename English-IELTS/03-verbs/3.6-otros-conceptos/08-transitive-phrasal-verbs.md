# Transitive Phrasal Verbs

A **transitive phrasal verb** is a combination of a verb and one or more particles that **requires an object** to complete its meaning.

In other words:

> **Transitive Phrasal Verb = Phrasal Verb + Object**

The object is usually a noun, pronoun, noun phrase, or gerund.

---

## 1. Structure

A transitive phrasal verb consists of:

- **Main verb**
- **Particle** (adverb or preposition)
- **Object**

### Basic Structure

`Verb + Particle + Object`

### Example

> She **put on** her coat.

- `put` → verb
- `on` → particle
- `her coat` → object
- `put on` → transitive phrasal verb

---

## 2. Common Examples

| **Phrasal Verb** | **Meaning** | **Example** |
|---|---|---|
| `put on` | ponerse / vestir | She put on her coat. |
| `take off` | quitarse | He took off his glasses. |
| `look after` | cuidar | They looked after the dog. |
| `bring up` | mencionar / plantear | He brought up an interesting point. |
| `turn on` | encender | Please turn on the computer. |
| `turn off` | apagar | Please turn off the lights. |
| `pick up` | recoger | She picked up the book. |
| `call off` | cancelar | They called off the meeting. |
| `look for` | buscar | I am looking for my keys. |
| `find out` | averiguar | We need to find out the truth. |

---

## 3. Object Placement

One important characteristic of some transitive phrasal verbs is that the object can be placed in different positions.

### Pattern 1: Verb + Particle + Object

> She **put on** her coat.

### Pattern 2: Verb + Object + Particle

> She **put her coat on**.

Both sentences have the same meaning.

---

## 4. Pronouns

When the object is a **pronoun**, the pronoun usually must go **between the verb and the particle** with separable phrasal verbs.

### Correct

> Put **it** on. ✅

### Incorrect

> Put on **it**. ❌

Another example:

> Turn **it** off. ✅

> Turn off **it**. ❌

---

## 5. Separable vs. Inseparable

Not all transitive phrasal verbs behave in the same way.

### A. Separable Phrasal Verbs

The object can go between the verb and the particle.

> She **turned on** the computer.

> She **turned the computer on**.

Both are correct.

Examples:

- `turn on`
- `turn off`
- `put on`
- `take off`
- `pick up`
- `bring up`

---

### B. Inseparable Phrasal Verbs

The verb and particle must remain together.

Example:

> They **looked after** the dog.

Correct:

> They looked after the dog. ✅

Incorrect:

> They looked the dog after. ❌

Examples:

- `look after`
- `look for`
- `deal with`
- `run into`
- `get over`

---

## 6. Meaning

The meaning of a transitive phrasal verb is often different from the individual meanings of its components.

### Example: `take off`

`take` = tomar

`off` = fuera

But:

`take off` = **quitarse / despegar**

Example:

> He **took off** his jacket.

= Él se **quitó** la chaqueta.

---

### Example: `look after`

`look` = mirar

`after` = después de

But:

`look after` = **cuidar**

Example:

> She **looks after** her brother.

= Ella **cuida** a su hermano.

---

## 7. Transitive vs. Intransitive Phrasal Verbs

The key difference is whether the phrasal verb requires an object.

| **Transitive** | **Intransitive** |
|---|---|
| Requires an object | Does not require an object |
| `turn off the TV` | `wake up` |
| `look after the dog` | `get up` |
| `pick up the phone` | `take off` |
| `call off the meeting` | `show up` |

### Transitive

> She **turned off the TV**.

`the TV` = object

### Intransitive

> The plane **took off**.

There is no object.

---

## 8. Quick Identification Method

To identify a **transitive phrasal verb**, ask:

> **"What?" or "Whom?"**

### Example

> She picked up the book.

What did she pick up?

→ `the book`

Therefore:

`pick up` = **transitive phrasal verb**

---

### Example

> They called off the meeting.

What did they call off?

→ `the meeting`

Therefore:

`call off` = **transitive phrasal verb**

---

## 9. Important Rule

Remember:

> **A transitive phrasal verb needs an object.**

### Formula

`Transitive Phrasal Verb + Object`

### Examples

`turn on + the computer`

`pick up + the phone`

`look after + the child`

`call off + the meeting`

`bring up + an issue`

---

## Summary

### Transitive Phrasal Verb

> A phrasal verb that **requires an object** to complete its meaning.

### Main characteristics

- Contains a **verb + particle**.
- Requires an **object**.
- The meaning may be **idiomatic**.
- Some are **separable**.
- Some are **inseparable**.
- With separable phrasal verbs, pronouns usually go between the verb and particle.

### Key Formula

`Verb + Particle + Object`

**Example:**

> She **turned off** the computer.

`turned off` → transitive phrasal verb

`the computer` → object
