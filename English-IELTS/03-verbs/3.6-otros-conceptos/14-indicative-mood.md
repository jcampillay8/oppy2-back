# Indicative Mood

The **indicative mood** is the most common and frequently used grammatical mood in English. It is used to make **statements, ask questions, and provide information** about facts, reality, opinions, beliefs, certainty, and events.

## 1. Statements

The indicative mood is used to make straightforward statements and communicate information.

### Examples

- He is a teacher.
- She works at a bank.
- They live in Chile.
- I studied English yesterday.

---

## 2. Questions

The indicative mood is also used to ask questions and obtain information.

### Examples

- Did you see the movie?
- Are you working today?
- Where does she live?
- Will they arrive tomorrow?

---

## 3. Facts and Reality

The indicative mood is used to express facts, describe events, and state information that is believed to be true.

### Examples

- The sun rises in the east.
- Water freezes at 0°C.
- Chile is in South America.
- The company opened a new office last year.

---

## 4. Opinions and Beliefs

The indicative mood can also be used to express opinions, thoughts, or beliefs.

### Examples

- I think she is talented.
- I believe this is the best option.
- He thinks the project will succeed.
- She believes they are ready.

---

## 5. Certainty and Predictions

The indicative mood can express things that the speaker considers certain, likely, or true.

### Examples

- They will arrive on time.
- She is going to pass the exam.
- The meeting starts at 9 AM.
- He knows the answer.

---

## 6. Verb Forms

In the indicative mood, verbs use their normal forms according to the tense being used.

### Present Simple

**Subject + base verb**

- She works every day.
- They live in Santiago.

### Past Simple

**Subject + past form**

- She worked yesterday.
- They lived in Santiago.

### Future Simple

**Subject + will + base verb**

- She will work tomorrow.
- They will travel next week.

### Present Continuous

**Subject + am/is/are + verb-ing**

- She is working now.
- They are studying.

---

## 7. Indicative Mood vs. Other Moods

The indicative mood is mainly used to **state information or ask questions**.

| Mood | Main Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Indicative** | Facts, statements, questions, opinions | She works here. |
| **Imperative** | Commands or instructions | Open the door. |
| **Subjunctive** | Wishes, hypothetical situations, recommendations | I suggest that he be careful. |

---

## Key Idea

The **indicative mood** is the **default mood in English**.

It is used when we are:

- Making statements
- Asking questions
- Describing facts
- Expressing opinions
- Expressing beliefs
- Describing real or expected events
- Talking about actions in different tenses

### Simple Formula

**Indicative Mood = Information about what is, was, or is expected to be true.**

### Examples

- **Present:** She works here.
- **Past:** She worked here.
- **Future:** She will work here.
- **Question:** Does she work here?
- **Opinion:** I think she works here.
