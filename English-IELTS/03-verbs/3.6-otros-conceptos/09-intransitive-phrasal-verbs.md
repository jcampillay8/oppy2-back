# Intransitive Phrasal Verbs

An **intransitive phrasal verb** is a combination of a verb and one or more particles that **does not require a direct object** to complete its meaning.

In other words:

> **Intransitive Phrasal Verb = Phrasal Verb without a Direct Object**

The sentence can be grammatically complete without adding a noun or pronoun after the phrasal verb.

---

## 1. Structure

An intransitive phrasal verb consists of:

- **Main verb**
- **Particle** (adverb or preposition)

### Basic Structure

`Verb + Particle`

### Example

> I **woke up**.

- `woke` → verb
- `up` → particle
- `woke up` → intransitive phrasal verb
- There is **no direct object**.

The sentence is complete.

---

## 2. Common Examples

| **Phrasal Verb** | **Meaning** | **Example** |
|---|---|---|
| `wake up` | despertarse | I woke up early. |
| `break down` | averiarse / dejar de funcionar | The car broke down. |
| `come in` | entrar | Please come in. |
| `get up` | levantarse | I get up at 7 AM. |
| `show up` | aparecer / presentarse | He didn't show up. |
| `grow up` | crecer | She grew up in Chile. |
| `sit down` | sentarse | Please sit down. |
| `stand up` | ponerse de pie | Everyone stood up. |
| `hurry up` | apurarse | Hurry up! |
| `slow down` | reducir la velocidad | Please slow down. |
| `go away` | irse | The dog went away. |
| `come back` | regresar | He came back yesterday. |

---

## 3. No Direct Object

The main characteristic of an intransitive phrasal verb is that it **does not require a direct object**.

### Example

> I **woke up**.

The sentence is complete.

You do not need to add an object:

> I woke up **the bed**. ❌

`wake up` in this use means **despertarse** and does not take a direct object.

---

### Another Example

> The car **broke down**.

The sentence is complete.

`broke down` = dejó de funcionar / se averió

There is no direct object.

---

## 4. Prepositional Information Is Not Always a Direct Object

An intransitive phrasal verb can still be followed by other information, such as a **prepositional phrase**.

Example:

> The car broke down **on the highway**.

`on the highway` gives information about **where** the action happened.

It is not a direct object.

Another example:

> She grew up **in Chile**.

`in Chile` = prepositional phrase indicating location.

---

## 5. Meaning

Like other phrasal verbs, intransitive phrasal verbs can have meanings that are different from the individual words.

### `wake up`

`wake` = despertar

`up` = arriba

But:

`wake up` = **despertarse**

> I **wake up** at 7 AM.

---

### `break down`

`break` = romper

`down` = abajo

But:

`break down` = **averiarse / dejar de funcionar**

> My car **broke down** yesterday.

---

### `come in`

`come` = venir

`in` = dentro

But:

`come in` = **entrar**

> Please **come in**.

---

### `run out`

`run` = correr

`out` = fuera

But:

`run out` = **acabarse / quedarse sin algo**

> We **ran out**.

However, note that **run out of + noun** is a related construction:

> We **ran out of milk**.

Here, `milk` is the object of the preposition `of`, not a direct object of `run out`.

---

## 6. Transitive vs. Intransitive Phrasal Verbs

The key difference is whether the phrasal verb requires a **direct object**.

| **Transitive** | **Intransitive** |
|---|---|
| Requires a direct object | Does not require a direct object |
| `turn off the TV` | `wake up` |
| `pick up the phone` | `get up` |
| `look after the child` | `come in` |
| `call off the meeting` | `show up` |
| `bring up an issue` | `break down` |

### Transitive

> She **turned off the TV**.

`the TV` → direct object

### Intransitive

> She **woke up**.

No direct object.

---

## 7. How to Identify an Intransitive Phrasal Verb

Ask:

> **"What?" or "Whom?"**

If the phrasal verb does not need an answer to these questions, it may be intransitive.

### Example

> The car **broke down**.

What did the car break down?

There is no answer.

Therefore:

`break down` → **intransitive phrasal verb**

---

### Example

> He **showed up** late.

What did he show up?

There is no answer.

Therefore:

`show up` → **intransitive phrasal verb**

---

## 8. Important Characteristic

Intransitive phrasal verbs generally **cannot be separated** because there is no direct object to place between the verb and particle.

Example:

> She **woke up**. ✅

Not:

> She **woke** herself **up**. ❌

However, some verbs can have **both transitive and intransitive uses**, and their meaning can change depending on the structure.

For example:

> The car **broke down**.  
> → Intransitive

But:

> He **broke down** the door.  
> → Here, `break down` is transitive and means **demolish / destroy**.

Therefore, always consider the **specific meaning and structure** being used.

---

## 9. Key Formula

### Intransitive Phrasal Verb

`Subject + Verb + Particle`

Example:

> The car **broke down**.

---

### With Additional Information

`Subject + Verb + Particle + Additional Information`

Example:

> The car **broke down** on the highway.

`on the highway` → prepositional phrase

---

## Summary

### Intransitive Phrasal Verb

> A phrasal verb that **does not require a direct object**.

### Main characteristics

- Contains a **verb + particle**.
- Does not require a **direct object**.
- The sentence can be complete without an object.
- It can be followed by additional information such as adverbs or prepositional phrases.
- Its meaning can be **literal or idiomatic**.
- Some verbs can be both **transitive and intransitive**, depending on their meaning and use.

### Key Formula

`Subject + Verb + Particle`

**Examples:**

> I **woke up**.

> The car **broke down**.

> Please **come in**.

> He **showed up**.

> She **grew up** in Chile.

---

## Quick Comparison

**Transitive:**

> She **turned off the computer**.

`turned off` → phrasal verb  
`the computer` → direct object

**Intransitive:**

> The computer **broke down**.

`broke down` → phrasal verb  
No direct object
