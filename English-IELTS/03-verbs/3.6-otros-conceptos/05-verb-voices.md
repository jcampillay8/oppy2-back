# Verb Voice

**Verb voice** refers to the form or structure of a verb that indicates whether the **subject performs the action** or **receives the action**.

There are two main types of verb voice:

1. **Active Voice**
2. **Passive Voice**

---

# 1. Active Voice

The **active voice** is a grammatical construction in which the **subject performs the action** expressed by the verb.

It is the most common and straightforward way of presenting information.

### Basic Structure

**Subject + Verb + Object**

### Example

> "She wrote a novel."

- **She** → subject / doer
- **wrote** → verb / action
- **a novel** → object / receiver

### Characteristics of Active Voice

- The subject performs the action.
- It usually follows **Subject + Verb + Object (SVO)** word order.
- It is generally clear, direct, and concise.
- It emphasizes the **doer** of the action.

### Examples

- "She **wrote** a novel."
- "The cat **chased** the mouse."
- "They **are building** a house."
- "He **sings** beautifully."
- "We **ate** dinner together."

---

# 2. Advantages of Active Voice

### Clarity

Active voice clearly identifies who or what performs the action.

> "The engineer designed the system."

The reader immediately knows who designed the system.

### Conciseness

Active voice often produces shorter sentences.

> "The team completed the project."

Instead of:

> "The project was completed by the team."

### Directness

Active voice presents information in a direct and straightforward way.

### Common Uses

Active voice is frequently used in:

- Everyday conversation
- Instructions
- Narratives
- News articles
- Business communication
- Technical writing

---

# 3. Passive Voice

The **passive voice** is a grammatical construction in which the **subject receives or experiences the action** rather than performing it.

The focus is usually placed on the **receiver of the action** rather than the doer.

### Basic Structure

**Subject + form of "be" + Past Participle + (by + agent)**

### Example

> "The book was written by the author."

- **The book** → subject / receiver
- **was** → auxiliary verb
- **written** → past participle
- **by the author** → agent / doer

### Important

The person or thing that performs the action can be introduced with **by**, but it does not always need to be mentioned.

> "The cake was baked."

We know that someone baked the cake, but the doer is not specified.

---

# 4. Characteristics of Passive Voice

- The subject receives the action.
- The doer may be introduced using **by**.
- The doer may be unknown, irrelevant, or intentionally omitted.
- The verb uses a form of **be + past participle**.
- It is commonly used when the action or result is more important than the doer.

### Examples

- "The report **was prepared** by the team."
- "The window **was broken** by a baseball."
- "The cake **has been baked**."
- "The flowers **were being watered** by the gardener."
- "The decision **will be made** by the committee."

---

# 5. Why Use Passive Voice?

## Focus on the Receiver

The passive voice emphasizes the person or thing receiving the action.

> "The new system was implemented in 2026."

The focus is on **the new system**, not necessarily on who implemented it.

## The Doer Is Unknown

> "My car was stolen."

The person who performed the action is unknown.

## The Doer Is Irrelevant

> "The documents were uploaded to the system."

Who uploaded them may not be important.

## Formal or Academic Writing

Passive voice is frequently used in scientific, technical, and formal writing when the process or result is more important than the individual performing the action.

> "The data was analyzed using SQL."

---

# 6. Drawbacks of Passive Voice

### Lack of Clarity

Passive constructions can sometimes hide who performed the action.

> "Mistakes were made."

Who made the mistakes?

### Wordiness

Passive sentences can sometimes be longer.

> "The report was prepared by the analyst."

Active:

> "The analyst prepared the report."

### Reduced Directness

Excessive use of passive voice can make writing less direct or engaging.

---

# 7. Active vs. Passive Voice

| Active Voice | Passive Voice |
|---|---|
| The subject performs the action. | The subject receives the action. |
| Focuses on the doer. | Focuses on the receiver. |
| Usually more direct. | Can be more formal or impersonal. |
| Usually S + V + O. | Usually S + be + past participle. |
| "The engineer designed the system." | "The system was designed by the engineer." |

---

# 8. Active → Passive

To convert an active sentence into passive voice:

### Step 1: Identify the object

> "The engineer designed **the system**."

Object = **the system**

### Step 2: Move the object to the subject position

> "**The system** ..."

### Step 3: Use the appropriate form of "be"

The original verb is in the simple past:

> designed → **was**

### Step 4: Use the past participle

> design → **designed**

### Step 5: Add the original subject with "by" if necessary

> "The system **was designed by the engineer**."

### Transformation

> **Active:** The engineer designed the system.

> **Passive:** The system was designed by the engineer.

---

# 9. Passive → Active

To convert a passive sentence into active voice:

### Example

> **Passive:** The cake was baked by her.

Identify the agent:

> **her**

Make the agent the subject:

> **She**

Change the verb to its active form:

> **baked**

Move the original subject to the object position:

> **the cake**

### Result

> **Active:** She baked the cake.

---

# 10. Passive Voice in Different Tenses

The passive voice can be formed in different verb tenses.

| Tense | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Simple Present | They build houses. | Houses **are built**. |
| Present Continuous | They are building a house. | A house **is being built**. |
| Present Perfect | They have built a house. | A house **has been built**. |
| Simple Past | They built a house. | A house **was built**. |
| Past Continuous | They were building a house. | A house **was being built**. |
| Past Perfect | They had built a house. | A house **had been built**. |
| Simple Future | They will build a house. | A house **will be built**. |
| Future Perfect | They will have built a house. | A house **will have been built**. |

### General Pattern

**Passive Voice = Form of "be" + Past Participle**

The form of **be** changes according to the tense.

Examples:

- **is** built
- **was** built
- **is being** built
- **has been** built
- **had been** built
- **will be** built
- **will have been** built

---

# 11. Key Concept

The main difference can be remembered as:

### Active Voice

**DOER → ACTION → RECEIVER**

> The engineer → designed → the system.

### Passive Voice

**RECEIVER → ACTION → DOER**

> The system → was designed → by the engineer.

---

# Summary

| Feature | Active Voice | Passive Voice |
|---|---|---|
| Main focus | Doer | Receiver |
| Subject | Performs the action | Receives the action |
| Typical structure | Subject + Verb + Object | Subject + be + Past Participle |
| "By" | Usually unnecessary | Can introduce the doer |
| Style | Direct and concise | Often more formal or impersonal |
| Example | "The team completed the project." | "The project was completed by the team." |

**Active voice** is generally preferred when you want to make your writing **clear, direct, and concise**.

**Passive voice** is useful when the **receiver, action, result, or process** is more important than the person performing the action.
