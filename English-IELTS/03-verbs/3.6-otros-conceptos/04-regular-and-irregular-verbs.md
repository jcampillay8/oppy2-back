# Regular and Irregular Verbs

Regular and irregular verbs are two categories of verbs based on how they form their **past tense** and **past participle** forms.

---

# 1. Regular Verbs

Regular verbs follow a **predictable pattern** when forming their past tense and past participle.

In most cases, they form both by adding **-ed** or **-d** to the base form.

## Formation

### General Rule

**Base form + -ed = Past tense / Past participle**

Examples:

| Base Form | Past Tense | Past Participle |
|---|---|---|
| talk | talked | talked |
| play | played | played |
| walk | walked | walked |
| jump | jumped | jumped |
| work | worked | worked |

### Verbs Ending in "e"

If the verb already ends in **e**, add only **-d**.

| Base Form | Past Tense | Past Participle |
|---|---|---|
| love | loved | loved |
| like | liked | liked |
| live | lived | lived |
| move | moved | moved |

### Doubling the Final Consonant

Some verbs ending in a single consonant preceded by a single vowel **double the final consonant** before adding **-ed**.

| Base Form | Past Tense | Past Participle |
|---|---|---|
| stop | stopped | stopped |
| plan | planned | planned |
| admit | admitted | admitted |

## Examples in Sentences

- "She **talked** to her friend yesterday."
- "They **played** soccer last weekend."
- "He **worked** late yesterday."
- "I have **finished** my homework."
- "She has **visited** that city before."

Regular verbs are useful because their past forms are generally **predictable and systematic**.

---

# 2. Irregular Verbs

Irregular verbs **do not follow the standard -ed/-d pattern** when forming their past tense and/or past participle.

Their forms can change in different ways and generally need to be **learned individually**.

## Examples

| Base Form | Past Tense | Past Participle |
|---|---|---|
| go | went | gone |
| be | was/were | been |
| eat | ate | eaten |
| write | wrote | written |
| take | took | taken |
| see | saw | seen |
| speak | spoke | spoken |
| begin | began | begun |
| drive | drove | driven |
| break | broke | broken |

## Examples in Sentences

### Simple Past

- "He **went** to the store yesterday."
- "She **ate** breakfast early."
- "They **wrote** a report."
- "I **saw** him yesterday."

### Present Perfect

- "He has **gone** to the store."
- "She has **eaten** breakfast."
- "They have **written** a report."
- "I have **seen** that movie."

Notice that the **past tense** and **past participle** can be different:

**write → wrote → written**

- Past: "She **wrote** a letter."
- Past participle: "She has **written** a letter."

---

# 3. Key Difference

| Feature | Regular Verbs | Irregular Verbs |
|---|---|---|
| Past tense | Usually **-ed/-d** | Does not follow a single standard pattern |
| Past participle | Usually **-ed/-d** | Can have a different form |
| Pattern | Predictable | Often unpredictable |
| Example | work → worked → worked | go → went → gone |
| Learning | Rules can be applied | Forms generally need to be learned |

---

# 4. Important Verb Forms

When learning verbs, it is useful to remember these three forms:

**Base Form → Past Tense → Past Participle**

### Regular Verb

**work → worked → worked**

### Irregular Verb

**go → went → gone**

The **past tense** is commonly used with the **simple past**:

> "She **went** to work yesterday."

The **past participle** is used with auxiliary verbs such as **have** and **be**:

> "She has **gone** to work."

> "The letter was **written** yesterday."

# 6. Main Idea

The easiest way to remember the difference is:

**Regular verbs:**

> Base + **-ed** → Past → Past Participle

**Example:**

> work → worked → worked

**Irregular verbs:**

> Base → **different form** → Past Participle

**Example:**

> go → went → gone

Therefore, regular verbs follow a predictable pattern, while irregular verbs have forms that must generally be learned through **practice, repetition, and exposure**.
