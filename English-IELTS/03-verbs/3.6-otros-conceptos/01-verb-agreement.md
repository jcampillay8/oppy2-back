# Subject-Verb Agreement

**Subject-verb agreement**, also known as **verb agreement**, is the grammatical rule that requires the verb to agree with the subject in **number** (singular or plural) and, in some cases, **person**.

## 1. Singular Subject + Singular Verb

When the subject refers to **one person, thing, or entity**, the verb must agree with a singular subject.

- "She **walks** to school every day."
- "The dog **barks** at strangers."
- "He **eats** breakfast every morning."

> In the **simple present**, a third-person singular subject (he, she, it) usually takes a verb ending in **-s** or **-es**.

### Examples

- He **works** every day.
- She **watches** TV.
- The computer **runs** efficiently.

---

## 2. Plural Subject + Plural Verb

When the subject refers to **more than one person, thing, or entity**, the verb must agree with a plural subject.

- "They **walk** to school every day."
- "The dogs **bark** at strangers."
- "They **eat** breakfast every morning."

> In the **simple present**, plural subjects generally use the **base form** of the verb.

### Compare

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| He **works**. | They **work**. |
| She **runs**. | They **run**. |
| The dog **barks**. | The dogs **bark**. |
| The company **operates**. | The companies **operate**. |

---

## 3. The Verb "Be"

The verb **be** changes according to the subject and tense.

### Present

- I **am** a student.
- He **is** a student.
- She **is** happy.
- They **are** students.
- We **are** ready.

### Past

- I **was** tired.
- He **was** at home.
- They **were** tired.
- We **were** at work.

---

## 4. Third-Person Singular in the Simple Present

With **he, she, and it**, most verbs take **-s** or **-es**.

- He **works** here.
- She **drives** a car.
- It **looks** good.
- He **watches** TV.
- She **goes** to work.

Some verbs are irregular:

- He **has** a car.
- She **does** her homework.
- He **is** happy.

---

## 5. Auxiliary Verbs and Agreement

When an auxiliary verb is used, the agreement usually appears in the **auxiliary**, while the main verb remains in its required form.

### Present Continuous

- She **is working**.
- They **are working**.

### Present Perfect

- He **has finished**.
- They **have finished**.

### Simple Present Questions

- **Does** she work here?
- **Do** they work here?

### Simple Past

- She **did** her homework.
- They **did** their homework.

> Notice that after **does/did**, the main verb returns to its **base form**.

❌ She does **works** here.  
✅ She does **work** here.

❌ Did he **went** home?  
✅ Did he **go** home?

---

## 6. Collective Nouns

Collective nouns refer to a group, such as:

- team
- family
- company
- government
- staff

In American English, these nouns are generally treated as **singular** when the group is considered as one unit.

- "The team **is** ready."
- "The company **has** announced its results."
- "The family **lives** in Santiago."

In some varieties of British English, collective nouns can be treated as plural when emphasizing the individuals within the group.

---

## 7. Indefinite Pronouns

Some indefinite pronouns are grammatically **singular**, even when they refer to many people.

Examples:

- everyone
- everybody
- someone
- somebody
- anyone
- anybody
- nobody
- each
- either
- neither

### Examples

- Everyone **is** ready.
- Someone **has** called you.
- Everybody **wants** to participate.
- Each student **has** a computer.
- Neither option **is** correct.

---

## 8. The Most Important Rule

The verb must agree with the **subject**, not necessarily with the noun closest to the verb.

### Example

"The list of employees **is** on the desk."

The subject is **the list**, not **employees**.

- **List** → singular
- Therefore → **is**

❌ The list of employees **are** on the desk.  
✅ The list of employees **is** on the desk.

Another example:

"The students in the classroom **are** studying."

- **Students** → plural
- Therefore → **are**

# Quick Reference

|---|---|---|

| We | work | We **work** here. |
| They | work | They **work** here. |

## Easy Rule to Remember

> **Singular subject → singular verb**  
> **Plural subject → plural verb**

Especially in the **simple present**:

> **He / She / It → verb + S**  
> **I / You / We / They → base verb**

### Example

- He **works**.
- She **studies**.
- It **runs**.
- I **work**.
- We **study**.
- They **run**.
