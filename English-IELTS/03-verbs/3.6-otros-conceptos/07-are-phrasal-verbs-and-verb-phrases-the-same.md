# Phrasal Verbs vs. Verb Phrases

**Phrasal verbs and verb phrases are not the same.**

Although they are related, they describe different grammatical concepts.

---

## 1. Phrasal Verbs

A **phrasal verb** is a combination of:

`Verb + Particle`

The particle is usually an adverb or preposition, and together they create a specific meaning.

The meaning can be different from the individual meanings of the words.

### Examples

- `look after` → cuidar de
- `take off` → despegar / quitarse
- `give up` → rendirse
- `find out` → averiguar
- `get along` → llevarse bien

### Example in a sentence

> She **looks after** her younger brother.

`looks after` = cuida de

The meaning of the complete expression is different from simply understanding `look` and `after` separately.

---

## 2. Verb Phrases

A **verb phrase** is a broader grammatical structure that contains a main verb and, when applicable, auxiliary or modal verbs.

### Examples

- `is playing`
- `have finished`
- `will have been studying`
- `might have gone`
- `can speak`

### Example

> She **is studying** English.

`is studying` = verb phrase

- `is` → auxiliary verb
- `studying` → main verb

Another example:

> They **will have been working** all day.

`will have been working` = verb phrase

- `will` → modal auxiliary
- `have` → auxiliary
- `been` → auxiliary
- `working` → main verb

---

## 3. Key Difference

| **Phrasal Verb** | **Verb Phrase** |
|---|---|
| A specific combination of a verb + particle | A broader grammatical structure |
| Creates a specific meaning | Expresses an action, state, tense, aspect, mood, etc. |
| Often has an idiomatic meaning | Does not necessarily have an idiomatic meaning |
| Example: `give up` | Example: `has been studying` |
| Example: `look after` | Example: `will have finished` |
| Example: `take off` | Example: `can speak` |

---

## 4. The Important Relationship

A useful way to understand the relationship is:

**Verb Phrase = broader category**

**Phrasal Verb = specific type of verb + particle construction**

Therefore:

> **Not all verb phrases are phrasal verbs.**

For example:

> She **is studying** English.

`is studying` → **verb phrase**  
`is studying` → **not a phrasal verb**

---

However:

> She **gave up** smoking.

`gave up` → **phrasal verb**

And the complete verb phrase in this sentence can be:

> She **gave up smoking**.

Here, `gave up` is the **phrasal verb**, while the larger verbal structure can be analyzed as the **verb phrase**.

---

## 5. Easy Way to Remember

### Phrasal Verb

Think:

`Verb + Particle = New Meaning`

Examples:

`give + up = give up` → rendirse

`look + after = look after` → cuidar

`find + out = find out` → averiguar

---

### Verb Phrase

Think:

`Auxiliary/Modal + Main Verb`

Examples:

`is + working` → is working

`has + finished` → has finished

`will + go` → will go

`might + have + gone` → might have gone

---

## Summary

**Phrasal verb:**

> A verb combined with a particle to create a specific meaning.

**Verb phrase:**

> A grammatical unit containing a main verb and potentially auxiliary/modal verbs and other elements.

### The simplest distinction:

**Phrasal verb → focuses on the combination and meaning of the verb + particle.**

**Verb phrase → focuses on the complete verbal structure of the sentence.**

Therefore:

> **Phrasal verbs and verb phrases are related, but they are NOT the same thing.**
