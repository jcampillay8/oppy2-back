# Passive Voice

The **passive voice** is a grammatical structure in which the **subject receives or experiences the action** expressed by the verb.

Unlike the active voice, the focus is usually on the **receiver of the action**, the action itself, or its result.

---

## 1. Structure of Passive Voice

The basic structure is:

**Subject + be + Past Participle + (by + Agent)**

### Example

> "The letter was written by John."

- **Subject:** The letter
- **Verb:** was written
- **Agent:** John

The subject **the letter** receives the action.

---

## 2. Characteristics of Passive Voice

- The subject **receives** the action.
- The verb uses a form of **to be + past participle**.
- The person or thing performing the action is called the **agent**.
- The agent can be introduced using **by**.
- The agent can be omitted when it is unknown, irrelevant, or obvious.
- Passive voice often focuses on the **action, receiver, or result** rather than the doer.

---

## 3. Examples of Passive Voice

- "The report was prepared by the team."
- "The window was broken by the baseball."
- "The cake was baked by Sarah."
- "The system has been updated."
- "The decision will be announced tomorrow."

### Example Analysis

**The report was prepared by the team.**

- **Subject:** The report
- **Passive verb:** was prepared
- **Agent:** the team

The report **receives** the action of being prepared.

---

## 4. Passive Voice Without an Agent

The agent does not always need to be mentioned.

### Example

**Active:**

> "Someone stole my computer."

**Passive:**

> "My computer was stolen."

We do not know who stole the computer, or the identity of the person is not important.

Other examples:

- "The window was broken."
- "The report was completed."
- "The documents were lost."
- "The system was updated."
- "The meeting was canceled."

---

## 5. Passive Voice in Different Tenses

Passive voice can be used with different verb tenses.

| **Tense** | **Passive Structure** | **Example** |
|---|---|---|
| Simple Present | am/is/are + past participle | The report is written. |
| Present Continuous | am/is/are being + past participle | The report is being written. |
| Simple Past | was/were + past participle | The report was written. |
| Past Continuous | was/were being + past participle | The report was being written. |
| Present Perfect | has/have been + past participle | The report has been written. |
| Past Perfect | had been + past participle | The report had been written. |
| Simple Future | will be + past participle | The report will be written. |
| Future Perfect | will have been + past participle | The report will have been written. |

---

## 6. How to Form the Passive Voice

To convert an active sentence into passive voice:

### Step 1: Identify the object

> "The engineer analyzed the data."

The object is:

**the data**

### Step 2: Move the object to the subject position

> "The data..."

### Step 3: Change the verb to passive

"analyzed" → "was analyzed"

### Step 4: Add the agent if necessary

> "The data was analyzed by the engineer."

> "The machine was repaired by the technician."

---

**Passive:**

> "The software was developed by the company."

**Active:**

> "Someone stole the documents."

> "The documents were stolen."

The agent is omitted because it is unknown or not important.

---

## 8. When to Use Passive Voice

Passive voice is useful when:

### 1. The doer is unknown

> "The computer was stolen."

We don't know who stole it.

### 2. The doer is not important

> "The report was submitted yesterday."

The important information is that the report was submitted.

### 3. The action or result is more important

> "The system was successfully updated."

The focus is on the system and the result.

### 4. The writing is formal or technical

Passive voice is frequently used in:

- Scientific writing
- Technical documentation
- Academic writing
- Reports
- Procedures
- Business communication

### Example

> "The data were analyzed using Python."

The focus is on **the data and the analysis**, not necessarily on the person who performed it.

---

## 9. Advantages of Passive Voice

- Focuses on the receiver of the action.
- Useful when the agent is unknown.
- Useful when the agent is not important.
- Can emphasize the result of an action.
- Common in formal, scientific, and technical contexts.

---

## 10. Drawbacks of Passive Voice

- Can make sentences less direct.
- Can make sentences longer.
- Can hide who performed the action.
- Can sometimes make writing more difficult to understand.
- Excessive use can make writing sound unnecessarily formal.

### Compare

**Active:**

> "The engineer fixed the problem."

**Passive:**

> "The problem was fixed by the engineer."

The active version is more direct.

---

## 11. Active Voice vs. Passive Voice

| **Feature** | **Active Voice** | **Passive Voice** |
|---|---|---|
| **Subject** | Performs the action | Receives the action |
| **Focus** | Doer of the action | Receiver or result |
| **Verb** | Active form | Be + past participle |
| **Agent** | Usually the subject | Can be introduced with "by" |
| **Structure** | Subject + Verb + Object | Subject + Be + Past Participle |
| **Style** | Direct and concise | More formal or indirect |
| **Example** | The engineer fixed the machine. | The machine was fixed by the engineer. |

---

## 12. Quick Rule

Remember:

> **PASSIVE = The subject receives the action.**

### Formula

**Receiver → be + Past Participle → (by + Doer)**

### Example

> "The software was developed by the company."

**The software** → receives the action  
**was developed** → passive verb  
**the company** → performs the action

---

## Summary

| **Passive Voice** | **Description** |
|---|---|
| **Subject** | Receives the action |
| **Verb** | Form of "be" + past participle |
| **Agent** | The person or thing performing the action |
| **Agent Marker** | Usually "by" |
| **Structure** | Subject + be + Past Participle + (by + Agent) |
| **Main Focus** | Receiver, action, or result |
| **Common Uses** | Formal, academic, scientific, and technical writing |

### Key Example

> **"The security logs were analyzed by the analyst."**

**Security logs = Receiver**  
**were analyzed = Passive verb**  
**the analyst = Agent**
