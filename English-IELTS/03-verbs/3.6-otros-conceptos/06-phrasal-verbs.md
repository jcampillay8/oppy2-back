# Phrasal Verbs

**Phrasal verbs** are combinations of a verb and one or more particles (typically prepositions or adverbs) that together form a single meaning. The addition of the particle often changes the original verb's meaning or adds a new sense to it.

Phrasal verbs are a common feature of English and are used extensively in both formal and informal contexts.

---

## 1. Structure

Phrasal verbs consist of:

- **Main verb** + **particle**
- The particle can be a preposition or an adverb.
- Common particles include: `up`, `down`, `on`, `off`, `out`, `in`, `away`, etc.
- The meaning of the phrasal verb is often different from the meaning of the individual words.

### Example

> **Turn** + **off** = **turn off**

The meaning of `turn off` is not simply "turn" + "off". It means **apagar**.

**Example:**

> Please **turn off** the lights.

---

## 2. Meaning and Usage

Phrasal verbs often have meanings that are different from their individual words or the original verb.

They can be:

- **Separable**
- **Inseparable**

### Separable Phrasal Verbs

The particle can sometimes be placed between the verb and the object.

**Example:**

> Turn off the TV.  
> Turn the TV off.

Both sentences have the same meaning.

However, when using a pronoun, the object must come between the verb and the particle:

> Turn **it** off. ✅  
> Turn off **it**. ❌

### Inseparable Phrasal Verbs

The verb and particle must remain together.

**Example:**

> I am looking for my keys.

Not:

> I am looking my keys for. ❌

---

## 3. Common Types of Phrasal Verbs

### A. Transitive Phrasal Verbs

Transitive phrasal verbs require an **object**.

**Example:**

> Turn on the lights.

- `turn on` → phrasal verb
- `the lights` → object

Another example:

> She **called off** the meeting.

- `called off` → phrasal verb
- `the meeting` → object

---

### B. Intransitive Phrasal Verbs

Intransitive phrasal verbs **do not require an object**.

**Example:**

> The plane **took off**.

There is no object after `took off`.

Another example:

> He **woke up** early.

---

### C. Phrasal Verbs with Literal Meaning

The meaning can be understood relatively easily from the individual words.

**Example:**

> The cat **jumped off** the table.

`jumped off` literally means that the cat moved by jumping from the table to another place.

---

### D. Phrasal Verbs with Idiomatic Meaning

The meaning is different from the literal meaning of the individual words.

**Example:**

> They **called off** the meeting.

`call off` = **cancel**

It does not literally mean "call" + "off".

---

## 4. Examples

| Type | Phrasal Verb | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Transitive | `turn on` | Turn on the lights. | encender |
| Transitive | `call off` | They called off the meeting. | cancelar |
| Intransitive | `wake up` | I wake up at 7 AM. | despertarse |
| Intransitive | `take off` | The plane took off. | despegar |
| Literal | `jump off` | The cat jumped off the table. | saltar desde |
| Idiomatic | `give up` | Don't give up. | rendirse |
| Idiomatic | `find out` | I found out the truth. | descubrir / averiguar |

---

## 5. Key Points to Remember

- A phrasal verb usually consists of a **verb + particle**.
- The particle can change the meaning of the original verb.
- Some phrasal verbs are **separable**.
- Some phrasal verbs are **inseparable**.
- Some phrasal verbs require an **object** (transitive).
- Some phrasal verbs do **not** require an object (intransitive).
- Some have a **literal meaning**.
- Others have an **idiomatic meaning**.
- Phrasal verbs are extremely common in everyday English.

---

## 6. Quick Formula

### Basic Structure

`Verb + Particle = Phrasal Verb`

### Examples

`turn + on = turn on` → encender

`give + up = give up` → rendirse

`find + out = find out` → averiguar

`look + for = look for` → buscar

`take + off = take off` → despegar / quitarse

---

## 7. Important Examples

> **I turned off the computer.**  
> Apagué el computador.

> **She gave up smoking.**  
> Ella dejó de fumar.

> **We need to find out what happened.**  
> Necesitamos averiguar qué pasó.

> **He is looking for his keys.**  
> Él está buscando sus llaves.

> **The plane took off at 8 AM.**  
> El avión despegó a las 8 AM.

---

## Summary

Phrasal verbs are an essential part of English because they allow speakers to express ideas naturally and efficiently.

The most important thing to remember is:

**The meaning of a phrasal verb cannot always be predicted from the individual words.**

For example:

`give` = dar  
`up` = arriba  

But:

`give up` = **rendirse / dejar de hacer algo**

Therefore, it is useful to learn phrasal verbs as **complete expressions**, rather than translating each word individually.
