# Phrasal Verbs with an Idiomatic Meaning

**Phrasal verbs with an idiomatic meaning** are combinations of a verb and one or more particles (prepositions or adverbs) whose meaning **cannot be understood by translating the individual words literally**.

Instead, the combination creates a **figurative or idiomatic meaning**.

---

## 1. Structure

A phrasal verb with an idiomatic meaning generally consists of:

`Verb + Particle = New Meaning`

The particle can be an adverb or preposition.

The combination creates a meaning that is different from the literal meanings of the individual words.

### Example

> They **called off** the meeting.

`call` = llamar  
`off` = fuera  

But:

`call off` = **cancelar**

Therefore, the meaning must be learned as a complete expression.

---

## 2. Common Examples

| **Phrasal Verb** | **Idiomatic Meaning** | **Example** |
|---|---|---|
| `break up` | terminar una relación | They decided to break up. |
| `get over` | superar / recuperarse de algo | She got over the problem. |
| `bring up` | mencionar / criar | He brought up an interesting topic. |
| `put off` | posponer | They put off the meeting. |
| `call off` | cancelar | They called off the meeting. |
| `give up` | rendirse / abandonar | Don't give up. |
| `find out` | averiguar / descubrir | I found out the truth. |
| `look after` | cuidar | She looks after her brother. |
| `run into` | encontrarse con alguien inesperadamente | I ran into an old friend. |
| `work out` | resolver / salir bien | Everything worked out. |

---

## 3. Idiomatic Interpretation

The important characteristic is that the **complete expression has a figurative meaning**.

### `break up`

Literal interpretation:

`break` = romper  
`up` = arriba

Idiomatic meaning:

`break up` = **terminar una relación**

> They decided to **break up**.

---

### `get over`

Literal interpretation:

`get` = obtener / conseguir  
`over` = sobre / encima

Idiomatic meaning:

`get over` = **superar / recuperarse de algo**

> It took her a long time to **get over** the loss.

---

### `bring up`

`bring` = traer  
`up` = arriba

But:

`bring up` can mean **mencionar un tema**.

> He **brought up** an interesting topic.

It can also mean **criar**:

> He was **brought up** by his grandparents.

---

### `put off`

`put` = poner  
`off` = fuera / apagado

But:

`put off` = **posponer / retrasar**

> Let's **put off** the meeting until next week.

---

## 4. Literal vs. Idiomatic Meaning

It is useful to distinguish between literal and idiomatic phrasal verbs.

### Literal

> The cat **jumped off** the table.

Here, `jumped off` has a relatively literal meaning:

`jump` + `off` → saltar desde una superficie

### Idiomatic

> They **called off** the meeting.

Here:

`call` + `off` ≠ llamar + fuera

`call off` = **cancelar**

---

## 5. Object Placement

Idiomatic phrasal verbs can be **transitive or intransitive**, depending on the specific phrasal verb.

Therefore, the position of the object depends on the phrasal verb.

### Intransitive

> They decided to **break up**.

There is no direct object.

---

### Transitive

> Let's **put off the meeting**.

`the meeting` → object

Some transitive phrasal verbs are separable:

> Put **the meeting** off.  
> Put **it** off.

The pronoun normally goes between the verb and the particle:

> Put **it** off. ✅

> Put off **it**. ❌

---

## 6. Why Are They Called "Idiomatic"?

An expression is **idiomatic** when its meaning cannot be understood simply by combining the literal meanings of its individual words.

For example:

> **Give up**

`give` = dar  
`up` = arriba

But:

`give up` = **rendirse / abandonar**

Therefore, learning `give up` as a complete expression is more useful than translating each word separately.

---

## 7. How to Learn Idiomatic Phrasal Verbs

It is better to learn them as **complete units**:

Instead of memorizing:

> `put` = poner  
> `off` = fuera

Learn:

> `put off` = **posponer**

And learn it in context:

> We **put off** the meeting until Friday.

This helps you understand:

- The meaning
- The grammar
- Whether it takes an object
- Whether it is separable
- How it is used in a real sentence

---

## 8. Quick Identification

Ask yourself:

> **Does the combined meaning differ significantly from the literal meaning of the individual words?**

If yes, the phrasal verb is likely being used **idiomatically**.

### Example

> She **gave up**.

`give` + `up` → not literally "dar + arriba"

Meaning:

> **give up = rendirse**

Therefore, it has an idiomatic meaning.

---

## Summary

### Phrasal Verb with Idiomatic Meaning

> A combination of a **verb + particle** whose combined meaning is **figurative or different from the literal meanings of the individual words**.

### Key Characteristics

- Contains a **verb + particle**.
- Creates a new meaning.
- The meaning often cannot be translated word by word.
- Can be **transitive or intransitive**.
- Some transitive phrasal verbs are **separable**.
- The best way to learn them is as **complete expressions in context**.
