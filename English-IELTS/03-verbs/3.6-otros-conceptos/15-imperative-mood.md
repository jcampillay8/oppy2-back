# Imperative Mood

The **imperative mood** is used to express **commands, requests, instructions, advice, or prohibitions**. It is commonly used when we want someone to do or not do something.

## 1. Commands

The imperative mood is used to give direct commands or orders.

The subject **"you"** is usually understood and is not explicitly stated.

### Structure

**Base form of the verb + complement**

### Examples

- Close the window.
- Sit down.
- Open the door.
- Listen carefully.
- Stop talking.

> The implied subject is **you**:
> - *(You) close the window.*
> - *(You) sit down.*

---

## 2. Requests

The imperative mood can also be used to make requests.

Adding **"please"** makes the request more polite.

### Examples

- Please pass me the salt.
- Please open the door.
- Please wait here.
- Please speak more slowly.
- Please send me the report.

### Without "please"

- Pass me the salt.
- Open the door.

### With "please"

- Please pass me the salt.
- Please open the door.

---

## 3. Instructions

The imperative mood is commonly used to give instructions, directions, or procedures.

### Examples

- Mix the ingredients in a bowl.
- Turn on the computer.
- Enter your password.
- Click the button.
- Add the data to the table.

### Example of step-by-step instructions

1. Open the application.
2. Enter your username.
3. Enter your password.
4. Click "Login".
5. Select the desired option.

---

## 4. Advice

The imperative mood can also be used to give advice or recommendations.

### Examples

- Take a break.
- Get some rest.
- Study regularly.
- Be careful.
- Practice every day.

The tone depends heavily on context.

For example:

- **Be careful.** → Advice or warning.
- **Be quiet.** → Command.

---

## 5. Prohibitions and Negative Imperatives

Negative imperatives are used to tell someone **not to do something**.

### Structure

**Don't / Do not + base verb**

### Examples

- Don't touch that!
- Don't open the door.
- Don't be late.
- Don't worry.
- Do not enter.
- Do not use this equipment.

### Important

After **don't**, use the **base form** of the verb.

- Correct: **Don't go.**
- Incorrect: ~~Don't goes.~~

---

## 6. "Let's" for Suggestions

The imperative structure can also be used with **"let's"** to make suggestions involving the speaker and other people.

### Structure

**Let's + base verb**

### Examples

- Let's go.
- Let's start the meeting.
- Let's study together.
- Let's take a break.
- Let's watch a movie.

### Negative form

**Let's not + base verb**

- Let's not argue.
- Let's not waste time.
- Let's not forget the deadline.

---

## 7. Verb Forms

In the imperative mood, the verb normally uses its **base form**.

| Verb | Imperative |
|---|---|
| go | Go! |
| stop | Stop! |
| listen | Listen! |
| work | Work! |
| study | Study! |
| be | Be careful! |

Unlike many other grammatical structures, the imperative does not change the verb according to the subject.

### Examples

- Work hard.
- Study English.
- Be careful.
- Come here.

Not:

- ~~Works hard.~~
- ~~Studies English.~~
- ~~Is careful.~~

---

## 8. The Verb "To Be"

The imperative form of **"to be"** is simply:

**Be + complement**

### Examples

- Be careful.
- Be quiet.
- Be patient.
- Be honest.
- Be ready.

### Negative

**Don't be + complement**

- Don't be late.
- Don't be afraid.
- Don't be careless.

---

## 9. Imperative vs. Indicative

The main difference is the purpose of the sentence.

| Mood | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Indicative** | States information or asks questions | You close the door. |
| **Imperative** | Gives a command or instruction | Close the door. |

### Compare

**Indicative:**
> You need to close the door.

**Imperative:**
> Close the door.

---

## 10. Key Characteristics

The imperative mood generally:

- Uses the **base form of the verb**.
- Usually has an **implied subject "you"**.
- Is used for commands, requests, instructions, advice, and warnings.
- Uses **don't + base verb** for negative commands.
- Does not normally show tense distinctions.
- Can use **please** to make requests more polite.
- Can use **let's** to make suggestions involving the speaker.

## Key Formula

### Affirmative

**Base verb + complement**

> Open the door.

### Negative

**Don't + base verb + complement**

> Don't open the door.

### Polite Request

**Please + base verb + complement**

> Please open the door.

### Suggestion

**Let's + base verb + complement**

> Let's open the door.

### Simple Idea

**Imperative Mood = Tell someone what to do or what not to do.**
