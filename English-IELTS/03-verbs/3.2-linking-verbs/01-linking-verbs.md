# 🔗 Linking Verbs — Verbos Copulativos

> **Linking verbs**, también conocidos como **copular verbs**, son verbos que conectan el **sujeto** de una oración con un **complemento** que lo describe, identifica o proporciona información adicional sobre él.

A diferencia de los **action verbs**, los linking verbs generalmente **no expresan una acción**, sino un **estado, condición o relación**.

---

# 1. 🔹 Linking Verbs

Los linking verbs conectan el sujeto con un complemento que describe o identifica al sujeto.

### Example

> She **is** a doctor.

- **She** → Subject
- **is** → Linking verb
- **a doctor** → Subject complement

El verbo **is** conecta a **she** con **a doctor**, indicando qué es el sujeto.

---

# 2. 🎯 Subject Complement

El **subject complement** es la palabra o grupo de palabras que aparece después del linking verb y proporciona información sobre el sujeto.

Puede ser:

- **Noun** → identifica al sujeto.
- **Pronoun** → identifica al sujeto.
- **Adjective** → describe al sujeto.

### Noun

> She **is** a doctor.

**a doctor** → Subject complement

### Adjective

> He **seems** tired.

**tired** → Subject complement

### Another Example

> They **became** friends.

**friends** → Subject complement

# 5. 🔄 Linking Verbs vs. Action Verbs

Some verbs can function as either **linking verbs** or **action verbs**, depending on their use in the sentence.

### Linking Verb

> The soup **tastes** delicious.

**tastes** connects the subject **the soup** with the adjective **delicious**.

### Action Verb

> She **tasted** the soup.

Here, **tasted** describes a physical action performed by **she**.

---

### Another Example

**Linking:**

> He **looks** tired.

**looks** → Links **he** with **tired**.

**Action:**

> He **looked** at the painting.

**looked** → Describes an action.

---

# 6. 🧠 The Verb "Be"

The verb **to be** is the most common linking verb.

Forms of **be** include:

- am
- is
- are
- was
- were
- be
- been
- being

### Examples

> I **am** tired.

> She **is** a teacher.

> They **are** happy.

> He **was** nervous.

> We **were** ready.

In each example, the verb **be** connects the subject with information describing or identifying it.

---

# 🔍 How to Identify a Linking Verb

Ask yourself:

> **Does the verb connect the subject to a description, identification, or state?**

If yes, the verb may be functioning as a linking verb.

### Example

> She **is** happy.

The verb **is** connects:

**She → happy**

Therefore, **is** is a linking verb.

---

# 🎯 Key Takeaways

- **Linking verbs** connect the subject to a **subject complement**.
- They generally express a **state, condition, characteristic, or relationship**, rather than an action.
- The complement can be a **noun, pronoun, or adjective**.
- **Be** is the most common linking verb.
- Other common linking verbs include **seem, become, look, feel, sound, remain,** and **grow**.
- Some verbs, such as **look, feel, taste,** and **sound**, can function as either linking or action verbs depending on the context.

### 💡 Quick Rule

> **Subject + Linking Verb + Subject Complement**

> She **is** intelligent.

> He **seems** tired.

> They **became** friends.

> The cake **smells** delicious.
