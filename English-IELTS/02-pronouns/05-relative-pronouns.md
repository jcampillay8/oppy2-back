# 🔗 Relative Pronouns — Pronombres Relativos

> **Relative pronouns** are used to introduce **relative clauses**, which provide additional information about a noun or noun phrase.
>
> They connect the relative clause to the main clause and establish a relationship between the two parts of the sentence.

The most common relative pronouns in English are:

- **who**
- **whom**
- **whose**
- **which**
- **that**

---

# 1. 👤 Who

**Who** is used to refer to **people**.

It usually functions as the **subject** of the relative clause.

### Examples

> The woman **who lives next door** is a doctor.

> The students **who studied hard** got excellent grades.

> I have a friend **who loves to travel**.

### Structure

**Person + who + Verb + ...**

> The man **who works here** is my manager.

Here:

- **The man** → noun being described
- **who** → relative pronoun
- **works here** → relative clause

---

# 2. 🎯 Whom

**Whom** is used to refer to **people when they are the object** of a verb or preposition.

It is more common in **formal English**.

### Examples

> The woman **whom I met yesterday** is a famous actress.

> The teacher **to whom I gave the gift** was very grateful.

> He has a neighbor **whom he often helps** with gardening.

### Compare

> The woman **who called me** is my teacher.

**Who** → subject of the relative clause.

> The woman **whom I called** is my teacher.

**Whom** → object of the relative clause.

### 💡 Informal English

In everyday English, **who** is often used instead of **whom**:

> The woman **who I met yesterday** is a famous actress.

This is very common in modern English.

---

# 3. 🔑 Whose

**Whose** indicates **possession**.

It can refer to **people or things**.

### Examples

> The boy **whose bicycle was stolen** reported it to the police.

> The house **whose roof is red** belongs to my friend.

> I read a book **whose author is highly acclaimed**.

### Structure

**Noun + whose + Possession + ...**

> The student **whose laptop was stolen** reported the incident.

Here, **whose laptop** indicates that the laptop belongs to the student.

---

# 4. 📘 Which

**Which** is generally used to refer to **things or animals**.

### Examples

> The car **which is parked in front of the house** is mine.

> The movie **which we watched last night** was very entertaining.

> I have a dog **which loves to play fetch**.

### Structure

**Thing/Animal + which + Relative Clause**

> The book **which I bought yesterday** is excellent.

---

# 5. 🔗 That

**That** can refer to **people, things, or animals**.

It is especially common in **restrictive (essential) relative clauses**.

### Examples

> The book **that I borrowed from the library** is very informative.

> The person **that called you** is waiting outside.

> The dog **that lives next door** is very friendly.

---

# 6. 📌 Restrictive Relative Clauses

A **restrictive relative clause** provides information that is **essential to identify the noun**.

Without the relative clause, the meaning may become unclear or change.

### Example

> The book **that I borrowed from the library** is very informative.

The relative clause identifies **which book** we are talking about.

### Common Relative Pronouns

Restrictive clauses can use:

- **who**
- **whom**
- **whose**
- **which**
- **that**

---

# 7. ℹ️ Non-Restrictive Relative Clauses

A **non-restrictive relative clause** provides **additional information** that is not essential to identify the noun.

It is normally separated by **commas**.

### Example

> John, **who is my neighbor**, invited me to his party.

The information **who is my neighbor** is additional information about John.

### Important Rule

**That** is generally **not used** in non-restrictive relative clauses.

> ❌ John, **that is my neighbor**, invited me.

> ✅ John, **who is my neighbor**, invited me.

---

# 📊 Relative Pronouns — Complete Overview

| Relative Pronoun | Refers To | Main Function | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **who** | People | Subject | The woman **who works here** is a doctor. |
| **whom** | People | Object | The woman **whom I met** was friendly. |
| **whose** | People / Things | Possession | The student **whose book was lost** is here. |
| **which** | Things / Animals | Subject or Object | The car **which I bought** is new. |
| **that** | People / Things / Animals | Subject or Object | The book **that I read** was excellent. |

---

# 🧠 Relative Pronoun vs. Relative Clause

A **relative pronoun** introduces or connects a **relative clause**.

### Example

> The woman **who lives next door** is a doctor.

### Breakdown

| Part | Function |
|---|---|
| **The woman** | Main noun |
| **who** | Relative pronoun |
| **lives next door** | Relative clause |
| **who lives next door** | Describes "the woman" |

---

# 🔄 Relative Pronoun as Subject vs. Object

Relative pronouns can have different roles inside the relative clause.

## Subject

> The man **who works here** is my manager.

**Who** performs the action.

> who → works

---

## Object

> The man **whom I met** is my manager.

**I** performs the action, while **whom** receives it.

> I → met → whom

In informal English:

> The man **who I met** is my manager.

---

# ⚠️ Important: Omitting the Relative Pronoun

When the relative pronoun functions as the **object**, it can often be omitted in a restrictive relative clause.

### With Relative Pronoun

> The book **that I bought** is excellent.

### Without Relative Pronoun

> The book **I bought** is excellent.

Both are grammatical.

However, when the relative pronoun is the **subject**, it generally cannot be omitted.

> ✅ The woman **who lives here** is a doctor.

> ❌ The woman **lives here** is a doctor.

---

# 🎯 Choosing the Correct Relative Pronoun

A simple way to choose the correct pronoun is to ask two questions:

### 1. What are you referring to?

- **Person** → who / whom / whose
- **Thing or animal** → which / whose
- **Person, thing, or animal** → that

### 2. What is the role?

- **Subject** → who / which / that
- **Object** → whom / which / that
- **Possession** → whose

---

# 📊 Quick Decision Table

| Situation | Relative Pronoun |
|---|---|
| Person as subject | **who** |
| Person as object | **whom** |
| Possession | **whose** |
| Thing/animal as subject | **which** |
| Thing/animal as object | **which** |
| Person/thing/animal in restrictive clause | **that** |

---

# 🧠 Key Takeaways

- **Relative pronouns** connect a noun to a **relative clause**.
- **Who** → people, usually as the subject.
- **Whom** → people as the object; more formal.
- **Whose** → possession; people or things.
- **Which** → things and animals.
- **That** → people, things, or animals, especially in restrictive clauses.
- **Restrictive clauses** provide essential identifying information.
- **Non-restrictive clauses** provide additional information and use commas.
- **That** is generally not used in non-restrictive clauses.
- A relative pronoun can sometimes be omitted when it functions as the **object**.

### 🎯 Quick Rule

> **WHO** → person / subject  
> **WHOM** → person / object  
> **WHOSE** → possession  
> **WHICH** → thing or animal  
> **THAT** → person, thing, or animal in a restrictive clause
