# 🔄 Pronoun Agreement — Concordancia de Pronombres

> **Pronoun agreement** refers to the grammatical matching of a pronoun with its **antecedent** in terms of **number, gender, and person**.
>
> It ensures that pronouns correctly agree with the nouns they replace or refer to in a sentence.

---

# 1. 🔢 Number Agreement

Pronouns must agree with their antecedents in terms of **number**:

- **Singular antecedent** → singular pronoun
- **Plural antecedent** → plural pronoun

### Singular

> The **dog** wagged **its** tail.

**Dog** → singular  
**Its** → singular

### Plural

> The **dogs** wagged **their** tails.

**Dogs** → plural  
**Their** → plural

### ❌ Incorrect

> The dog wagged **their** tail.

### ✅ Correct

> The dog wagged **its** tail.

---

# 2. ⚧️ Gender Agreement

Pronouns can also indicate **gender** when referring to a specific person.

### Traditionally gender-specific pronouns

- **he / him / his** → masculine
- **she / her / hers** → feminine

### Examples

> John said **he** would join us.

> Mary said **she** would join us.

When the gender is unknown, irrelevant, or the person uses gender-neutral pronouns, **they / them / their** can be used.

### Example

> Alex said **they** would join us.

> Someone left **their** phone on the table.

Using singular **they** is common in modern English when the person's gender is unknown or unspecified.

---

# 3. 👤 Person Agreement

Pronouns also need to correspond to the **grammatical person** of the antecedent.

## First Person

Refers to the **speaker or speakers**.

- **I**
- **me**
- **my**
- **we**
- **us**
- **our**

### Example

> **I** told them about **my** project.

---

## Second Person

Refers to the **person or people being spoken to**.

- **you**
- **your**
- **yours**

### Example

> **You** should bring **your** notebook.

---

## Third Person

Refers to someone or something **being spoken about**.

- **he**
- **she**
- **it**
- **they**
- **him**
- **her**
- **them**
- **his**
- **hers**
- **their**
- **theirs**

### Example

> **She** told **him** to come in.

---

# 4. 📊 Number, Gender, and Person

Pronoun agreement can involve several characteristics at the same time.

| Antecedent | Number | Person | Possible Pronoun |
|---|---|---|---|
| **John** | Singular | Third | he / him / his |
| **Mary** | Singular | Third | she / her / hers |
| **The dog** | Singular | Third | it / its |
| **The students** | Plural | Third | they / them / their |
| **I** | Singular | First | I / me / my |
| **We** | Plural | First | we / us / our |
| **You** | Singular/Plural | Second | you / your |

---

# 5. 🎯 Pronoun Agreement with Singular Nouns

A singular noun generally requires a singular pronoun.

### Example

> The **student** handed in **his** assignment.

Or, when gender is unknown or unspecified:

> The **student** handed in **their** assignment.

The use of singular **they/their** is standard in modern English when referring to one person without specifying gender.

---

# 6. 👥 Pronoun Agreement with Plural Nouns

Plural nouns require plural pronouns.

### Example

> The **students** handed in **their** assignments.

### Breakdown

- **students** → plural antecedent
- **their** → plural pronoun
- **assignments** → plural noun

### ❌ Incorrect

> The students handed in **his** assignments.

### ✅ Correct

> The students handed in **their** assignments.

---

# 7. 🔗 What Is an Antecedent?

An **antecedent** is the noun or noun phrase that a pronoun refers to.

### Example

> The **teacher** explained **her** lesson.

Here:

- **teacher** → antecedent
- **her** → pronoun

The pronoun **her** refers back to **teacher**.

### Another Example

> The **students** finished **their** homework.

- **students** → antecedent
- **their** → pronoun

---

# 8. ⚠️ Avoiding Pronoun Disagreement

Incorrect pronoun agreement can make a sentence grammatically incorrect or confusing.

### ❌ Disagreement in Number

> The **student** handed in **their** assignment.

This example can be acceptable in modern English when **their** is used as singular gender-neutral language. However, traditional grammar exercises may expect:

> The **student** handed in **his or her** assignment.

In contemporary English, singular **they/their** is generally preferred when gender is unknown or unspecified.

### ❌ Clear Mismatch

> The **students** handed in **his** assignments.

### ✅ Correct

> The **students** handed in **their** assignments.

---

# 9. 🧠 Pronoun Agreement and Clarity

Correct pronoun agreement helps readers understand **who or what a pronoun refers to**.

### Unclear

> When Maria spoke to Ana, **she** was upset.

It is unclear whether **she** refers to Maria or Ana.

### Clearer

> When Maria spoke to Ana, **Maria** was upset.

or:

> When Maria spoke to Ana, **Ana** was upset.

Good pronoun usage is therefore not only about grammatical agreement but also about **clarity of reference**.

---

# 📊 Common Pronoun Agreement Patterns

| Antecedent | Correct Pronoun | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **The dog** | its | The dog wagged **its** tail. |
| **The woman** | she / her | **She** finished **her** work. |
| **The man** | he / him | **He** finished **his** work. |
| **The students** | they / them / their | **They** finished **their** work. |
| **Someone** | they / them / their | Someone forgot **their** bag. |
| **Everyone** | they / them / their | Everyone should bring **their** ID. |

---

# 🎯 Key Takeaways

- A pronoun should clearly refer to its **antecedent**.
- Pronouns should generally agree with their antecedents in:
  - **Number**
  - **Person**
  - **Gender**, when relevant
- **Singular nouns** generally use singular pronouns.
- **Plural nouns** use plural pronouns.
- **Singular they/their** is commonly used when a person's gender is unknown or unspecified.
- Correct pronoun agreement improves **grammatical accuracy and clarity**.
- Always check that it is clear **what the pronoun refers to**.

### 💡 Quick Rule

> **NUMBER** → singular ↔ singular / plural ↔ plural  
>
> **PERSON** → first ↔ first / second ↔ second / third ↔ third  
>
> **GENDER** → use an appropriate pronoun when gender is known and relevant  
>
> **CLARITY** → make sure the antecedent is clear
