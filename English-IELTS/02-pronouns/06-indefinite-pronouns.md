# 🔀 Indefinite Pronouns — Pronombres Indefinidos

> **Indefinite pronouns** are pronouns used to refer to **non-specific people, places, things, or quantities**.
>
> They represent an unknown, general, or unspecified person or number of people or things.

Unlike specific pronouns such as **he**, **she**, or **they**, indefinite pronouns do not identify a particular individual or group.

---

# 1. 👤 Singular Indefinite Pronouns

Singular indefinite pronouns refer to **one person or thing**, although the exact identity may be unknown or unspecified.

### Common Singular Indefinite Pronouns

- **anyone**
- **anybody**
- **someone**
- **somebody**
- **nobody**
- **no one**
- **everyone**
- **everybody**
- **each**
- **either**
- **neither**

### Examples

> **Anyone** can participate in the competition.

> **Someone** is knocking at the door.

> **Nobody** likes to be ignored.

> **Everybody** enjoyed the party.

> **No one** has the right to judge others.

> **Somebody** left their umbrella behind.

---

# 2. 👥 Plural Indefinite Pronouns

Plural indefinite pronouns refer to **more than one person or thing**, without identifying them specifically.

### Common Plural Indefinite Pronouns

- **several**
- **many**
- **few**
- **both**
- **others**

### Examples

> **Several** students volunteered for the project.

> **Many** have tried, but **few** have succeeded.

> **Few** understand the complexity of the situation.

> **Both** are responsible for their actions.

> Some went home, while **others** stayed late.

---

# 3. 🔢 Indefinite Pronouns Indicating Quantity

Some indefinite pronouns express an **unspecified quantity or amount**.

### Common Examples

- **all**
- **some**
- **any**
- **none**
- **much**
- **little**
- **enough**
- **more**
- **most**

### Examples

> **All** are welcome to attend the meeting.

> **Some** of the books are missing from the shelf.

> **Any** will do; just pick one.

> **None** of the information was accurate.

---

# 4. 📊 Singular vs. Plural

It is important to know whether an indefinite pronoun is **singular or plural**, because this affects subject-verb agreement.

### Singular

> **Everyone is** ready.

> **Someone has** called.

> **Nobody knows** the answer.

### Plural

> **Several are** ready.

> **Many have** tried.

> **Both are** responsible.

---

# 5. ⚠️ Indefinite Pronouns and Verb Agreement

Some indefinite pronouns are always treated as **singular**.

### Singular

- everyone
- everybody
- someone
- somebody
- anyone
- anybody
- nobody
- no one
- each
- either
- neither

### Examples

> **Everyone is** welcome.

> **Someone has** taken my book.

> **Neither is** correct.

> **Each has** a different opinion.

Even though **everyone** and **everybody** refer to multiple people collectively, they normally take a **singular verb**.

> ❌ Everyone **are** ready.

> ✅ Everyone **is** ready.

---

# 6. 👥 Pronouns That Are Normally Plural

Some indefinite pronouns are normally treated as plural.

### Examples

- **both**
- **few**
- **many**
- **several**
- **others**

### Examples

> **Both are** correct.

> **Many have** applied.

> **Several were** selected.

> **Few understand** the problem.

---

# 7. 🔢 Indefinite Pronouns for Quantity

Some pronouns can refer to an unspecified quantity.

### All

> **All** are welcome.

### Some

> **Some** are missing.

### Any

> **Any** will work.

### None

> **None** were available.

The verb agreement with words such as **all**, **some**, and **none** can depend on whether they refer to **countable plural nouns** or **uncountable nouns**.

### Countable

> **Some of the students are** absent.

### Uncountable

> **Some of the information is** incorrect.

---

# 8. 📌 Indefinite Pronouns vs. Indefinite Determiners

Some indefinite words can function as either **pronouns** or **determiners**, depending on whether they stand alone or come before a noun.

## Indefinite Pronoun

The word stands alone.

> **Many** have applied.

> **Some** are missing.

## Indefinite Determiner

The word comes before a noun.

> **Many students** have applied.

> **Some books** are missing.

### Compare

| Function | Example |
|---|---|
| **Pronoun** | **Many** have applied. |
| **Determiner** | **Many students** have applied. |
| **Pronoun** | **Some** are missing. |
| **Determiner** | **Some books** are missing. |

---

# 9. 🧠 Gender-Neutral Language

Indefinite pronouns such as **someone**, **somebody**, **everyone**, and **anyone** do not specify gender.

Modern English commonly uses **they/their** when the person's gender is unknown or irrelevant.

### Example

> **Someone left their umbrella behind.**

This avoids assuming whether the person is male or female.

# 🎯 Common Indefinite Pronoun Groups

## Some-

Generally used when referring to an unspecified person or thing.

- **someone**
- **somebody**
- **something**

> **Someone** called me.

---

## Any-

Commonly used in questions, negatives, or general statements.

- **anyone**
- **anybody**
- **anything**

> **Does anyone** know the answer?

> I don't know **anything** about it.

---

## Every-

Refers collectively to all members of a group.

- **everyone**
- **everybody**
- **everything**

> **Everyone is** invited.

> **Everything is** ready.

---

## No-

Indicates zero people or things.

- **nobody**
- **no one**
- **nothing**

> **Nobody knows** the answer.

> **Nothing is** impossible.

---

# 🧠 Key Takeaways

- **Indefinite pronouns** refer to people, things, or quantities without specifying exactly who or what.
- Some are **singular**:
  - everyone
  - someone
  - anyone
  - nobody
- Some are **plural**:
  - many
  - few
  - several
  - both
  - others
- Some indicate **quantity**:
  - all
  - some
  - any
  - none
- Singular indefinite pronouns generally take **singular verbs**.
- Some indefinite words can function as either **pronouns** or **determiners**.

### 🎯 Quick Rule

> **SINGULAR** → everyone, someone, anyone, nobody  
>
> **PLURAL** → many, few, several, both, others  
>
> **QUANTITY** → all, some, any, none

> 💡 **Remember:** The key idea behind indefinite pronouns is **non-specific reference** — they allow us to talk about people, things, or quantities without identifying them precisely.
