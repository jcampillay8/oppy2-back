
# ❓ Interrogative Pronouns — Pronombres Interrogativos

> **Interrogative pronouns** are pronouns used to **introduce questions** and obtain information about people, things, choices, ownership, or identity.
>
> They can replace nouns or noun phrases when the specific information is unknown.

The main interrogative pronouns in English are:

- **who**
- **whom**
- **whose**
- **which**
- **what**

---

# 1. 👤 Who

**Who** is used to ask about **people**, especially when the person is the subject of the question.

### Examples

> **Who** is the new employee joining our team?

> **Who** won the singing competition?

> **Who** is responsible for this project?

### Meaning

**Who = qué persona / quién**

### Example Structure

**Who + Verb + ...?**

> **Who** called you?

> **Who** works here?

---

# 2. 🎯 Whom

**Whom** is used to ask about **people who are the object of a verb or preposition**.

It is more formal and is less common in everyday spoken English, where **who** is often used instead.

### Examples

> To **whom** did you lend your phone?

> **Whom** did she invite to the wedding?

> With **whom** are you going to the party?

### Meaning

**Whom = a quién / con quién**

### Quick Comparison

> **Who** called you?  
> → Who = subject

> **Whom** did you call?  
> → Whom = object

### 💡 Practical Tip

In informal English, it is common to hear:

> **Who did you call?**

instead of:

> **Whom did you call?**

Both can be grammatically acceptable, but **whom** sounds more formal.

---

# 3. 🔑 Whose

**Whose** is used to ask about **ownership or possession**.

It can be used before a noun or independently.

### Examples

> **Whose car** is blocking the driveway?

> **Whose idea** was it to start the book club?

> **Whose turn** is it to do the dishes?

### Meaning

**Whose = de quién**

### Structure

**Whose + Noun + ...?**

> **Whose book** is this?

> **Whose phone** is ringing?

It can also stand alone:

> **Whose is this?**

---

# 4. 🔎 Which

**Which** is used when asking someone to choose or identify something from a **limited or known set of options**.

### Examples

> **Which dress** should I wear to the party?

> **Which restaurant** serves the best pizza in town?

> **Which movie** did you watch last night?

### Meaning

**Which = cuál / cuáles**

### Structure

**Which + Noun + ...?**

> **Which car** is yours?

> **Which book** do you want?

It can also stand alone:

> **Which** do you prefer?

### 💡 Which vs. What

A useful distinction is:

- **Which** → a limited or specific set of options.
- **What** → a broader or open-ended choice.

> **Which** color do you prefer: blue or green?

> **What** color do you like?

---

# 5. ❓ What

**What** is used to ask about **things, information, actions, ideas, or identities**.

### Examples

> **What time** does the concert start?

> **What** are your plans for the weekend?

> **What** is the capital city of France?

### Meaning

**What = qué / cuál**

### Structure

**What + Noun + ...?**

> **What time** is it?

> **What movie** did you watch?

It can also stand alone:

> **What** happened?

> **What** do you want?

---

# 📊 Complete Overview

| Pronoun | Main Use | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **who** | People | quién | **Who** called you? |
| **whom** | People as objects | a quién / con quién | **Whom** did you invite? |
| **whose** | Possession | de quién | **Whose** book is this? |
| **which** | Limited choice | cuál / cuáles | **Which** do you prefer? |
| **what** | General information | qué / cuál | **What** happened? |

---

# 🧠 Who vs. Whom

This distinction is particularly important for formal English.

### Who → Subject

> **Who** invited you?

**Who** performs the action.

### Whom → Object

> **Whom** did you invite?

The person represented by **whom** receives the action.

### Quick Test

Try replacing the pronoun with:

- **he/she/they** → use **who**
- **him/her/them** → use **whom**

> **Who** called?

→ **She** called.

> **Whom** did you call?

→ I called **her**.

---

# 🧠 Which vs. What

Both can be used to ask for information or make a choice, but there is an important difference.

### Which

Used when the possible options are **limited or identifiable**.

> **Which** shirt do you want, the blue one or the black one?

### What

Used when the possibilities are **open or unlimited**.

> **What** do you want to eat?

---

# ⚠️ Important: Interrogative Pronoun vs. Interrogative Determiner

Some interrogative words can function as **pronouns** or **determiners**.

### Interrogative Determiner

The word is followed by a noun.

> **Which book** do you want?

> **What time** is the meeting?

### Interrogative Pronoun

The word stands alone and replaces the noun.

> **Which** do you want?

> **What** happened?

---

# 🎯 Common Question Structures

### Who

**Who + Verb + ...?**

> Who called you?

### Whom

**Whom + Auxiliary + Subject + Verb?**

> Whom did you invite?

### Whose

**Whose + Noun + Verb + ...?**

> Whose car is this?

### Which

**Which + Noun + Auxiliary + Subject + Verb?**

> Which movie did you watch?

### What

**What + Auxiliary + Subject + Verb?**

> What did you buy?

---

# 🔑 Key Takeaways

- **Who** → asks about a person as the subject.
- **Whom** → asks about a person as the object; mainly formal.
- **Whose** → asks about possession.
- **Which** → asks about a specific or limited choice.
- **What** → asks for general or open-ended information.
- Some interrogative words can function as **pronouns** or **determiners** depending on whether they stand alone or are followed by a noun.

### 🎯 Quick Rule

> **WHO** → Person  
> **WHOM** → Person as object  
> **WHOSE** → Possession  
> **WHICH** → Limited choice  
> **WHAT** → General information
