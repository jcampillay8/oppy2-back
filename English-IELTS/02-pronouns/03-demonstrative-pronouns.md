# 👉 Demonstrative Pronouns — Pronombres Demostrativos

> **Demonstrative pronouns** are used to **point to or identify specific people, places, things, or ideas** in relation to the speaker or the context.
>
> In English, there are four demonstrative pronouns:
>
> **this, that, these, those**

---

# 1. 📊 The Four Demonstrative Pronouns

| Pronoun | Number | Proximity | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **this** | Singular | Close | este / esta / esto |
| **that** | Singular | Farther away | ese / esa / eso / aquel / aquella |
| **these** | Plural | Close | estos / estas |
| **those** | Plural | Farther away | esos / esas / aquellos / aquellas |

---

# 2. 📍 This

**This** is used to indicate **one person, object, place, or idea that is close** to the speaker.

### Structure

**This + Singular Noun**

### Examples

> **This book** is interesting.

> **This car** is expensive.

> **This computer** is new.

When **this** replaces the noun, it functions as a demonstrative pronoun:

> **This** is interesting.

> **This** is my computer.

---

# 3. 📍 These

**These** is the plural form of **this**.

It is used to indicate **multiple people, objects, places, or ideas that are close** to the speaker.

### Structure

**These + Plural Noun**

### Examples

> **These cookies** are delicious.

> **These books** are mine.

> **These shoes** are new.

When **these** replaces the noun:

> **These** are delicious.

> **These** are my books.

---

# 4. 📌 That

**That** is used to indicate **one person, object, place, or idea that is farther away** from the speaker.

### Structure

**That + Singular Noun**

### Examples

> **That car** is expensive.

> **That house** is beautiful.

> **That building** is very old.

When **that** replaces the noun:

> **That** is my car.

> **That** looks expensive.

---

# 5. 📌 Those

**Those** is the plural form of **that**.

It is used to indicate **multiple people, objects, places, or ideas that are farther away** from the speaker.

### Structure

**Those + Plural Noun**

### Examples

> **Those flowers** are beautiful.

> **Those cars** are expensive.

> **Those buildings** are very old.

When **those** replaces the noun:

> **Those** are beautiful.

> **Those** belong to me.

---

# 6. 📏 Proximity

The main distinction between demonstrative pronouns is based on:

1. **Number** → singular or plural
2. **Proximity** → close or farther away

### Singular

**Close → this**

> **This** is my phone.

**Farther away → that**

> **That** is my car.

### Plural

**Close → these**

> **These** are my books.

**Farther away → those**

> **Those** are my shoes.

---

# 7. 🧠 Demonstrative Pronoun vs. Demonstrative Determiner

The same four words can function either as **demonstrative pronouns** or **demonstrative determiners**.

## Demonstrative Determiner

The demonstrative is followed by a noun.

> **This book** is interesting.

> **Those flowers** are beautiful.

Here, **this** and **those** determine which nouns we are referring to.

---

## Demonstrative Pronoun

The demonstrative **replaces the noun**.

> **This** is interesting.

> **Those** are beautiful.

Here, **this** and **those** stand alone.

### Compare

| Function | Example |
|---|---|
| **Determiner** | **This book** is mine. |
| **Pronoun** | **This** is mine. |
| **Determiner** | **Those cars** are expensive. |
| **Pronoun** | **Those** are expensive. |

---

# 8. 🗣️ Demonstratives in Context

Demonstrative pronouns do not always refer to physical distance.

They can also refer to **ideas, situations, events, or things previously mentioned**.

### Example

> I failed my exam. **That** was disappointing.

Here, **that** refers to the situation of failing the exam rather than to a physical object.

### Another Example

> We finally finished the project. **This** is a great achievement.

Here, **this** refers to the recently completed project or situation.

### Examples

|---|---|---|
| **Close** | **This is my book.** | **These are my books.** |
| **Farther away** | **That is my car.** | **Those are my cars.** |

# ⚠️ Common Mistakes

## Singular vs. Plural

> ❌ **This books** are mine.

> ✅ **These books** are mine.

> ❌ **Those book** is mine.

> ✅ **That book** is mine.

---

## Verb Agreement

Demonstrative pronouns must agree with the appropriate singular or plural form.

> **This is** my book.

> **That is** my car.

> **These are** my books.

> **Those are** my cars.

---

# 🧠 Key Takeaways

- There are **four demonstrative pronouns**:
  - **this**
  - **that**
  - **these**
  - **those**
- **This** → singular + close.
- **These** → plural + close.
- **That** → singular + farther away.
- **Those** → plural + farther away.
- Demonstratives can refer to **physical objects** as well as **ideas, situations, and events**.
- The same words can function as either **demonstrative pronouns** or **demonstrative determiners**.

### 🎯 Quick Rule

> **THIS** → one + close  
> **THESE** → many + close  
> **THAT** → one + far  
> **THOSE** → many + far
