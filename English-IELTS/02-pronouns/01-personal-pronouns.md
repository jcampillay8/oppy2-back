# 👤 Personal Pronouns — Pronombres Personales

> **Personal pronouns** in English are used to refer to specific **people, animals, things, or entities**. They replace nouns or noun phrases and can function as **subjects, objects, or expressions of possession** in sentences.

---

# 1. 🧑 Subject Pronouns

**Subject pronouns** are used when the pronoun performs the action or functions as the subject of the sentence.

### Singular

- **I**
- **you**
- **he**
- **she**
- **it**

### Plural

- **we**
- **you**
- **they**

### Examples

> **I am going to the store.**

> **She works in a hospital.**

> **They live in Chile.**

### Structure

**Subject + Verb + ...**

> **He** plays football.

> **We** study English.

---

# 2. 🎯 Object Pronouns

**Object pronouns** are used when the pronoun receives the action of the verb or follows a preposition.

### Singular

- **me**
- **you**
- **him**
- **her**
- **it**

### Plural

- **us**
- **you**
- **them**

### Examples

> **She gave it to me.**

> **I saw him yesterday.**

> **They invited us to the party.**

### Structure

**Subject + Verb + Object**

> She called **me**.

> I helped **him**.

> They invited **us**.

---

# 3. 🔑 Possessive Pronouns

**Possessive pronouns** indicate ownership or possession and can stand alone without a following noun.

### Singular

- **mine**
- **yours**
- **his**
- **hers**
- **its**

### Plural

- **ours**
- **yours**
- **theirs**

### Examples

> **Is this book yours?**

> **That car is hers.**

> **The house is theirs.**

### Compare

> This is **my book**. → possessive determiner

> This book is **mine**. → possessive pronoun

---

# 4. 📌 Possessive Determiners

**Possessive determiners** indicate possession and are placed **before a noun**.

### Singular

- **my**
- **your**
- **his**
- **her**
- **its**

### Plural

- **our**
- **your**
- **their**

### Examples

> **I lost my keys.**

> **She forgot her phone.**

> **They sold their house.**

### Structure

**Possessive Determiner + Noun**

> **my** car

> **your** book

> **his** computer

> **their** house

---

# 5. 🔄 Reflexive Pronouns

**Reflexive pronouns** are used when the subject and the object of the sentence refer to the **same person or thing**.

### Singular

- **myself**
- **yourself**
- **himself**
- **herself**
- **itself**

### Plural

- **ourselves**
- **yourselves**
- **themselves**

### Examples

> **He taught himself** how to play the guitar.

> **She hurt herself.**

> **We prepared ourselves for the exam.**

### Structure

**Subject + Verb + Reflexive Pronoun**

> He taught **himself**.

> She introduced **herself**.

> They prepared **themselves**.

# 👥 Person and Number

Personal pronouns change according to **person** and **number**.

## First Person

Refers to the speaker or speakers.

- **I** → one speaker
- **we** → speaker + others

### Examples

> **I** am studying English.

> **We** are studying English.

---

## Second Person

Refers to the person or people being spoken to.

- **you** → singular or plural

### Examples

> **You** are my friend.

> **You** are my friends.

> 💡 **Important:** English uses **you** for both singular and plural.

---

## Third Person

Refers to someone or something being talked about.

### Singular

- **he**
- **she**
- **it**

### Plural

- **they**

### Examples

> **He** works here.

> **She** lives in London.

> **It** is expensive.

> **They** are students.

---

# ⚠️ Common Mistakes

## Subject vs. Object

After a verb or preposition, use the appropriate **object pronoun**.

> ❌ She gave the book to **I**.

> ✅ She gave the book to **me**.

---

## Possessive Determiner vs. Possessive Pronoun

A possessive determiner must be followed by a noun.

> ✅ This is **my book**.

A possessive pronoun stands alone.

> ✅ This book is **mine**.

> ❌ This book is **my**.

---

## Apostrophes

Possessive pronouns do **not** use apostrophes.

> ❌ your's

> ❌ her's

> ❌ their's

> ✅ yours

> ✅ hers

> ✅ theirs

---

# 🧠 Key Takeaways

- **Subject pronouns** → perform the action.
- **Object pronouns** → receive the action or follow a preposition.
- **Possessive determiners** → show possession before a noun.
- **Possessive pronouns** → show possession without a following noun.
- **Reflexive pronouns** → refer back to the subject.
- Pronouns change according to **person** and **number**.
- English uses **you** for both singular and plural.

### Quick Example

> **I** gave **her** **my** book because it was **mine**, and she thanked **me** herself.

| Word | Function |
|---|---|
| **I** | Subject pronoun |
| **her** | Object pronoun |
| **my** | Possessive determiner |
| **mine** | Possessive pronoun |
| **me** | Object pronoun |
| **herself** | Reflexive pronoun |
