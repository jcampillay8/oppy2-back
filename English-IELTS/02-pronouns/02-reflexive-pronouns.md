# 🔄 Reflexive Pronouns — Pronombres Reflexivos

> **Reflexive pronouns** are a specific type of pronoun used when the **subject of a sentence performs an action on itself**.
>
> They indicate that the subject and the object of the sentence refer to the **same person or thing**.

---

# 1. 📚 Reflexive Pronouns in English

Reflexive pronouns are generally formed using:

- **-self** → singular
- **-selves** → plural

### Singular

- **myself**
- **yourself**
- **himself**
- **herself**
- **itself**

### Plural

- **ourselves**
- **yourselves**
- **themselves**

---

# 2. 📊 Complete Overview

| Person | Subject Pronoun | Reflexive Pronoun |
|---|---|---|
| 1st Singular | **I** | **myself** |
| 2nd Singular | **you** | **yourself** |
| 3rd Singular | **he** | **himself** |
| 3rd Singular | **she** | **herself** |
| 3rd Singular | **it** | **itself** |
| 1st Plural | **we** | **ourselves** |
| 2nd Plural | **you** | **yourselves** |
| 3rd Plural | **they** | **themselves** |

---

# 3. 🎯 Main Function

Reflexive pronouns are typically used when the **subject and object refer to the same person or thing**.

### Basic Structure

**Subject + Verb + Reflexive Pronoun**

### Examples

> **I taught myself** how to play the piano.

> **He blamed himself** for the mistake.

> **She enjoyed herself** at the party.

> **The cat is grooming itself.**

In each case, the person or thing performing the action is also receiving the action.

# 5. 🧠 Reflexive Action

The easiest way to understand reflexive pronouns is to think:

> **The subject does something to the same person or thing.**

### Compare

> **John washed John.** ❌  
> **John washed himself.** ✅

The second sentence makes it clear that **John washed himself**.

### Another Example

> **She looked at her.**

This means that **she** looked at another female person.

> **She looked at herself.**

This means that **she** looked at **the same person as the subject**.

---

# 6. 💆 Common Uses

Reflexive pronouns are frequently used with actions involving:

### Self-care

- wash yourself
- dress yourself
- take care of yourself

### Self-directed actions

- teach yourself
- introduce yourself
- prepare yourself

### Emotions or reactions

- blame yourself
- enjoy yourself
- control yourself

### Examples

> **Take care of yourself.**

> **She introduced herself to the team.**

> **He taught himself Python.**

> **They prepared themselves for the exam.**

---

# 7. ⚠️ Reflexive Pronouns Are Not Always Necessary

A reflexive pronoun should only be used when the action is directed back toward the subject or when the reflexive form serves an emphatic function.

### Compare

> **I washed myself.**  
> → I washed my own body.

> **I washed the car.**  
> → The action is directed toward the car, so **myself** is not needed.

---

# 8. 💡 Reflexive vs. Object Pronouns

Compare the following sentences:

| Sentence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **I hurt myself.** | I hurt me. |
| **I hurt him.** | I hurt another male person. |
| **She introduced herself.** | She introduced her own person. |
| **She introduced her.** | She introduced another female person. |

The reflexive pronoun changes the meaning by showing that the **subject and object are the same**.

---

# 🧠 Key Takeaways

- Reflexive pronouns refer back to the **subject** of the sentence.
- They are commonly used when the **subject and object are the same person or thing**.
- Singular forms generally end in **-self**.
- Plural forms generally end in **-selves**.
- They can express **self-directed actions**, such as:
  - **teach yourself**
  - **blame yourself**
  - **prepare yourself**
  - **take care of yourself**
- They are **not necessary** when the action is directed toward another person or thing.

### Quick Rule

> **Subject + Verb + Same Person/Thing → Reflexive Pronoun**

**I → myself**  
**you → yourself**  
**he → himself**  
**she → herself**  
**it → itself**  
**we → ourselves**  
**you → yourselves**  
**they → themselves**
