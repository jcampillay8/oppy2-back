# Prepositions of Time

**Prepositions of time** are words used to indicate when an action, event, or situation happens.

They help establish a temporal relationship between different elements of a sentence.

## 1. Common Prepositions of Time

Some of the most common prepositions of time include:

- `in`
- `on`
- `at`
- `during`
- `before`
- `after`
- `since`
- `until`

---

## 2. "In"

**"In"** is used for **general periods of time**, including:

- Months
- Years
- Seasons
- Decades
- Centuries
- Parts of the day
- Longer periods of time

### Examples

- "in July"
- "in 2022"
- "in the summer"
- "in the 1990s"
- "in the 21st century"
- "in the morning"

### Example Sentences

> "I was born **in 1995**."

> "We usually travel **in July**."

> "She exercises **in the morning**."

### Key Idea

> **IN → General or longer periods**

---

## 3. "On"

**"On"** is used for **specific days and dates**.

It is commonly used with:

- Days of the week
- Specific dates
- Special days
- Days + parts of the day

### Examples

- "on Monday"
- "on Friday"
- "on June 5th"
- "on Christmas Day"
- "on Monday morning"

### Example Sentences

> "I have a meeting **on Monday**."

> "Her birthday is **on June 5th**."

> "We arrived **on Friday morning**."

### Key Idea

> **ON → Specific days and dates**

---

## 4. "At"

**"At"** is used for **specific points in time**.

It is commonly used with:

- Exact times
- Clock times
- Noon
- Midnight
- Certain fixed expressions

### Examples

- "at 8 o'clock"
- "at 10:30"
- "at noon"
- "at midnight"
- "at night"

### Example Sentences

> "The meeting starts **at 8 o'clock**."

> "I usually go to bed **at midnight**."

> "She works **at night**."

### Key Idea

> **AT → Specific points in time**

---

## 5. "During"

**"During"** is used to indicate that something happens **within a particular period or event**.

### Examples

- "during the summer"
- "during the meeting"
- "during the movie"
- "during the vacation"
- "during the night"

### Example Sentences

> "I met him **during the summer**."

> "Please don't talk **during the meeting**."

> "She fell asleep **during the movie**."

### Key Idea

> **DURING → Within a period or event**

---

## 6. "Before"

**"Before"** indicates that something happens **earlier than another event or point in time**.

### Examples

- "before dinner"
- "before the meeting"
- "before Monday"
- "before sunrise"

### Example Sentences

> "I always have coffee **before work**."

> "Please arrive **before 8 o'clock**."

> "She called me **before the meeting**."

### Key Idea

> **BEFORE → Earlier than**

---

## 7. "After"

**"After"** indicates that something happens **later than another event or point in time**.

### Examples

- "after dinner"
- "after the movie"
- "after work"
- "after midnight"

### Example Sentences

> "We went home **after the movie**."

> "I usually exercise **after work**."

> "She arrived **after midnight**."

### Key Idea

> **AFTER → Later than**

---

## 8. "Since"

**"Since"** indicates the **starting point of a period that continues until the present**.

It is commonly used with the **present perfect** or **present perfect continuous**.

### Examples

- "since yesterday"
- "since 2010"
- "since Monday"
- "since last year"
- "since I was a child"

### Example Sentences

> "I have lived here **since 2010**."

> "She has worked there **since Monday**."

> "They have known each other **since childhood**."

### Key Idea

> **SINCE → Starting point of a continuing period**

---

## 9. "Until"

**"Until"** indicates the **endpoint of a period or activity**.

It means that something continues up to a specific time or event.

### Examples

- "until tomorrow"
- "until Monday"
- "until midnight"
- "until the end of the week"
- "until the meeting ends"

### Example Sentences

> "I will stay here **until tomorrow**."

> "The store is open **until midnight**."

> "We worked **until the end of the day**."

### Key Idea

> **UNTIL → Endpoint of a period**

---

# 10. IN vs. ON vs. AT

One of the most important distinctions when learning prepositions of time is the difference between **in, on, and at**.

| **Preposition** | **Used For** | **Examples** |
|---|---|---|
| `in` | General or longer periods | `in July`, `in 2022`, `in the morning` |
| `on` | Specific days and dates | `on Monday`, `on June 5th` |
| `at` | Specific points in time | `at 8 o'clock`, `at noon`, `at midnight` |

### Simple Rule

> **IN → Period**

> **ON → Day / Date**

> **AT → Exact Time**

### Example

> "I was born **in 1995**."

> "I was born **on June 5th**."

> "I was born **at 8 o'clock**."

---

# 11. Quick Reference

| **Preposition** | **Main Use** | **Example** |
|---|---|---|
| `in` | General periods | "in 2022" |
| `on` | Days and dates | "on Monday" |
| `at` | Exact times | "at 8 o'clock" |
| `during` | Within a period/event | "during the meeting" |
| `before` | Earlier than | "before dinner" |
| `after` | Later than | "after work" |
| `since` | Starting point continuing to the present | "since 2010" |
| `until` | Endpoint of a period | "until midnight" |

# 12. Common Time Expressions

Some expressions use specific prepositions that are important to learn as fixed combinations.

### With "in"

- `in the morning`
- `in the afternoon`
- `in the evening`
- `in July`
- `in 2022`
- `in the future`
- `in the past`

### With "on"

- `on Monday`
- `on Friday`
- `on June 5th`
- `on Christmas Day`
- `on Monday morning`

### With "at"

- `at 8 o'clock`
- `at noon`
- `at midnight`
- `at night`
- `at the moment`
- `at the weekend` *(British English)*

---

# Important Note

Prepositions of time are essential for expressing **when something happens** and establishing clear temporal relationships.

The most important distinction to remember is:

> **IN → General period**

> **ON → Specific day or date**

> **AT → Specific point in time**

Other prepositions such as **during, before, after, since, and until** help describe relationships between events and periods of time.

> **Key idea:** Choosing the correct preposition of time makes the timing and sequence of events clear and precise.
