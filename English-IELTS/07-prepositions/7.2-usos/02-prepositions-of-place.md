# Prepositions of Place

**Prepositions of place** are words used to indicate the **location, position, or spatial relationship** of a person, object, or place.

They help answer questions such as:

- **Where?**
- **Where is something located?**
- **What is the position of something in relation to another object?**

## 1. Common Prepositions of Place

Some of the most common prepositions of place include:

- `in`
- `on`
- `at`
- `under`
- `above`
- `below`
- `inside`
- `outside`
- `between`
- `among`

---

## 2. "In"

**"In"** is used to indicate that someone or something is **inside the boundaries or confines of a place or space**.

### Examples

- "in the house"
- "in the city"
- "in the room"
- "in the box"
- "in the garden"

### Example Sentences

> "She is **in the kitchen**."

> "They live **in Santiago**."

> "There is a book **in the box**."

### Key Idea

> **IN → Inside an area, space, or container**

---

## 3. "On"

**"On"** is used to indicate that something is **on a surface or in contact with a surface**.

### Examples

- "on the table"
- "on the floor"
- "on the wall"
- "on the roof"
- "on the desk"

### Example Sentences

> "The book is **on the table**."

> "There is a picture **on the wall**."

> "The keys are **on the desk**."

### Key Idea

> **ON → On a surface**

---

## 4. "At"

**"At"** is used to indicate a **specific point, location, or place**.

It often refers to a location viewed as a point rather than as an enclosed area.

### Examples

- "at the park"
- "at the bus stop"
- "at the office"
- "at the airport"
- "at school"

### Example Sentences

> "She is **at the bus stop**."

> "I am **at the office**."

> "They are waiting **at the airport**."

### Key Idea

> **AT → Specific point or location**

---

> "The cat is **under the table**."

> "We stood **under the bridge**."

### Key Idea

> **UNDER → Lower than something**

---

# 6. "Above"

**"Above"** indicates that something is **higher than or over another object or position**.

### Examples

- "above the clouds"
- "above the door"
- "above the surface"
- "above the table"

### Example Sentences

> "The plane is flying **above the clouds**."

> "There is a clock **above the door**."

### Key Idea

> **ABOVE → Higher than something**

---

# 7. "Below"

**"Below"** indicates that something is **lower than another object, position, or level**.

### Examples

- "below sea level"

- "below the surface"
- "below the bridge"

### Example Sentences

> "The village is **below sea level**."

> **BELOW → Lower than a reference point or level**

---

# 8. "Inside"

**"Inside"** refers to being **within the interior of something**.

It is similar to **"in"**, but it can place stronger emphasis on the interior.

### Examples

- "inside the house"
- "inside the box"
- "inside the car"
- "inside the building"

### Example Sentences

> "The children are **inside the house**."

> "There is something **inside the box**."

### Key Idea

> **INSIDE → Within the interior**

---

# 9. "Outside"

**"Outside"** indicates that someone or something is **beyond the boundaries or exterior of a place**.

### Examples

- "outside the building"
- "outside the house"
- "outside the fence"
- "outside the room"

### Example Sentences

> "The children are playing **outside the house**."

> "Please wait **outside the building**."

### Key Idea

> **OUTSIDE → Beyond the boundaries**

---

# 10. "Between"

**"Between"** indicates a position or relationship involving **two people, objects, places, or points**.

### Examples

- "between the two chairs"
- "between the buildings"
- "between the two cities"
- "between Monday and Tuesday"

### Example Sentences

> "The table is **between the two chairs**."

> "The bank is **between the supermarket and the pharmacy**."

### Key Idea

> **BETWEEN → In the space separating two things**

---

# 11. "Among"

**"Among"** indicates a position or relationship **within a group or among multiple people or objects**.

### Examples

- "among friends"
- "among the books"
- "among the students"
- "among the trees"

### Example Sentences

> "She was sitting **among her friends**."

> "I found the document **among the books**."

### Key Idea

> **AMONG → Within a group or collection**

---

# 12. IN vs. ON vs. AT

One of the most important distinctions when learning prepositions of place is the difference between **in, on, and at**.

| **Preposition** | **Main Use** | **Examples** |
|---|---|---|
| `in` | Inside an area or space | `in the room`, `in the city` |
| `on` | On a surface | `on the table`, `on the wall` |
| `at` | Specific point or location | `at the bus stop`, `at the office` |

### Simple Rule

> **IN → Inside an area**

> **ON → Surface**

> **AT → Specific point**

### Example

> "She is **in the office**."

Focus: She is physically **inside the office**.

> "The documents are **on the desk**."

Focus: The documents are **on a surface**.

> "She is **at the office**."

Focus: The office is the **location where she is**.

# Important Note

Prepositions of place are essential for describing **where something or someone is located** and establishing clear spatial relationships.

The most important distinction to remember is:

> **IN → Inside an area or space**

> **ON → On a surface**

> **AT → Specific point or location**

Other prepositions such as **under, above, below, inside, outside, between, and among** allow us to describe more precise spatial relationships.

> **Key idea:** Choosing the correct preposition of place makes the position and spatial relationship between objects, people, and places clear and precise.
