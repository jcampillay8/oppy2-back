# Prepositional Phrases as Adverbial

**Prepositional phrases as adverbials** are prepositional phrases that function as adverbials in a sentence.

They provide additional information about an **action, event, or circumstance**, usually modifying a verb.

They commonly answer questions such as:

- **Where?**
- **When?**
- **How?**
- **Why?**
- **Under what condition?**
- **For what purpose?**

---

## 1. Structure

A prepositional phrase used as an adverbial generally follows this structure:

> **Preposition + Object**

The object can be a **noun, pronoun, or noun phrase**.

### Example

> "She studied **in the library**."

- **Preposition:** `in`
- **Object:** `the library`
- **Prepositional phrase:** `in the library`
- **Function:** Adverbial
- **Modifies:** `studied`

The phrase tells us:

> **Where did she study?** → In the library.

---

# 2. Adverbial Prepositional Phrases of Place

These phrases indicate **where an action happens**.

### Examples

> "He works **in the office**."

> "They played **in the park**."

> "She waited **at the bus stop**."

> "The children ran **through the garden**."

### Questions

They answer:

> **Where?**

> **Where did they play?** → In the park.

> **Where did she wait?** → At the bus stop.

---

# 3. Adverbial Prepositional Phrases of Time

These phrases indicate **when an action happens**.

### Examples

> "We arrived **in the morning**."

> "She left **after the meeting**."

> "They worked **until midnight**."

> "He has lived here **since 2010**."

### Questions

They answer:

> **When?**

> **When did they arrive?** → In the morning.

> **When did they leave?** → After the meeting.

---

# 4. Adverbial Prepositional Phrases of Manner

These phrases describe **how an action is performed**.

### Examples

> "She spoke **with confidence**."

> "He completed the task **with great care**."

> "They worked **with enthusiasm**."

### Questions

They answer:

> **How?**

> **How did she speak?** → With confidence.

> **How did he complete the task?** → With great care.

---

# 5. Adverbial Prepositional Phrases of Cause

These phrases explain **why something happens** or indicate the cause of an event.

### Examples

> "The flight was delayed **because of the weather**."

> "He missed the meeting **due to traffic**."

> "The game was canceled **because of the rain**."

### Questions

They answer:

> **Why?**

> **Why was the flight delayed?** → Because of the weather.

> **Why did he miss the meeting?** → Due to traffic.

---

# 6. Adverbial Prepositional Phrases of Purpose

These phrases indicate **the purpose or reason for an action**.

### Examples

> "She went to the store **for some groceries**."

> "He studied hard **for the exam**."

> "They met **for a discussion**."

### Questions

They answer:

> **For what purpose?**

> **Why did he study hard?** → For the exam.

---

# 7. Adverbial Prepositional Phrases of Direction

These phrases indicate **movement toward, away from, or through a location**.

### Examples

> "She walked **to the station**."

> "They drove **through the tunnel**."

> "He ran **toward the exit**."

> "The dog jumped **over the fence**."

### Questions

They answer:

> **Where to?**

> **Where from?**

> **Through where?**

> **Toward where?**

---

# 8. Position in a Sentence

Adverbial prepositional phrases can appear in different positions depending on the sentence structure and the intended emphasis.

## Beginning

> "**In the morning**, we went for a walk."

The phrase establishes the **time** before the main action.

## Middle

> "We, **after the meeting**, went for lunch."

This position is possible, but it is less common and may require commas depending on the structure.

## End

> "We went for a walk **in the morning**."

This is a very common position for adverbial prepositional phrases.

---

# 9. Adverbial vs. Adjectival

The most important distinction is **what the prepositional phrase modifies**.

### Adverbial

> "The students studied **in the library**."

**"In the library"** modifies the verb **"studied"**.

Question:

> **Where did the students study?**

Answer:

> In the library.

Therefore, it is **adverbial**.

### Adjectival

> "The students **in the library** are studying."

**"In the library"** modifies the noun **"students"**.

Question:

> **Which students?**

Answer:

> The students in the library.

Therefore, it is **adjectival**.

---

# 10. Identification Method

To identify a prepositional phrase functioning as an adverbial, follow these steps:

### Step 1 — Find the prepositional phrase

Look for:

> **Preposition + Object**

Example:

> "She worked **in the office**."

### Step 2 — Identify what the phrase modifies

Ask:

> **What does the phrase provide information about?**

If it describes the **action or circumstances**, it is likely functioning as an adverbial.

### Step 3 — Ask the appropriate question

Try asking:

- **Where?**
- **When?**
- **How?**
- **Why?**
- **For what purpose?**
- **In what direction?**

### Step 4 — Confirm the function

If the phrase answers one of these questions about the action, it is functioning as an **adverbial**.

## Important Note

A prepositional phrase functions as an **adverbial** when it provides additional information about an action, event, or circumstance.

It can tell us:

> **Where? → Place**

> **When? → Time**

> **How? → Manner**

> **Why? → Cause**

> **For what purpose? → Purpose**

> **Where to/from? → Direction**

> **Key idea:** A **prepositional phrase as an adverbial** adds information about the circumstances of an action rather than describing or identifying a noun.
