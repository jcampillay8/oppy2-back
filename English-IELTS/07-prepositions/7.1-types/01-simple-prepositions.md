# Simple Prepositions

**Simple prepositions** are single-word prepositions that express relationships of **time, place, direction, manner**, or other relationships.

They are the most basic and commonly used type of prepositions.

## 1. Examples of Simple Prepositions

Some common examples include:

- `in`
- `on`
- `at`
- `over`
- `under`
- `with`
- `by`
- `from`
- `to`
- `through`
- `about`
- `for`
- `during`
- `after`
- `before`
- `since`

---

## 2. Usage

Simple prepositions are followed by a **noun, pronoun, or noun phrase**, which is called the **object of the preposition**.

They establish relationships involving:

- Location
- Time
- Direction
- Possession
- Manner
- Means
- Other relationships

### Basic Structure

> **Preposition + Object**

**Examples:**

- `in` + `the park`
- `on` + `the table`
- `with` + `a smile`

---

## 3. Location and Place

Simple prepositions such as **"in," "on,"** and **"at"** are commonly used to indicate location or place.

### Examples

- "in the park"
- "on the table"
- "at the office"

---

## 4. Time

Simple prepositions can also indicate **time relationships**.

### Examples

- "during the party"
- "after the game"
- "before the meeting"
- "at noon"

---

## 5. Direction

Simple prepositions such as **"to," "from," "into," "out of,"** and **"through"** can indicate movement or direction.

### Examples

- "go to the store"
- "come from work"
- "jump into the pool"
- "walk out of the room"
- "run through the forest"

> **Note:** "out of" is technically a **prepositional phrase**, not a single-word simple preposition, because it contains more than one word.

---

## 6. Other Relationships

Simple prepositions can indicate relationships of **manner, means, source**, and more.

### Examples

- "with a smile" → manner
- "by car" → means
- "from the book" → source
- "to help" → purpose
- "through hard work" → means

---

# Quick Reference

| **Relationship** | **Common Prepositions** | **Example** |
|---|---|---|
| **Place** | `in`, `on`, `at`, `under` | "The book is **on the table**." |
| **Time** | `during`, `after`, `before`, `at`, `since` | "We met **after the game**." |
| **Direction** | `to`, `from`, `into`, `through` | "She walked **through the forest**." |
| **Manner** | `with`, `by` | "He answered **with confidence**." |
| **Means** | `by`, `through` | "We traveled **by car**." |
| **Source** | `from` | "I learned it **from the book**." |
| **Purpose** | `to`, `for` | "She went there **to help**." |

---

## Important Note

Simple prepositions are typically **short words**, but they play a crucial role in expressing relationships and adding clarity to sentences.

Understanding their usage and common examples will help you use prepositions effectively in both **writing and communication**.

> **Key idea:** A preposition connects its object to another element in the sentence and shows the relationship between them.
