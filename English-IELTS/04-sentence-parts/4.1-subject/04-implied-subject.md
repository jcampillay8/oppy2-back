# Implied Subject

An **implied subject** is a subject that is **not explicitly stated** in a sentence but can be understood from the context or the verb form.

It is especially common in **imperative sentences**, where the subject **"you"** is normally understood.

## 1. Definition

An implied subject is the person or thing performing the action, even though it is **not directly mentioned** in the sentence.

**Example:**

> Sit down.

The sentence does not explicitly say **"you"**, but the meaning is:

> **You** sit down.

Therefore:

- Implied subject: **you**
- Verb: **sit**

---

## 2. Implied Subject in Imperative Sentences

Imperative sentences are used to:

- Give commands
- Make requests
- Give instructions
- Offer advice

In these sentences, the subject **you** is normally implied.

**Examples:**

> Open the door.

- Implied subject: **you**
- Verb: **open**

> Please wait here.

- Implied subject: **you**
- Verb: **wait**

> Take a seat.

- Implied subject: **you**
- Verb: **take**

> Be careful.

- Implied subject: **you**
- Verb: **be**

---

## 3. Verb Form

In imperative sentences, the verb normally appears in its **base form**.

There is usually no subject pronoun before the verb.

### Normal statement:

> You open the door.

### Imperative:

> Open the door.

The subject **you** is understood but omitted.

---

## 4. Negative Imperatives

Negative commands also have an implied subject **you**.

The typical structure is:

**Don't + base verb**

**Examples:**

> Don't touch that.

- Implied subject: **you**
- Verb: **touch**

> Don't open the door.

- Implied subject: **you**
- Verb: **open**

> Don't worry.

- Implied subject: **you**
- Verb: **worry**

The complete meaning can be understood as:

> **You don't touch that.**

However, the natural imperative form is:

> **Don't touch that.**

---

## 5. Implied Subject with "Please"

Adding **please** makes a request more polite, but the subject remains implied.

**Examples:**

> Please sit down.

- Implied subject: **you**

> Please open the window.

- Implied subject: **you**

> Please wait outside.

- Implied subject: **you**

---

## 6. Implied Subject in Instructions

Implied subjects are very common when giving instructions or explaining procedures.

**Examples:**

> Click the button.

- Implied subject: **you**

> Enter your password.

- Implied subject: **you**

> Select the file.

- Implied subject: **you**

> Press Enter.

- Implied subject: **you**

These sentences are commonly used in:

- Tutorials
- Manuals
- Recipes
- Software instructions
- Workplace procedures

---

## 7. Implied Subject vs. Explicit Subject

### Explicit Subject

The subject is directly written in the sentence.

> You open the door.

- Subject: **you**

### Implied Subject

The subject is understood but not written.

> Open the door.

- Implied subject: **you**

## 9. Important Rule

The easiest way to remember implied subjects is:

> **Imperative sentence → implied subject = YOU**

**Example:**

> Close the window.

Meaning:

> **You** close the window.

**Example:**

> Don't touch the machine.

Meaning:

> **You** don't touch the machine.

---

## Key Difference

**Explicit Subject:**

> You open the door.

The subject **you** is written.

**Implied Subject:**

> Open the door.

The subject **you** is understood but not written.

### Remember:

**Implied Subject = The subject is understood, but it is not explicitly stated.**

In imperative sentences:

**Implied Subject = YOU**
