# Simple Subject

The **simple subject** is the main noun or pronoun that performs the action or is the topic of the sentence. It is the essential part of the subject, excluding modifiers or additional descriptive words.

## 1. Definition

The simple subject represents the **core noun or pronoun** that acts as the subject of the sentence.

It tells us **who or what** the sentence is about or who or what performs the action.

**Example:**

> The cat is sleeping.

- Complete subject: **The cat**
- Simple subject: **cat**

The word **"The"** is a determiner, so it is not part of the simple subject.

---

## 2. How to Identify the Simple Subject

To identify the simple subject, ask:

> **Who or what is performing the action?**

or:

> **Who or what is the sentence about?**

**Example:**

> The young boy plays soccer.

Ask:

> Who plays soccer?

Answer:

> **The young boy**

The main noun is:

> **boy**

Therefore, **boy** is the simple subject.

---

## 3. Simple Subject vs. Complete Subject

The **complete subject** includes the simple subject plus its modifiers.

The **simple subject** contains only the main noun or pronoun.

**Example:**

> The tall man with a blue shirt is running.

- Complete subject: **The tall man with a blue shirt**
- Simple subject: **man**

Another example:

> The three students from my class are studying.

- Complete subject: **The three students from my class**
- Simple subject: **students**

---

## 4. Subject-Verb Agreement

The simple subject is important because it determines **subject-verb agreement**.

### Singular:

> He runs every morning.

- Simple subject: **He**
- Verb: **runs**

### Plural:

> They run every morning.

- Simple subject: **They**
- Verb: **run**

The verb must agree with the **simple subject**, not necessarily with the words that appear closest to the verb.

**Example:**

> The box of books is heavy.

- Simple subject: **box**
- "books" is part of a prepositional phrase.
- Therefore, the verb is **is**, not **are**.

---

## 5. Simple Subjects Can Be Nouns

A noun can function as the simple subject.

**Examples:**

> The dog barks loudly.

- Simple subject: **dog**

> Cars drive on the road.

- Simple subject: **Cars**

> The sun shines brightly.

- Simple subject: **sun**

> Mary sings beautifully.

- Simple subject: **Mary**

---

## 6. Simple Subjects Can Be Pronouns

Pronouns can also function as simple subjects.

**Examples:**

> She dances gracefully.

- Simple subject: **She**

> They read books.

- Simple subject: **They**

> He works at a bank.

- Simple subject: **He**

> We study English.

- Simple subject: **We**

---

## 7. Simple Subject with Modifiers

Modifiers provide additional information about the subject, but they are **not part of the simple subject**.

**Example:**

> The beautiful old house is for sale.

- Complete subject: **The beautiful old house**
- Simple subject: **house**
- Modifiers: **The, beautiful, old**

Another example:

> The students in the classroom are studying.

- Complete subject: **The students in the classroom**
- Simple subject: **students**
- Additional phrase: **in the classroom**

---

## 8. Compound Subjects

A sentence can have more than one simple subject. This is called a **compound subject**.

**Example:**

> Mary and Tom went to the party.

- Simple subject 1: **Mary**
- Simple subject 2: **Tom**
- Compound subject: **Mary and Tom**

Another example:

> The dog and the cat are sleeping.

- Simple subject 1: **dog**
- Simple subject 2: **cat**
- Compound subject: **The dog and the cat**

---

## 9. Simple Subject in Questions

The subject can appear after the auxiliary verb in questions.

**Example:**

> Does John work here?

- Simple subject: **John**
- Auxiliary verb: **Does**
- Main verb: **work**

Another example:

> Are the students ready?

- Simple subject: **students**
- Auxiliary verb: **Are**
- Subject: **the students**

---

## 10. Simple Subject in Imperative Sentences

In imperative sentences, the subject is usually **implied**.

**Example:**

> Sit down.

The implied subject is:

> **You**

The complete meaning is:

> **You sit down.**

Another example:

> Open the door.

- Implied subject: **you**
- Verb: **open**

---

## Key Difference

The easiest way to remember the concept is:

> **Complete Subject = Simple Subject + Modifiers**

**Example:**

> The very tall man with a blue shirt is running.

- Complete subject: **The very tall man with a blue shirt**
- Simple subject: **man**

### Remember:

**The simple subject is the core noun or pronoun of the subject.**
