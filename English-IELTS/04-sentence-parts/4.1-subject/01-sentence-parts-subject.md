# Subject

The **subject** is a fundamental component of a sentence. It identifies **who or what performs the action** or **who or what the sentence is about**.

## 1. Definition

The subject is usually a **noun or pronoun** that performs an action or represents the main topic of the sentence.

### Examples

- **John** plays basketball.
- **She** works at a company.
- **The dog** is sleeping.
- **They** studied English.

In these examples:

- **John** → performs the action.
- **She** → performs the action.
- **The dog** → is the topic of the sentence.
- **They** → perform the action.

---

## 2. Position of the Subject

In most English sentences, the subject comes **before the verb**.

### Basic Structure

**Subject + Verb + Object**

### Examples

- **John** plays basketball.
- **She** reads books.
- **They** study English.
- **The company** hired a new employee.

However, the subject can appear after the verb in some structures, especially **questions**.

### Examples

- Where **is John**?
- Are **you** ready?
- Did **she** call you?

---

## 3. Simple Subject

A **simple subject** is the main noun or pronoun that performs the action or represents the topic.

### Examples

- **John** plays basketball.
- **She** studies English.
- **The dog** is sleeping.
- **Students** are studying.

The simple subject is:

- John
- She
- The dog
- Students

---

## 4. Compound Subject

A **compound subject** contains two or more nouns or pronouns joined together.

Common conjunctions include:

**and / or / nor**

### Examples

- **Mary and Tom** went to the party.
- **John and Sarah** work together.
- **The manager and the employees** attended the meeting.
- **You or I** need to make the decision.

### Structure

**Subject + and/or + Subject + Verb**

> **Mary and Tom** went to the party.

The subject consists of **Mary + Tom**.

---

## 5. Implied Subject

In imperative sentences, the subject is often **not explicitly stated**.

The implied subject is usually **you**.

### Examples

- Sit down.
- Open the door.
- Listen carefully.
- Be careful.
- Study harder.

The implied subject is:

> **(You) sit down.**

> **(You) open the door.**

> **(You) listen carefully.**

---

## 6. Gerund as a Subject

A **gerund** is a verb ending in **-ing** that functions as a noun.

A gerund can be used as the subject of a sentence.

### Examples

- **Swimming** is my favorite hobby.
- **Reading** improves your vocabulary.
- **Learning English** takes time.
- **Working from home** can be convenient.

In:

> **Swimming** is my favorite hobby.

**Swimming** is the subject.

---

## 7. Subject-Verb Agreement

The subject and verb must generally **agree in number**.

### Singular Subject

A singular subject normally takes a singular verb.

- **She works** every day.
- **He plays** football.
- **The dog runs** quickly.

### Plural Subject

A plural subject normally takes a plural verb.

- **They work** every day.
- **We play** football.
- **The dogs run** quickly.

### Compare

| Subject | Verb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| She | works | She works every day. |
| They | work | They work every day. |
| He | plays | He plays football. |
| They | play | They play football. |

---

## 8. Subject with the Verb "To Be"

The verb **"to be"** changes according to the subject.

### Present

| Subject | "To Be" | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I | am | I am tired. |
| He / She / It | is | She is happy. |
| You / We / They | are | They are ready. |

### Past

| Subject | "To Be" | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I / He / She / It | was | He was tired. |
| You / We / They | were | They were ready. |

---

## 9. How to Identify the Subject

To identify the subject, ask:

**"Who or what is performing the action?"**

or:

**"Who or what is the sentence about?"**

### Example 1

> The dog chased the cat.

Ask:

**Who chased the cat?**

Answer:

> **The dog** → Subject

### Example 2

> Sarah is studying English.

Ask:

**Who is studying English?**

Answer:

> **Sarah** → Subject

### Example 3

> The company released a new product.

Ask:

**Who released a new product?**

Answer:

> **The company** → Subject

---

## 10. Subject vs. Object

The **subject** performs the action, while the **object** usually receives the action.

### Example

> **John** wrote **a letter**.

- **John** → Subject
- **wrote** → Verb
- **a letter** → Object

### Structure

**Subject + Verb + Object**

> **John** + **wrote** + **a letter**.

---

## 11. Types of Subjects

| Type | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Simple Subject** | Main noun or pronoun | **John** works here. |
| **Compound Subject** | Two or more subjects | **John and Sarah** work here. |
| **Implied Subject** | Usually "you" in an imperative | **(You)** Sit down. |
| **Gerund Subject** | Gerund functioning as a noun | **Swimming** is fun. |

---

## Key Characteristics

The subject:

- Identifies **who or what** the sentence is about.
- Usually performs the action.
- Usually appears **before the verb**.
- Can be a noun, pronoun, gerund, or group of words.
- Can be **singular or plural**.
- Must generally agree with the verb.
- Can be **explicit** or **implied**.

## Key Formula

### Basic Sentence

**Subject + Verb + Object**

> She reads books.

### With an Adjective

**Subject + Verb + Adjective**

> She is happy.

### With a Compound Subject

**Subject + and + Subject + Verb**

> John and Sarah work together.

### Imperative

**(You) + Base Verb**

> (You) Open the door.

## Key Idea

**Subject = Who or what the sentence is about, or who/what performs the action.**
