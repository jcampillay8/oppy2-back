# Compound Subject

A **compound subject** consists of two or more nouns or pronouns that share the same verb. The subjects are usually connected by a coordinating conjunction such as **and, or,** or **nor**.

## 1. Definition

A compound subject represents **two or more people, things, or entities** that perform the same action or are described by the same verb.

**Example:**

> Mary and John went to the party.

- Compound subject: **Mary and John**
- Verb: **went**

Both **Mary** and **John** perform the action.

---

## 2. Structure

A common structure is:

**Subject + conjunction + subject + verb**

**Examples:**

> Jane and Mark are going to the movies.

> Dogs and cats are popular pets.

> Tom and Sarah work together.

The conjunction connects the individual subjects.

---

## 3. Coordinating Conjunctions

Compound subjects are commonly connected using:

- **and**
- **or**
- **nor**

### AND

**And** normally connects subjects and creates a plural subject.

> John and Mary are students.

- Subject 1: **John**
- Subject 2: **Mary**
- Compound subject: **John and Mary**
- Verb: **are**

### OR

**Or** presents alternatives.

> John or Mary is going to the meeting.

The verb usually agrees with the subject closest to it.

### NOR

**Nor** can also connect alternative subjects.

> Neither John nor Mary is available.

---

## 4. Subject-Verb Agreement

Subject-verb agreement is especially important with compound subjects.

### Two Singular Subjects + AND → Plural Verb

> The dog and the cat are sleeping.

- Dog = singular
- Cat = singular
- Compound subject = plural
- Verb = **are**

Another example:

> Sarah and Tom work together.

---

### Two Plural Subjects + AND → Plural Verb

> The students and teachers are meeting.

- Students = plural
- Teachers = plural
- Verb = **are**

---

### Singular Subjects with OR/NOR

When subjects are connected by **or** or **nor**, the verb generally agrees with the subject closest to it.

> The teacher or the student is responsible.

The closest subject is **student** → **is**

> The teacher or the students are responsible.

The closest subject is **students** → **are**

---

## 5. Compound Subject vs. Simple Subject

A **simple subject** contains one main noun or pronoun.

> John works here.

- Simple subject: **John**

A **compound subject** contains two or more subjects.

> John and Mary work here.

- Compound subject: **John and Mary**
- Simple subjects: **John**, **Mary**

---

## 6. Examples

| Sentence | Compound Subject | Verb |
|---|---|---|
| Jane and Mark are going to the movies. | **Jane and Mark** | **are** |
| Dogs and cats are popular pets. | **Dogs and cats** | **are** |
| Tom and Sarah work together. | **Tom and Sarah** | **work** |
| My brother and sister live in Chile. | **My brother and sister** | **live** |
| The manager and the engineer are meeting. | **The manager and the engineer** | **are** |
| She or her brother will drive. | **She or her brother** | **will drive** |
| Neither John nor Mary is available. | **Neither John nor Mary** | **is** |

---

## 7. Important Rule

The easiest rule to remember is:

### AND

**A + B → usually plural verb**

> John and Mary **are** here.

### OR / NOR

**The verb generally agrees with the subject closest to it.**

> John or Mary **is** coming.

> John or the students **are** coming.

---

## Key Difference

**Simple Subject:**

> The student is studying.

One subject → **student**

**Compound Subject:**

> The student and the teacher are studying.

Two subjects → **student and teacher**

### Remember:

**Compound Subject = Two or more subjects + the same verb**
