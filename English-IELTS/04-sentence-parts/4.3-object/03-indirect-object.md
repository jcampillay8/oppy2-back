# INDIRECT OBJECT

The **indirect object** is a noun, pronoun, or noun phrase that indicates the person or thing **to whom** or **for whom** an action is performed.

It usually identifies the **recipient or beneficiary** of the direct object.

## 1. Definition

The indirect object represents the person or thing that receives or benefits from the direct object.

**Example:**

- He gave **me** a gift.
  - Subject: **He**
  - Verb: **gave**
  - Indirect Object: **me**
  - Direct Object: **a gift**

**"Me"** is the recipient of the gift.

## 2. Position

The indirect object typically comes **before the direct object**.

**Example:**

- She gave **John** **a book**.
  - Indirect Object: **John**
  - Direct Object: **a book**

### Structure

**Subject + Verb + Indirect Object + Direct Object**

Example:

**She + gave + John + a book.**

## 3. Common Verbs with Indirect Objects

Indirect objects commonly appear with verbs involving:

- Giving
- Sending
- Showing
- Telling
- Buying
- Offering
- Teaching
- Lending
- Bringing

**Examples:**

- They sent **their friends** postcards.
- She told **me** a story.
- He bought **his mother** a gift.
- We offered **the customer** a solution.
- She taught **the students** English.

## 4. How to Identify the Indirect Object

To identify the indirect object, ask:

- **To whom?**
- **For whom?**

after the verb.

**Example:**

- She bought **her mother** a gift.

Ask:

**For whom did she buy the gift?**

→ **Her mother**

Therefore:

- Indirect Object: **her mother**
- Direct Object: **a gift**

## 5. Indirect Object Pronouns

Indirect objects can be pronouns.

Common object pronouns include:

| Subject Pronoun | Object Pronoun |
|---|---|
| I | me |
| you | you |
| he | him |
| she | her |
| it | it |
| we | us |
| they | them |

**Examples:**

- He gave **me** a book.
- She told **him** the truth.
- They sent **us** an email.
- I bought **her** a present.
- We offered **them** a job.

## 6. Indirect Object vs. Direct Object

The key difference is:

- **Direct Object** → receives the action directly.
- **Indirect Object** → indicates who receives or benefits from the direct object.

**Example:**

- She gave **John** **a book**.

**John** = Indirect Object  
**a book** = Direct Object

The book is the thing being given.

John is the person receiving the book.

## 7. Alternative Structure with "To" or "For"

Sentences with indirect objects can often be rewritten using a preposition such as **to** or **for**.

### Without a Preposition

- She gave **John** a book.

**John** = Indirect Object  
**a book** = Direct Object

### With "To"

- She gave **a book** to **John**.

**a book** = Direct Object  
**John** = Object of the Preposition "to"

Similarly:

- He sent **me** an email.
- He sent an email **to me**.

## 8. Quick Identification

| Sentence | Indirect Object | Direct Object |
|---|---|---|
| He gave me a gift. | me | a gift |
| She sent John an email. | John | an email |
| They bought their mother flowers. | their mother | flowers |
| I told him the story. | him | the story |
| We offered them a solution. | them | a solution |

## Key Point

The **indirect object** tells us **who receives or benefits from the direct object**.

### Formula

**Subject + Verb + Indirect Object + Direct Object**

**She + gave + John + a book.**

Ask:

**To whom did she give a book? → John.**

Therefore:

**"John" = Indirect Object**

**"a book" = Direct Object**
