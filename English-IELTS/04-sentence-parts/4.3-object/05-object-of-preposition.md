# OBJECT OF A PREPOSITION

The **object of a preposition** is a noun, pronoun, or noun phrase that comes after a preposition and completes its meaning.

It is an essential part of a **prepositional phrase**.

## 1. Definition

The object of a preposition is the person, thing, place, or idea that follows a preposition.

**Example:**

- She walked to **the park**.

Breakdown:

- Preposition: **to**
- Object of the Preposition: **the park**

The phrase **"to the park"** is a prepositional phrase.

## 2. Common Prepositions

Common prepositions include:

- **in**
- **on**
- **at**
- **to**
- **from**
- **with**
- **for**
- **about**
- **between**
- **under**
- **over**
- **through**
- **during**
- **after**
- **before**
- **without**
- **near**
- **by**

### Examples

- in **the house**
- on **the table**
- at **the office**
- with **my friends**
- about **the project**
- between **the buildings**
- during **the meeting**

The noun or noun phrase after the preposition is the **object of the preposition**.

## 3. Position

The object of a preposition normally comes **after the preposition**.

**Example:**

- He sat on **the chair**.

Breakdown:

- Preposition: **on**
- Object of the Preposition: **the chair**

Another example:

- They live in **Santiago**.

- Preposition: **in**
- Object of the Preposition: **Santiago**

## 4. Prepositional Phrase

A **prepositional phrase** consists of:

**Preposition + Object of the Preposition**

### Examples

- **in the house**
- **on the table**
- **with my brother**
- **during the meeting**
- **after the class**

**Example sentence:**

- She studied **during the night**.

Breakdown:

- Preposition: **during**
- Object of the Preposition: **the night**
- Prepositional Phrase: **during the night**

## 5. Types of Relationships

Prepositions can express different types of relationships.

### Location

- The book is on **the table**.
- She is in **the office**.
- The car is near **the building**.

### Direction

- He walked to **the park**.
- They traveled through **the city**.
- She came from **the airport**.

### Time

- We met during **the summer**.
- He arrived after **the meeting**.
- She worked until **midnight**.

### Manner or Association

- He went with **his friends**.
- She solved the problem with **a computer**.
- They talked about **the project**.

## 6. Modifiers

The object of a preposition can contain adjectives and other modifiers.

**Example:**

- They went on **a long journey**.

Breakdown:

- Preposition: **on**
- Object of the Preposition: **a long journey**
- Main noun: **journey**
- Modifier: **long**

Another example:

- She works in **a large office**.

The complete object is:

**a large office**

## 7. Object of a Preposition vs. Direct Object

These two concepts are different.

### Direct Object

The direct object receives the action of the verb.

- She opened **the door**.

**the door** = Direct Object

### Object of a Preposition

The object follows a preposition.

- She walked through **the door**.

**the door** = Object of the Preposition

In the second sentence, **"through"** is the preposition.

## 8. Object of a Preposition vs. Indirect Object

Compare:

### Indirect Object

- She gave **John** a book.

**John** = Indirect Object  
**a book** = Direct Object

### Object of a Preposition

- She gave a book to **John**.

**a book** = Direct Object  
**John** = Object of the Preposition **to**

The meaning is similar, but the grammatical function is different.

## 9. How to Identify the Object of a Preposition

To identify the object:

### Step 1: Find the preposition.

### Step 2: Look immediately after it.

### Step 3: Identify the noun, pronoun, or noun phrase that completes the preposition's meaning.

**Example:**

- They talked about **the new project**.

Step 1:

- Preposition → **about**

Step 2:

- What follows "about"? → **the new project**

Step 3:

- Object of the Preposition → **the new project**

## Key Point

The **object of a preposition** is the noun, pronoun, or noun phrase that follows a preposition.

### Formula

**Preposition + Object of the Preposition**

**in + the office**

**with + my friends**

**about + the project**

**during + the meeting**

Together, the preposition and its object form a **prepositional phrase**.
