# DIRECT OBJECT

The **direct object** is a noun, pronoun, or noun phrase that directly receives the action of the verb in a sentence. It completes the meaning of the verb and answers the questions **"What?"** or **"Whom?"** after the verb.

## 1. Definition

The direct object is the person, thing, or entity that directly receives the action of the verb.

**Example:**

- She ate **an apple**.
  - Subject: **She**
  - Verb: **ate**
  - Direct Object: **an apple**

The apple receives the action of the verb "ate."

## 2. Position

The direct object typically comes **after the verb**.

**Example:**

- He kicked **the ball**.
  - Verb: **kicked**
  - Direct Object: **the ball**

Other examples:

- She reads **a book**.
- They built **a house**.
- I watched **a movie**.

## 3. Transitive Verbs

Direct objects are normally associated with **transitive verbs**.

A transitive verb requires a direct object to complete or clarify its meaning.

**Examples:**

- She reads **a book**.
- He opened **the door**.
- They bought **a car**.
- We watched **the movie**.

### Structure

**Subject + Transitive Verb + Direct Object**

Example:

**She + reads + a book.**

## 4. How to Identify the Direct Object

To identify the direct object, ask:

- **What?**
- **Whom?**

after the verb.

**Example:**

- They watched **a movie**.

Ask:

- What did they watch?
- → **A movie**

Therefore:

- Direct Object: **a movie**

Another example:

- She called **her friend**.

Ask:

- Whom did she call?
- → **Her friend**

Therefore:

- Direct Object: **her friend**

## 5. Object Modifiers

A direct object can contain adjectives or other words that provide additional information.

**Example:**

- She bought **a beautiful dress**.

The complete direct object is:

**a beautiful dress**

The main noun is:

**dress**

The adjective **beautiful** describes the direct object.

More examples:

- He bought **a new computer**.
- They watched **an interesting movie**.
- She opened **the large box**.

## 6. Direct Object vs. Indirect Object

A sentence can contain both an indirect object and a direct object.

**Example:**

- He gave **Mary** **a gift**.

Here:

- Subject: **He**
- Verb: **gave**
- Indirect Object: **Mary**
- Direct Object: **a gift**

The **gift** is the thing being given.

**Mary** is the person receiving the gift.

### Structure

**Subject + Verb + Indirect Object + Direct Object**

Example:

**He + gave + Mary + a gift.**

## 7. Important Correction

A sentence can contain an indirect object and a direct object, but the indirect object is **not** itself a direct object.

For example:

- He gave **Mary** **a gift**.

**Mary** = Indirect Object  
**a gift** = Direct Object

Another possible structure is:

- He gave **a gift** **to Mary**.

Here:

**a gift** = Direct Object  
**Mary** = Object of the Preposition "to"

## 8. Quick Identification

| Sentence | Verb | Direct Object |
|---|---|---|
| She ate an apple. | ate | an apple |
| He kicked the ball. | kicked | the ball |
| They watched a movie. | watched | a movie |
| I bought a computer. | bought | a computer |
| She opened the door. | opened | the door |

## Key Point

The **direct object** is the person, thing, or entity that directly receives the action of a transitive verb.

### Formula

**Subject + Verb + Direct Object**

**She + reads + a book.**

Ask:

**What does she read? → A book.**

Therefore:

**"a book" = Direct Object**
