# OBJECT

The **object** is a component of a sentence that receives the action of the verb or is affected by it. It provides additional information and often completes the meaning of the verb.

## 1. Direct Object

A **direct object** is a noun, pronoun, or noun phrase that directly receives the action of the verb.

**Example:**

- She ate **an apple**.
  - Subject: **She**
  - Verb: **ate**
  - Direct Object: **an apple**

The direct object answers the question **"What?"** or **"Whom?"** after the verb.

## 2. Indirect Object

An **indirect object** is a noun, pronoun, or noun phrase that indicates **to whom**, **for whom**, or **for whose benefit** an action is performed.

**Example:**

- He gave **me** a gift.
  - Subject: **He**
  - Verb: **gave**
  - Indirect Object: **me**
  - Direct Object: **a gift**

The indirect object usually comes before the direct object.

**Example:**

- She sent **her friend** an email.
  - Indirect Object: **her friend**
  - Direct Object: **an email**

## 3. Object of a Preposition

The **object of a preposition** is a noun, pronoun, or noun phrase that follows a preposition and completes its meaning.

**Example:**

- She went to **the store**.
  - Preposition: **to**
  - Object of the Preposition: **the store**

More examples:

- We talked about **the project**.
- He works with **his brother**.
- They live in **Santiago**.

## 4. Verb Types and Objects

### Transitive Verbs

**Transitive verbs** require a direct object to complete their meaning.

**Example:**

- He kicked **the ball**.
- She opened **the door**.
- They built **a house**.

Without the object, the meaning may be incomplete:

- He kicked...
- She opened...
- They built...

### Intransitive Verbs

**Intransitive verbs** do not require a direct object.

**Example:**

- She laughed.
- He arrived.
- They slept.
- The baby cried.

These sentences are grammatically complete without a direct object.

## 5. Position of Objects

The position of an object depends on its type.

### Direct Object

Usually comes after the verb:

- She reads **books**.
- He bought **a car**.

### Indirect + Direct Object

The indirect object usually comes before the direct object:

- She gave **me** **a gift**.
- He sent **his manager** **an email**.

**Structure:**

**Subject + Verb + Indirect Object + Direct Object**

### Object of a Preposition

Comes after the preposition:

- She went to **the store**.
- He works with **his team**.
- They talked about **the project**.

## 6. Quick Comparison

| Type | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct Object** | Receives the action directly | She ate **an apple**. |
| **Indirect Object** | Receives or benefits from the action | He gave **me** a gift. |
| **Object of Preposition** | Follows and completes a preposition | She went to **the store**. |

## 7. How to Identify the Object

To identify the **direct object**, ask:

**What? / Whom?**

Example:

- She reads **a book**.
- She reads **what?** → **a book**

To identify the **indirect object**, ask:

**To whom? / For whom?**

Example:

- He gave **me** a gift.
- He gave a gift **to whom?** → **me**

To identify the **object of a preposition**, look for the noun or pronoun immediately following the preposition.

Example:

- They talked about **the problem**.
- Preposition: **about**
- Object of the preposition: **the problem**

## Key Point

The main distinction is:

- **Direct Object** → receives the action.
- **Indirect Object** → receives or benefits from the direct object/action.
- **Object of Preposition** → follows a preposition and completes its meaning.

Understanding objects is essential for analyzing sentence structure and constructing clear, grammatically correct sentences.
