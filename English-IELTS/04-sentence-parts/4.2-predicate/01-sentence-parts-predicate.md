# Predicate

The **predicate** is the part of a sentence that provides information about the **subject**. It contains the verb and can include objects, complements, adverbs, phrases, and other elements that complete the meaning of the sentence.

## 1. Definition

The predicate tells us **what the subject does, what happens to the subject, or what the subject is**.

**Example:**

> She runs every morning.

- Subject: **She**
- Predicate: **runs every morning**

The predicate contains the verb **runs** and provides information about the subject.

---

## 2. Basic Sentence Structure

A basic English sentence can be divided into:

**Subject + Predicate**

**Example:**

> The dog / barks loudly.

- Subject: **The dog**
- Predicate: **barks loudly**

Another example:

> My brother / works at a bank.

- Subject: **My brother**
- Predicate: **works at a bank**

---

## 3. The Verb in the Predicate

The **verb** is the central element of the predicate.

**Examples:**

> She **runs** every morning.

> He **works** in an office.

> They **studied** English yesterday.

> We **will travel** tomorrow.

The verb tells us what the subject **does, is, or experiences**.

---

## 4. Predicate with a Direct Object

The predicate can contain a **direct object**.

A direct object receives the action of the verb.

**Example:**

> She reads a book.

- Subject: **She**
- Verb: **reads**
- Direct object: **a book**
- Predicate: **reads a book**

Another example:

> He bought a new computer.

- Subject: **He**
- Predicate: **bought a new computer**
- Direct object: **a new computer**

---

## 5. Predicate with an Indirect Object

The predicate can also contain an **indirect object**.

The indirect object usually identifies the person or thing that receives the direct object or benefits from the action.

**Example:**

> She gave him a gift.

- Subject: **She**
- Verb: **gave**
- Indirect object: **him**
- Direct object: **a gift**
- Predicate: **gave him a gift**

Another example:

> I sent my friend an email.

- Subject: **I**
- Indirect object: **my friend**
- Direct object: **an email**
- Predicate: **sent my friend an email**

---

## 6. Predicate Adjective

A **predicate adjective** describes the subject and normally follows a **linking verb**.

**Example:**

> He is happy.

- Subject: **He**
- Linking verb: **is**
- Predicate adjective: **happy**
- Predicate: **is happy**

Another example:

> The food smells delicious.

- Subject: **The food**
- Linking verb: **smells**
- Predicate adjective: **delicious**
- Predicate: **smells delicious**

---

## 7. Predicate Noun

A **predicate noun** identifies or renames the subject after a linking verb.

**Example:**

> She is a doctor.

- Subject: **She**
- Linking verb: **is**
- Predicate noun: **a doctor**
- Predicate: **is a doctor**

Another example:

> John became the manager.

- Subject: **John**
- Linking verb: **became**
- Predicate noun: **the manager**
- Predicate: **became the manager**

---

## 8. Adverbs and Adverbial Phrases

The predicate can contain adverbs or adverbial phrases that provide additional information about the action.

They can tell us:

- How
- When
- Where
- Why
- How often

**Examples:**

> She ran **quickly**.

> He works **every day**.

> They studied **at the library**.

> We arrived **yesterday**.

The additional information is part of the predicate.

---

## 9. Predicate with Auxiliary Verbs

The predicate can contain several verbs working together in a **verb phrase**.

**Example:**

> She has been studying English.

- Subject: **She**
- Predicate: **has been studying English**
- Verb phrase: **has been studying**
- Direct object: **English**

Another example:

> They will have finished the project by Friday.

- Subject: **They**
- Predicate: **will have finished the project by Friday**
- Verb phrase: **will have finished**
- Direct object: **the project**

---

## 10. Action Verbs in the Predicate

An **action verb** expresses a physical or mental action.

**Examples:**

> He paints a picture.

- Predicate: **paints a picture**

> She studies English.

- Predicate: **studies English**

> They built a house.

- Predicate: **built a house**

---

## 11. Linking Verbs in the Predicate

A **linking verb** connects the subject to information that describes or identifies it.

Common linking verbs include:

- **be**
- **seem**
- **become**
- **appear**
- **feel**
- **look**
- **sound**
- **smell**
- **taste**

**Examples:**

> She is happy.

> He seems tired.

> They became successful.

> The food smells delicious.

In these sentences, the predicate provides information about the subject rather than expressing a direct action.

---

## 12. Simple Predicate vs. Complete Predicate

### Simple Predicate

The **simple predicate** is the main verb or verb phrase.

**Example:**

> The young boy is playing soccer in the park.

- Simple subject: **boy**
- Simple predicate: **is playing**

### Complete Predicate

The **complete predicate** includes the verb and all the words that provide information about the subject.

- Complete predicate: **is playing soccer in the park**

Therefore:

**Complete Predicate = Verb/Verb Phrase + Other Information**

---

## 14. How to Identify the Predicate

A simple method is:

1. Identify the **subject**.
2. Everything that tells us something about that subject is generally the **predicate**.

**Example:**

> The students are studying English at the library.

Ask:

> Who or what is the sentence about?

Answer:

> **The students**

Therefore:

- Subject: **The students**
- Predicate: **are studying English at the library**

---

## Key Difference

### Subject

Tells us **who or what** the sentence is about.

> **The engineer** works at the company.

### Predicate

Tells us **what the subject does, is, or experiences**.

> The engineer **works at the company**.

### Remember:

**Sentence = Subject + Predicate**

**Subject → Who or what?**

**Predicate → What does the subject do? / What is the subject like? / What happens to the subject?**
