# Comparative Adjectives

Comparative adjectives are used to compare the differences in quality or quantity between **two things, people, or groups**. They indicate that one has a higher, lower, or different degree of a particular characteristic compared with another.

## 1. Definition

A comparative adjective expresses a **higher or lower degree of a quality** when comparing two things.

**Example:**

- This car is **faster** than that car.
- Maria is **more intelligent** than John.

---

## 2. Formation of Comparative Adjectives

### Short adjectives

For most one-syllable adjectives, add **-er**.

| Base Adjective | Comparative |
|---|---|
| tall | taller |
| fast | faster |
| small | smaller |
| old | older |
| young | younger |

**Example:**

> My car is **faster** than yours.

### Adjectives ending in -e

Add **-r**.

| Base Adjective | Comparative |
|---|---|
| large | larger |
| nice | nicer |
| safe | safer |

**Example:**

> This house is **larger** than that one.

### Adjectives ending in consonant + y

Change **y → i** and add **-er**.

| Base Adjective | Comparative |
|---|---|
| happy | happier |
| easy | easier |
| busy | busier |
| friendly | friendlier |

**Example:**

> This exercise is **easier** than the previous one.

### Short adjectives with a consonant-vowel-consonant pattern

The final consonant is usually doubled before adding **-er**.

| Base Adjective | Comparative |
|---|---|
| big | bigger |
| hot | hotter |
| thin | thinner |

**Example:**

> This box is **bigger** than that one.

---

## 3. Using "More"

For many adjectives with **two or more syllables**, use:

**more + adjective**

| Base Adjective | Comparative |
|---|---|
| beautiful | more beautiful |
| expensive | more expensive |
| difficult | more difficult |
| interesting | more interesting |
| important | more important |

**Example:**

> This book is **more interesting** than that one.

---

## 4. Using "Less"

To express a lower degree of a quality, use:

**less + adjective**

Examples:

- This car is **less expensive** than that one.
- The second exercise is **less difficult** than the first.
- This option is **less complicated** than the other one.

---

## 5. Using "Than"

Comparative adjectives are commonly followed by **than** when identifying the second element of the comparison.

### Structure

**Subject + verb + comparative adjective + than + comparison**

**Examples:**

- John is **taller than** Mark.
- This computer is **faster than** mine.
- English is **more difficult than** Spanish.
- This exercise is **easier than** the previous one.

---

## 6. Irregular Comparative Adjectives

Some adjectives have irregular comparative forms.

| Base | Comparative | Example |
|---|---|---|
| good | better | This book is **better** than that one. |
| bad | worse | Today is **worse** than yesterday. |
| far | farther / further | The airport is **farther** than the station. |
| little | less | I have **less** time than you. |
| many | more | She has **more** books than me. |
| much | more | He has **more** experience than I do. |

### Important

Some words such as **well** and **badly** are adverbs rather than adjectives, but they also have irregular comparative forms:

- well → better
- badly → worse

**Examples:**

> She sings **better than** her sister.

> He performed **worse than** expected.

---

## 7. Two-Item Comparison

Comparative adjectives are normally used when comparing **two** things or people.

Examples:

- My house is **bigger than** yours.
- Sarah is **taller than** Anna.
- This computer is **more powerful than** the old one.
- Today is **colder than** yesterday.

---

## 8. Comparative Without "Than"

Sometimes the second element of the comparison is understood and **than** is omitted.

Examples:

- This car is **faster**.
- I need a **bigger** one.
- She wants a **more comfortable** chair.

The comparison is understood from the context.

---

## 9. Comparative + "and"

We can use a comparative adjective with **and** to describe a continuous change.

### Structure

**comparative + and + comparative**

Examples:

- The weather is getting **colder and colder**.
- The company is becoming **bigger and bigger**.
- English is becoming **more and more important**.
- The problem is getting **more and more difficult**.

---

## 10. Comparative Adjectives Do Not Change

Comparative adjectives do not change according to the **gender or number** of the noun.

Examples:

- a **tall** building
- two **tall** buildings
- a **taller** building
- two **taller** buildings

The comparative form remains **taller**.

---

## 11. Comparative vs. Superlative

Comparative adjectives generally compare **two** things, while superlative adjectives identify the highest or lowest degree among **three or more**.

| Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|
| taller | tallest |
| faster | fastest |
| bigger | biggest |
| more expensive | most expensive |
| better | best |
| worse | worst |

**Comparative:**

> John is **taller than** Mark.

**Superlative:**

> John is **the tallest** student in the class.

---

## 12. Common Structures

### Higher degree

**Subject + verb + comparative + than + noun/pronoun**

> This car is **faster than** mine.

### Lower degree

**Subject + verb + less + adjective + than + noun/pronoun**

> This car is **less expensive than** mine.

### More + adjective

**Subject + verb + more + adjective + than + noun/pronoun**

> This solution is **more effective than** the previous one.

---

## 13. Common Mistakes

### ❌ Using "more" with -er

Incorrect:

> She is **more taller** than me.

Correct:

> She is **taller than** me.

### ❌ Using "-er" with adjectives that normally require "more"

Incorrect:

> This is **expensiver** than that one.

Correct:

> This is **more expensive than** that one.

### ❌ Using "most" for a comparison between two things

Incorrect:

> John is **the tallest than** Mark.

Correct:

> John is **taller than** Mark.

---

## Quick Summary

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| Short adjective + **-er** | tall → taller |
| Adjective ending in **-e** + **-r** | large → larger |
| Consonant + y → **-ier** | happy → happier |
| CVC → double consonant + **-er** | big → bigger |
| Long adjective → **more + adjective** | expensive → more expensive |
| Lower degree → **less + adjective** | less expensive |
| Comparison → **than** | taller than |
| good → **better** | better than |
| bad → **worse** | worse than |
| many/much → **more** | more than |
| little → **less** | less than |

### Key Formula

**Comparative adjective = comparison between two elements**

> **A + verb + comparative adjective + than + B**

**Examples:**

> A is **taller than** B.

> A is **more expensive than** B.

> A is **less difficult than** B.
