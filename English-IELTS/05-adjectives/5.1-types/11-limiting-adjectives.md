# Limiting Adjectives

Limiting adjectives, also known as determiners, are words that specify or limit the meaning of a noun. They provide information about definiteness, quantity, possession, number, or which particular noun is being referred to.

## 1. Definition

Limiting adjectives identify or restrict the noun rather than describe its qualities.

Compare:

- a **beautiful** house → descriptive adjective
- **my** house → limiting adjective
- **three** houses → limiting adjective
- **those** houses → limiting adjective

## 2. Articles

Articles indicate whether a noun is specific or non-specific.

### Definite Article

**The** refers to a specific person, thing, or group.

Examples:

- the car
- the book
- the house

Example:
- The car is parked outside.

### Indefinite Articles

**A** and **an** refer to a non-specific person or thing.

Examples:

- a dog
- a car
- an apple
- an engineer

Example:
- I saw a dog in the park.

## 3. Possessive Determiners

Possessive determiners indicate possession or a relationship with the noun.

Examples:

- my
- your
- his
- her
- its
- our
- their

Examples:

- my car
- your house
- his computer
- her book
- our team
- their project

## 4. Quantifiers

Quantifiers indicate an amount or quantity without necessarily specifying an exact number.

Examples:

- some
- any
- many
- much
- few
- several
- most
- all
- enough

Examples:

- some students
- many books
- several options
- few people
- much time
- all employees

## 5. Demonstratives

Demonstrative determiners identify specific nouns based on distance or context.

Examples:

- this
- that
- these
- those

Examples:

- this book
- that car
- these students
- those buildings

## 6. Numbers and Ordinals

Numbers can limit nouns by indicating an exact quantity.

Examples:

- one book
- two cars
- three students
- ten computers

Ordinal numbers indicate position or order.

Examples:

- the first chapter
- the second place
- the third floor

## 7. Distributive Determiners

These indicate individual members of a group or distribution.

Examples:

- each
- every
- either
- neither
- both

Examples:

- each student
- every employee
- either option
- neither answer
- both hands

## 8. Placement

Limiting adjectives or determiners generally appear before the noun they modify.

Examples:

- the book
- my computer
- several students
- those cars
- three employees

They can also appear before descriptive adjectives.

Examples:

- my new computer
- the expensive car
- three large buildings
- those beautiful flowers

## 9. Important Grammar Note

In modern English grammar, terms such as **the, a, my, this, some,** and **each** are generally classified as **determiners**, rather than adjectives.

The term "limiting adjective" is commonly used in some traditional grammar systems, but **determiner** is the more precise modern grammatical term.

## Summary

| Type | Examples | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Articles | a, an, the | the car |
| Possessives | my, your, his, her, our, their | my car |
| Quantifiers | some, many, few, several | several books |
| Demonstratives | this, that, these, those | those students |
| Cardinal numbers | one, two, three | three cars |
| Ordinals | first, second, third | the first chapter |
| Distributives | each, every, either, neither, both | each student |

Limiting adjectives, or more precisely **determiners**, help specify exactly which noun, how many nouns, whose noun, or what quantity of a noun is being discussed.
