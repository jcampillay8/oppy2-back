# Descriptive Adjectives

Descriptive adjectives are one of the most common types of adjectives in English. They provide information about the qualities, characteristics, or attributes of a noun.

## 1. Definition

A descriptive adjective modifies a noun by describing its:

- Appearance
- Size
- Shape
- Color
- Texture
- Condition
- Quality
- Other characteristics

Example:

- The **beautiful** flower.
- The **large** building.
- The **red** car.
- The **old** computer.

In these examples, the adjectives provide additional information about the nouns.

## 2. Placement

Descriptive adjectives usually appear **before the noun** they modify.

Examples:

- A **beautiful** house.
- A **small** dog.
- An **old** computer.
- A **red** car.

However, descriptive adjectives can also appear **after linking verbs** such as "be," "seem," "appear," "look," "feel," "sound," "smell," and "taste."

Examples:

- The house is **beautiful**.
- The dog looks **tired**.
- The computer seems **slow**.
- The food smells **delicious**.

When the adjective appears after a linking verb and describes the subject, it is called a **predicate adjective**.

## 3. Types of Descriptive Adjectives

### Appearance

Examples:

- beautiful
- ugly
- attractive
- elegant
- stunning

Sentences:

- She has a **beautiful** smile.
- They live in an **elegant** house.

### Size

Examples:

- big
- small
- large
- tall
- short
- enormous

Sentences:

- He lives in a **large** apartment.
- They bought a **small** car.

### Color

Examples:

- red
- blue
- green
- yellow
- black
- white

Sentences:

- She has a **blue** backpack.
- He drives a **black** car.

### Shape

Examples:

- round
- square
- triangular
- rectangular
- flat

Sentences:

- We bought a **round** table.
- The room has a **rectangular** shape.

### Texture

Examples:

- soft
- smooth
- rough
- hard
- coarse

Sentences:

- The fabric is **soft**.
- The surface feels **rough**.

### Condition

Examples:

- new
- old
- broken
- damaged
- clean
- dirty
- shiny

Sentences:

- I bought a **new** computer.
- The machine is **broken**.

## 4. Comparative and Superlative Forms

Descriptive adjectives can be used to compare nouns.

### Comparative

Used to compare two things.

Examples:

- tall → taller
- small → smaller
- beautiful → more beautiful

Sentences:

- John is **taller** than Peter.
- This car is **more expensive** than that one.

### Superlative

Used to compare three or more things.

Examples:

- tall → tallest
- small → smallest
- beautiful → most beautiful

Sentences:

- John is the **tallest** student in the class.
- This is the **most beautiful** building in the city.

## 5. Multiple Descriptive Adjectives

Multiple adjectives can be used together to give a more detailed description.

A common adjective order is:

**Opinion → Size → Age → Shape → Color → Origin → Material → Purpose → Noun**

Example:

- A **beautiful small old round red Italian wooden dining table**.

Another example:

- A **beautiful old wooden chest**.

Here:

- beautiful → opinion
- old → age
- wooden → material
- chest → noun

## 6. Adjectives Do Not Change for Plural Nouns

Unlike nouns and verbs, English adjectives generally **do not change form** according to the number or gender of the noun.

Examples:

- A **red** car.
- Two **red** cars.
- A **beautiful** building.
- Three **beautiful** buildings.

Not:

- ~~redS cars~~
- ~~beautifulS buildings~~

The adjective remains the same.

## 7. How to Identify a Descriptive Adjective

Ask:

> **What is the noun like?**

Example:

- The **large** house is expensive.

Question:

- What is the house like?

Answer:

- **Large**

Therefore, "large" is a descriptive adjective.

Another example:

- She bought a **beautiful** dress.

Question:

- What is the dress like?

Answer:

- **Beautiful**

## Quick Summary

**Descriptive adjectives describe the qualities or characteristics of nouns.**

They can describe:

- **Appearance** → beautiful, elegant
- **Size** → big, small, tall
- **Color** → red, blue, green
- **Shape** → round, square
- **Texture** → soft, rough
- **Condition** → new, old, broken
- **Quality** → interesting, difficult, useful

Basic structure:

**Adjective + Noun**

- a **beautiful** house
- a **large** building
- a **red** car
- an **old** computer

They can also appear after linking verbs:

**Subject + Linking Verb + Adjective**

- The house is **beautiful**.
- The building looks **large**.
- The computer seems **slow**.
