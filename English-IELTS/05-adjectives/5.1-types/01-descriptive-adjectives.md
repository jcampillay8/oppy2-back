# 📚 English Grammar — Descriptive Adjectives

**Descriptive adjectives** are one of the most common types of adjectives. They provide specific details, descriptions, or qualities about a noun, making sentences more precise and informative.

---

## 1. Definition

A **descriptive adjective** modifies a noun by describing one or more of its qualities, such as:

- Appearance
- Size
- Shape
- Color
- Texture
- Age
- Condition
- Other characteristics

### Example

> She bought a **beautiful** dress.

- **Noun:** dress
- **Descriptive adjective:** beautiful

The adjective **"beautiful"** describes the noun **"dress."**

---

## 2. Placement

Descriptive adjectives generally appear **before the noun** they modify.

### Example

> The **beautiful flower** is in the garden.

- **Adjective:** beautiful
- **Noun:** flower

However, descriptive adjectives can also appear **after linking verbs** such as:

- be
- seem
- appear
- become
- feel
- look
- sound

### Example

> The flower **is beautiful**.

Here, **"beautiful"** describes the subject **"flower"** after the linking verb **"is."**

## 4. Comparative and Superlative Forms

Descriptive adjectives can be used to compare people, objects, or situations.

### Comparative

Used to compare **two** things.

> John is **taller** than Peter.

> This car is **more expensive** than that one.

Common structures:

- Short adjectives → **-er**
  - tall → taller
  - fast → faster
  - small → smaller

- Longer adjectives → **more + adjective**
  - beautiful → more beautiful
  - expensive → more expensive
  - interesting → more interesting

### Superlative

Used to compare **three or more** things.

> John is the **tallest** student in the class.

> This is the **most expensive** car in the store.

Common structures:

- Short adjectives → **-est**
  - tall → tallest
  - fast → fastest
  - small → smallest

- Longer adjectives → **most + adjective**
  - beautiful → most beautiful
  - expensive → most expensive
  - interesting → most interesting

---

## 5. Multiple Descriptive Adjectives

It is possible to use several adjectives to describe the same noun.

The usual adjective order is:

> **Opinion → Size → Age → Shape → Color → Origin → Material → Purpose → Noun**

### Example

> She bought a **beautiful old wooden chest**.

Breakdown:

- **beautiful** → opinion
- **old** → age
- **wooden** → material
- **chest** → noun

Another example:

> He drives a **large new red German sports car**.

Breakdown:

- **large** → size
- **new** → age
- **red** → color
- **German** → origin
- **sports** → purpose/type
- **car** → noun

---

## 6. Adjectives and Nouns

Unlike verbs, English adjectives **do not change according to the number or gender of the noun**.

For example:

> The **red car** is fast.

> The **red cars** are fast.

The adjective **"red"** remains the same.

### Important Correction

English adjectives generally **do not agree in number or gender** with the noun.

Therefore:

❌ The adjective changes according to the plural noun.

✅ The adjective normally stays the same.

Examples:

> a **tall** building  
> **tall** buildings

> a **beautiful** woman  
> **beautiful** women

## 🧠 Key Rules

1. **Adjectives describe nouns.**
   > a **beautiful** house

2. **They usually come before the noun.**
   > a **large** car

3. **They can come after linking verbs.**
   > The car is **large**.

4. **Several adjectives can describe one noun.**
   > a beautiful old wooden house

5. **Adjectives can have comparative and superlative forms.**
   > tall → taller → tallest

6. **English adjectives normally do not change for plural or gender.**
   > a **red** car / two **red** cars

### 💡 Easy way to remember

> **Adjective = describes a noun**

**What kind? → beautiful**  
**What size? → large**  
**What color? → red**  
**What condition? → broken**
