# Proper Adjectives

Proper adjectives are adjectives derived from proper nouns. They describe nouns by referring to a specific person, place, culture, nationality, historical period, or other proper name.

## 1. Definition

A proper adjective is derived from a proper noun and is used to modify or describe another noun.

- America → American
- Shakespeare → Shakespearean
- Italy → Italian
- Rome → Roman

**Example:**

> We enjoyed an **Italian** meal.

Here, **Italian** is a proper adjective because it comes from the proper noun **Italy** and describes the noun **meal**.

## 2. Formation

Proper adjectives are commonly formed from proper nouns.

| Proper Noun | Proper Adjective |
|---|---|
| America | American |
| China | Chinese |
| France | French |
| Italy | Italian |
| Rome | Roman |
| Shakespeare | Shakespearean |
| Victoria | Victorian |
| Greece | Greek |

## 3. Capitalization

Proper adjectives are always capitalized because they come from proper nouns.

- **French** cuisine.
- **Chinese** culture.
- **Italian** fashion.
- **Roman** architecture.
- **Victorian** literature.

❌ french cuisine  
✅ **French** cuisine

## 4. Descriptive Function

Like other adjectives, proper adjectives modify nouns and provide additional information about them.

- **Chinese** cuisine
- **Roman** architecture
- **Olympic** Games
- **Shakespearean** literature
- **Mexican** food

In:

> She studies **Greek** mythology.

**Greek** is the proper adjective, and **mythology** is the noun it modifies.

## 5. Specificity

Proper adjectives provide specific information about the origin, association, or characteristics of a noun.

- **American** history
- **Japanese** technology
- **Brazilian** music
- **Roman** culture
- **Victorian** architecture

Compare:

- **traditional architecture** → general description
- **Roman architecture** → specific historical/geographical association

## 6. Cultural, Geographic, and Historical Associations

Proper adjectives are frequently used to describe cultural, geographic, or historical characteristics.

### Cultural

- **Japanese** culture
- **Chinese** traditions
- **Mexican** cuisine

### Geographic

- **African** wildlife
- **European** cities
- **Canadian** forests

### Historical

- **Roman** Empire
- **Victorian** society
- **Shakespearean** theater

## 7. Proper Adjectives from People's Names

Proper adjectives can also be derived from the names of people.

- Shakespeare → **Shakespearean**
- Darwin → **Darwinian**
- Marx → **Marxist**
- Mozart → **Mozartian**

**Examples:**

> He enjoys **Shakespearean** plays.

> The museum has a collection of **Mozartian** compositions.

## 8. Proper Adjectives Do Not Change Form

Proper adjectives generally do not change according to the number or gender of the noun they modify.

- **Italian** food
- **Italian** restaurants
- **Italian** traditions
- **Italian** artists

The adjective **Italian** remains the same.

## 10. How to Identify a Proper Adjective

To identify a proper adjective, ask:

> **Does this adjective come from or refer to a specific proper noun?**

If yes, it is likely a proper adjective.

**Example:**

> They visited a **Roman** museum.

- **Roman** → proper adjective
- **museum** → noun
- **Rome** → proper noun from which "Roman" is derived

## Summary

| Feature | Proper Adjective |
|---|---|
| Function | Modifies a noun |
| Origin | Proper noun |
| Capitalization | Always capitalized |
| Purpose | Indicates specific origin or association |
| Examples | American, French, Roman, Chinese |
| Changes for gender/number? | Generally no |
