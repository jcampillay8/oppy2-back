# Demonstrative Adjectives

Demonstrative adjectives are used to point out or identify specific nouns. They indicate whether the noun is close to or far from the speaker.

## 1. Definition

A demonstrative adjective modifies a noun by pointing to a specific person, place, thing, or idea.

Example:

- This book is interesting.

"This" is a demonstrative adjective because it identifies the specific noun "book."

## 2. Demonstrative Adjectives

There are four main demonstrative adjectives:

| Demonstrative | Number | Distance |
|---|---|---|
| **this** | Singular | Near |
| **that** | Singular | Far |
| **these** | Plural | Near |
| **those** | Plural | Far |

## 3. This

"This" is used with **singular nouns** that are close to the speaker.

Examples:

- This book is interesting.
- This car is new.
- This computer is fast.
- This house is beautiful.

Meaning:

- "This book" = one book that is near or being discussed.

## 4. That

"That" is used with **singular nouns** that are farther away from the speaker.

Examples:

- That car is expensive.
- That building is very tall.
- That dog is friendly.
- That restaurant is popular.

Meaning:

- "That car" = one specific car that is farther away.

## 5. These

"These" is used with **plural nouns** that are close to the speaker.

Examples:

- These books are interesting.
- These shoes are comfortable.
- These computers are new.
- These flowers are beautiful.

Meaning:

- "These books" = several books that are near.

## 6. Those

"Those" is used with **plural nouns** that are farther away from the speaker.

Examples:

- Those cars are expensive.
- Those buildings are very tall.
- Those students are studying.
- Those mountains are beautiful.

Meaning:

- "Those cars" = several cars that are farther away.

## 7. Singular vs. Plural

The demonstrative adjective must match the noun in number.

### Singular

- This book
- That car
- This computer
- That house

### Plural

- These books
- Those cars
- These computers
- Those houses

A useful pattern is:

- **This** → one + near
- **That** → one + far
- **These** → more than one + near
- **Those** → more than one + far

## 8. Placement

Demonstrative adjectives normally appear **before the noun** they modify.

Examples:

- This book
- That computer
- These students
- Those buildings

Sentence examples:

- This book belongs to me.
- That computer is broken.
- These students are studying.
- Those buildings are very old.

## 9. Demonstrative Adjectives vs. Demonstrative Pronouns

The main difference is whether the word is followed by a noun.

### Demonstrative adjective

The demonstrative adjective modifies a noun.

- This book is mine.
- That car is expensive.
- These shoes are new.
- Those houses are beautiful.

Here, "this," "that," "these," and "those" are followed by nouns.

### Demonstrative pronoun

The demonstrative pronoun replaces the noun.

- This is mine.
- That is expensive.
- These are new.
- Those are beautiful.

Here, there is no noun immediately after the demonstrative.

## 10. Examples

| Sentence | Demonstrative | Noun | Number | Distance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| This book is interesting. | this | book | Singular | Near |
| That car is expensive. | that | car | Singular | Far |
| These flowers are beautiful. | these | flowers | Plural | Near |
| Those buildings are old. | those | buildings | Plural | Far |

## Quick Summary

**Demonstrative adjectives point to specific nouns.**

- **This** → singular + near
- **That** → singular + far
- **These** → plural + near
- **Those** → plural + far

Examples:

- **This** phone is new.
- **That** phone is old.
- **These** phones are new.
- **Those** phones are old.

Remember:

> **This / These = near**
>
> **That / Those = far**
>
> **This / That = singular**
>
> **These / Those = plural**
