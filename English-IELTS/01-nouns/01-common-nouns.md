# 📚 Common Nouns — Sustantivos Comunes

> **Common nouns** are words that represent general names for people, places, things, or ideas. Unlike **proper nouns**, common nouns are not capitalized unless they appear at the beginning of a sentence.

---

## 1. General Names

Common nouns are used to refer to **broad categories** of people, places, things, or ideas.

### Examples

- `cat`
- `city`
- `table`
- `book`
- `happiness`

---

## 2. Categories and Classes

Common nouns can represent a **group or class of similar entities**.

### Examples

- `animal`
- `vehicle`
- `fruit`
- `furniture`
- `emotion`

---

## 3. Non-Specific References

Common nouns do not refer to specific individuals or entities. Instead, they refer to **any member of a particular category**.

### Examples

- **A dog** → refers to any dog.
- **The book** → refers to a book without necessarily specifying its title or identity.

---

## 4. Countable and Uncountable Nouns

Common nouns can be classified as either **countable** or **uncountable**.

### 🔢 Countable Nouns

These nouns have **singular and plural forms** and can be counted.

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| `dog` | `dogs` |
| `chair` | `chairs` |

### 💧 Uncountable Nouns

These nouns **cannot normally be counted individually** and generally do not have a plural form.

### Examples

- `water`
- `information`
- `happiness`

---

## 5. Modifiers and Determiners

Common nouns can be modified by **adjectives** or preceded by **determiners**, such as:

- Articles → `a`, `an`, `the`
- Demonstratives → `this`, `that`
- Possessives → `my`, `your`
- Quantifiers → `some`, `many`

### Examples

- `a cat`
- `this city`
- `my table`
- `some books`

---

## 6. Function in Sentences

Common nouns can perform different functions within a sentence.

### 6.1 Subject

The common noun that **performs the action** or is the main focus of the sentence.

**Example:**

> `Cats are playful.`

Here, **cats** is the subject.

---

### 6.2 Object

The common noun that **receives the action** of the verb.

**Example:**

> `She bought a book.`

Here, **book** is the object.

---

### 6.3 Modifier

Common nouns can be used as **modifiers** to describe or specify another noun. These are also known as **attributive nouns**.

### Examples

- `book cover`
- `car engine`

In these examples:

- `book` modifies `cover`.
- `car` modifies `engine`.

---

# 🧠 Key Takeaways

- **Common nouns** refer to general people, places, things, or ideas.
- They are generally **not capitalized** unless they begin a sentence.
- They can represent **categories or classes**.
- They can be **countable or uncountable**.
- They can be modified by **adjectives and determiners**.
- They can function as **subjects, objects, or modifiers** within sentences.

> 💡 **In simple terms:** Common nouns are the general names we use for everyday people, places, things, and ideas. They are fundamental to English communication because they allow us to express concepts without referring to a specific individual or entity.
