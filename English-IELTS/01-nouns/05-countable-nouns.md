# 🔢 Countable Nouns — Sustantivos Contables

> **Countable nouns**, also known as **count nouns** or **countables**, are nouns that can be counted as separate individual items or units. They have **singular and plural forms** and can be used with numerical quantities or quantifiers.

---

## 1. 🔤 Singular and Plural Forms

Countable nouns have distinct **singular and plural forms**.

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| `cat` | `cats` |
| `book` | `books` |
| `chair` | `chairs` |

---

## 2. 🔢 Counting and Quantifying

Countable nouns can be counted or quantified using:

- Numbers
- Indefinite articles (`a/an`)
- Quantifiers

### Examples

- `three cats`
- `an apple`
- `many books`
- `a few students`

---

## 3. 📊 Quantifiers

Countable nouns can be used with quantifiers to indicate a **specific or approximate quantity**.

### Common Quantifiers

- `some`
- `any`
- `several`
- `few`
- `many`
- `a couple`
- `a dozen`

### Examples

- `some students`
- `several books`
- `many cars`
- `a few questions`
- `a couple of chairs`

---

## 4. 🔄 Subject-Verb Agreement

The number of a countable noun affects the **agreement between the subject and the verb**.

### Singular

> `The cat is sleeping.`

- `cat` → singular
- `is` → singular verb

### Plural

> `The cats are playing.`

- `cats` → plural
- `are` → plural verb

### Rule

> **Singular noun → Singular verb**  
> **Plural noun → Plural verb**

---

## 5. 🎯 Individually Identifiable

Countable nouns refer to objects or entities that are **distinct and can be viewed as separate items**.

### Examples

- `desk`
- `chair`
- `student`
- `car`
- `pen`

For example:

- `one chair`
- `two chairs`
- `five chairs`

Each chair can be identified and counted separately.

---

## 6. ➕ Plural Formation

Countable nouns commonly form their plural by adding **`-s`** or **`-es`** to the singular form.

### Examples

- `cat` → `cats`
- `book` → `books`
- `box` → `boxes`
- `class` → `classes`

> ⚠️ Some countable nouns have **irregular plural forms**.
>
> Examples:
> - `child` → `children`
> - `person` → `people`
> - `man` → `men`
> - `woman` → `women`

---

## 7. 📝 Articles and Determiners

Countable nouns can be used with **articles and determiners** to specify particular instances.

### Examples

- `a cat`
- `the book`
- `these chairs`
- `his pen`

### Common Determiners

- `a`
- `an`
- `the`
- `this`
- `that`
- `these`
- `those`
- `my`
- `his`
- `her`
- `some`
- `many`

# 🔎 Countable vs. Uncountable Nouns

| Countable | Uncountable |
|---|---|
| `one book` | `information` |
| `two chairs` | `water` |
| `three apples` | `advice` |
| `many students` | `knowledge` |
| `a few cars` | `coffee` |

### Countable

> `I have three books.`

### Uncountable

> `I need some information.`

---

# 🧠 Key Takeaways

- **Countable nouns** can be counted individually.
- They have **singular and plural forms**.
- They can be used with **numbers**.
- Singular countable nouns can use **`a/an`**.
- They can be used with quantifiers such as:
  - `many`
  - `several`
  - `few`
  - `a couple`
- They follow **subject-verb agreement**.
- Their plural is commonly formed with **`-s` or `-es`**, although some nouns have irregular forms.

> 💡 **In simple terms:** If you can say **one, two, three, four...** of something, it is generally a **countable noun**.
>
> **Examples:** `one dog → two dogs`, `one book → three books`, `one car → five cars`.
