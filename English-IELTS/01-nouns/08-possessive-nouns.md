# 🔑 Possessive Nouns — Sustantivos Posesivos

> **Possessive nouns** are nouns that show **ownership, possession, relationship, or association**. They indicate that something belongs to or is connected to someone or something else.
>
> Possessive nouns are commonly formed using an **apostrophe (')** and sometimes an **s**.

---

## 1. 👤 Singular Nouns

For most **singular nouns**, add:

**'s**

### Examples

- **the dog's bone** → the bone belonging to the dog
- **Mary's book** → the book belonging to Mary
- **the company's logo** → the logo belonging to the company

### Structure

**Singular Noun + 's + Thing**

### Example

> **The dog's bone is missing.**

- **dog** → noun
- **'s** → possessive marker
- **bone** → thing being possessed

---

## 2. 🔤 Singular Nouns Ending in "s"

For singular nouns ending in **s**, **'s** is commonly used in modern English.

### Examples

- **James's car**
- **the waitress's tip**
- **the boss's office**

### Examples in Sentences

> **James's car is outside.**

> **The boss's office is on the second floor.**

> 💡 **Note:** Some style guides allow or prefer only an apostrophe (') in certain names ending in **s**, such as **James' car**. However, **James's** is a common and widely accepted form.

---

## 3. 👨‍👩‍👧 Plural Nouns NOT Ending in "s"

For irregular plural nouns that **do not end in s**, add:

**'s**

### Examples

- **the children's toys**
- **the women's team**
- **the mice's nest**

### Examples in Sentences

> **The children's toys are everywhere.**

> **The women's team won the competition.**

> **The mice's nest was hidden behind the wall.**

---

## 4. 👥 Plural Nouns Ending in "s"

For plural nouns that already end in **s**, add only an apostrophe:

**'**

### Examples

- **the students' backpacks**
- **the cars' tires**
- **the houses' roofs**

### Examples in Sentences

> **The students' backpacks are in the classroom.**

> **The cars' tires need to be replaced.**

> **The houses' roofs were damaged.**

---

# 📊 Possessive Noun Rules

| Type of Noun | Possessive Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Singular | + **'s** | dog → **dog's** |
| Singular ending in s | + **'s** | James → **James's** |
| Irregular plural | + **'s** | children → **children's** |
| Regular plural ending in s | + **'** | students → **students'** |

---

# 🔎 Possession vs. Association

Possessive nouns do not always indicate **literal ownership**. They can also express a **relationship, connection, or association**.

### Examples

> **My sister's wedding.**

This means:

> The wedding **of my sister**.

---

> **The city's skyline.**

This means:

> The skyline **of the city**.

---

> **The company's employees.**

This means:

> The employees **associated with the company**.

---

# ⚠️ Possessive Nouns vs. Possessive Pronouns

Not every word that expresses possession is a possessive noun.

**Possessive pronouns** already indicate ownership and **do not use an apostrophe**.

### Examples

- **yours**
- **hers**
- **theirs**
- **ours**
- **mine**

### Compare

| Possessive Noun | Possessive Pronoun |
|---|---|
| **John's book** | **his book** |
| **Mary's car** | **hers** |
| **the students' classroom** | **theirs** |

> ⚠️ **Important:** Do not write **your's**, **her's**, or **their's**. These forms are incorrect.

---

# 🧠 Key Takeaways

- **Possessive nouns** show ownership, possession, relationship, or association.
- Most singular nouns use **'s**.
- Singular nouns ending in **s** commonly use **'s**.
- Irregular plural nouns use **'s**.
- Regular plural nouns ending in **s** use **only '**.

### Quick Rule

| Noun Type | Possessive Form |
|---|---|
| Singular noun | **dog's** |
| Singular ending in s | **James's** |
| Irregular plural | **children's** |
| Plural ending in s | **students'** |

> 💡 **In simple terms:** The apostrophe tells us **who or what something belongs to or is associated with**.
>
> **the dog's bone** → the bone belongs to/is associated with the dog  
> **the students' books** → the books belong to/are associated with the students  
> **the city's skyline** → the skyline is associated with the city
