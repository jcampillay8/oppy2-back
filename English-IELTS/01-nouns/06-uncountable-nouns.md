# ♾️ Uncountable Nouns — Sustantivos Incontables

> **Uncountable nouns**, also known as **non-count nouns** or **mass nouns**, are nouns that do not normally have a plural form and cannot be counted as separate individual units. They represent substances, concepts, or qualities that are viewed as a whole or as an uncountable quantity.

---

## 1. 🚫 No Plural Form

Uncountable nouns are generally used in the **singular form** and do not normally take a plural `-s`.

### Examples

- `water` ❌ `waters`
- `information` ❌ `informations`
- `happiness` ❌ `happinesses`

> 💡 **Important:** This does not mean that an uncountable noun can never appear in a plural form in English. Some words can become countable when their meaning changes according to context.

---

## 2. 🌊 Indefinite Quantity

Uncountable nouns are viewed as a **mass, substance, or whole**, rather than as individual units.

### Examples

- `sugar`
- `sand`
- `air`
- `milk`
- `advice`

For example:

> `I need some water.`

We do not normally count `water` directly. Instead, we can use a unit:

> `I need two bottles of water.`

---

## 3. 🔢 Numerical Quantifiers

Uncountable nouns generally **cannot be used directly with numbers**.

### ❌ Incorrect

- `three waters`
- `two informations`
- `five advices`

### ✅ Correct

- `some water`
- `a lot of information`
- `a little sugar`
- `much happiness`

### Common Quantifiers

| Quantifier | Example |
|---|---|
| `some` | `some water` |
| `a lot of` | `a lot of information` |
| `a little` | `a little sugar` |
| `much` | `much happiness` |
| `a great deal of` | `a great deal of knowledge` |

---

## 4. 💭 Abstract Concepts

Uncountable nouns often represent **abstract concepts, qualities, or states** that cannot be physically perceived.

### Examples

- `love`
- `honesty`
- `courage`
- `intelligence`
- `knowledge`

---

## 5. 🪵 Materials and Substances

Uncountable nouns can represent **materials or substances** that are not viewed as individual objects.

### Examples

- `wood`
- `glass`
- `plastic`
- `metal`
- `rice`

For example:

> `The table is made of wood.`

We normally treat `wood` as a substance rather than counting individual pieces.

---

## 6. 🍞 Food and Beverages

Many food and beverage terms are commonly used as uncountable nouns.

### Examples

- `bread`
- `cheese`
- `butter`
- `coffee`
- `tea`
- `sugar`
- `rice`

### Example

> `I drink coffee every morning.`

---

## 7. 🔄 Countable or Uncountable Depending on Context

Some nouns can be **countable or uncountable**, depending on their meaning and how they are used.

### Example: `beer`

**Uncountable:**

> `I like to drink beer.`

Here, `beer` refers to the beverage in general.

**Countable:**

> `I have two beers.`

Here, `beers` refers to **two individual servings, bottles, cans, or glasses** of beer.

### Another Example: `coffee`

**Uncountable:**

> `I drink coffee every morning.`

**Countable:**

> `We ordered two coffees.`

Here, `coffees` refers to two individual servings.

# 🔎 Countable vs. Uncountable Nouns

| Countable | Uncountable |
|---|---|
| `one apple` | `some rice` |
| `two books` | `some information` |
| `three chairs` | `some furniture` |
| `many students` | `much knowledge` |
| `a few cars` | `a little water` |

### Countable

> `I bought three apples.`

### Uncountable

> `I bought some rice.`

---

# 📏 How to Count Uncountable Nouns

Although uncountable nouns cannot normally be counted directly, we can use **units or containers** to express quantities.

### Examples

Instead of:

> ❌ `two waters`

Use:

> ✅ `two bottles of water`

Instead of:

> ❌ `three breads`

Use:

> ✅ `three loaves of bread`

Instead of:

> ❌ `two informations`

Use:

> ✅ `two pieces of information`

### Common Structures

```text
number + unit/container + of + uncountable noun
