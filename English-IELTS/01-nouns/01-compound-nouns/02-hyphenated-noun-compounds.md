# 🔗 Hyphenated Noun Compounds

> **Hyphenated noun compounds** are a type of compound noun formed by connecting **two or more words with hyphens (-)**.
>
> Unlike **closed compounds**, where words are combined without spaces or hyphens, or **open compounds**, where words remain separate, hyphenated compounds use hyphens to connect the words and show that they function as a unit.

---

# 1. 🏗️ Formation

Hyphenated compound nouns are created by linking **two or more words with hyphens (-)** to form a single term.

### Structure

**Word + Word → Word-Word**

The hyphens help clarify the relationship between the words and indicate that they function together as a **single concept**.

### Examples

- **well-being**
- **sister-in-law**
- **high-school**
- **coffee-table**
- **make-up**

---

# 2. 📚 Examples

## 🌱 Well-being

**well-being** is formed from:

- **well** → indicating a state of good health or happiness
- **being** → the state of existing or living

> **Exercise can contribute to your well-being.**

---

## 👩 Sister-in-law

**sister-in-law** is formed from:

- **sister** → a female sibling
- **in-law** → a family relationship created through marriage

> **My sister-in-law lives in London.**

---

## 🏫 High-school

**high-school** combines:

- **high** → indicating a higher educational level
- **school** → an educational institution

> **He attends a high school near his home.**

> 💡 **Note:** In modern standard English, **high school** is normally written as an **open compound** when used as a noun. The hyphenated form **high-school** is generally used only in specific compound modifiers.

---

# 3. 🧠 Semantic Relationship

Like other compound nouns, hyphenated compounds create a **specific meaning** from the words they combine.

The relationship between the words can vary.

---

## 3.1 Noun + Noun

The first noun modifies or describes the second noun.

### Example

**coffee-table**

> A table specifically associated with or used for placing items such as coffee.

### Other examples

- **school-board**
- **train-station**
- **tax-return**

> 💡 **Note:** Many combinations that were historically or occasionally hyphenated are now commonly written as open or closed compounds depending on the word and context.

---

## 3.2 Adjective + Noun

The adjective provides additional information about the noun.

### Example

**high-school**

> A school providing education at a higher level than elementary or middle school.

### Other examples

- **full-time**
- **part-time**
- **last-minute**

---

## 3.3 Verb + Noun

The verb functions as a modifier for the noun.

### Example

**make-up**

> Cosmetics applied to the face or body to enhance appearance.

### Other examples

- **break-in**
- **take-off**
- **check-in**

---

# 4. 🔢 Plural Formation

The plural form of a hyphenated compound depends on its structure.

For compounds containing a clear **main noun**, the plural is generally formed by pluralizing that main noun.

### Examples

- **sister-in-law → sisters-in-law**
- **mother-in-law → mothers-in-law**
- **passer-by → passers-by**

### Example

> **She has two sisters-in-law.**

---

## ⚠️ Important

It is **not always correct** to simply add **-s** to the end of the entire compound.

For example:

> ❌ **sister-in-laws**

> ✅ **sisters-in-law**

The main noun is **sister**, so that is the word that becomes plural.

---

# 5. 🔊 Stress and Pronunciation

In many compound nouns, the primary stress tends to fall on the **first element** of the compound.

### Examples

- **WELL-being**
- **SISter-in-law**
- **CHECK-in**
- **BREAK-in**

> 💡 **Note:** Stress patterns vary depending on the specific word, dialect, and context. This is a general tendency rather than an absolute rule.

---

# 📊 Hyphenated Compound Nouns — Quick Reference

| Compound | Structure | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **well-being** | adjective + noun | State of being healthy or happy |
| **sister-in-law** | noun + preposition + noun | Female relative by marriage |
| **mother-in-law** | noun + preposition + noun | Mother of one's spouse |
| **check-in** | verb + particle | Process of registering or arriving |
| **break-in** | verb + particle | An instance of entering by force |
| **passer-by** | verb + preposition | A person who passes by |

---

# 🔄 Compound Nouns: Three Main Forms

Compound nouns can appear in three main written forms.

| Type | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Closed / Solid** | One word | **keyboard** |
| **Hyphenated** | Connected with hyphens | **well-being** |
| **Open** | Separate words | **coffee table** |

> 💡 The spelling of compound nouns can change over time. Some compounds begin as open or hyphenated forms and eventually become closed compounds as their usage becomes more established.

---

# 🧠 Key Takeaways

- **Hyphenated noun compounds** connect two or more words using **hyphens (-)**.
- The hyphens indicate that the words function together as a **single unit or concept**.
- Common structures include:
  - **Noun + Noun**
  - **Adjective + Noun**
  - **Verb + Noun**
  - **Noun + Preposition + Noun**
- The plural is generally formed by pluralizing the **main noun**.
- Some hyphenated compounds can have different spelling conventions depending on context and modern usage.
- Compound noun spelling can evolve over time from **open → hyphenated → closed**.

### Quick Rule

> **Closed:** keyboard  
>
> **Hyphenated:** well-being  
>
> **Open:** coffee table

> 🎯 **Remember:** The hyphen is not merely punctuation. In a hyphenated compound, it helps show that the words should be understood as a **single combined concept**.
