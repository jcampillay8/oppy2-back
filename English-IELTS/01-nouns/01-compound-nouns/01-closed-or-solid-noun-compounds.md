# 🔒 Closed or Solid Compound Nouns

> **Closed or solid compound nouns** are a type of compound noun where **two or more words are combined to form a single word**, without spaces or hyphens.
>
> In closed compounds, the words are tightly joined together, creating a unified term with a specific meaning.

---

# 1. 🏗️ Formation

Closed compound nouns are formed by combining **two or more words** into a single word.

### Structure

**Word + Word → Single Word**

### Examples

- **key + board → keyboard**
- **sun + rise → sunrise**
- **foot + ball → football**
- **bed + room → bedroom**
- **air + port → airport**

There are **no spaces or hyphens** between the elements.

---

# 2. 📚 Examples

## ⌨️ Keyboard

**keyboard** is formed from:

- **key** → an individual button
- **board** → a flat surface

> **I need a new keyboard for my computer.**

---

## 🌅 Sunrise

**sunrise** is formed from:

- **sun** → the star at the center of our solar system
- **rise** → the act of ascending or coming up

> **We woke up early to watch the sunrise.**

---

## ⚽ Football

**football** is formed from:

- **foot** → a part of the body
- **ball** → a round object used in sports

> **Football is popular in many countries.**

---

# 3. 🧠 Semantic Relationship

In closed compound nouns, the individual words work together to create a **new and specific meaning**.

The relationship between the words can vary.

---

## 3.1 Noun + Noun

The first noun modifies or describes the second noun.

### Example

**football**

> A ball associated with a specific sport.

Other examples:

- **keyboard**
- **bedroom**
- **sunflower**
- **toothbrush**

---

## 3.2 Adjective + Noun

The adjective provides additional information about the noun.

### Example

**whiteboard**

> A type of board that can be written on with markers and erased.

Other examples:

- **greenhouse**
- **blackboard**
- **highway**

---

## 3.3 Verb + Noun

The verb functions as a modifier for the noun.

### Example

**birthmark**

> A mark on the skin that is present from birth.

Other examples:

- **breakfast**
- **washroom**
- **pickpocket**

---

# 4. 🔢 Plural Formation

For closed compound nouns, the plural is generally formed by adding the plural marker to the **main or primary word**.

### Examples

- **keyboard → keyboards**
- **sunrise → sunrises**
- **football → footballs**
- **bedroom → bedrooms**
- **toothbrush → toothbrushes**

### Example

> **There are several keyboards in the office.**

---

# 5. 🔊 Stress and Pronunciation

In many closed compound nouns, the **primary stress tends to fall on the first element** of the compound.

### Examples

- **KEYboard**
- **SUNrise**
- **FOOTball**
- **BEDroom**
- **TOOTHbrush**

This stress pattern helps identify the words as compound nouns.

> 💡 **Note:** Stress patterns can vary depending on the specific word, dialect, and context, so this is a general tendency rather than an absolute rule.

---

# 📊 Closed Compound Nouns — Quick Reference

| Compound | Structure | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **keyboard** | key + board | A device with keys used for typing |
| **sunrise** | sun + rise | The appearance of the sun above the horizon |
| **football** | foot + ball | A sport or the ball used in that sport |
| **bedroom** | bed + room | A room used for sleeping |
| **airport** | air + port | A place where aircraft arrive and depart |
| **toothbrush** | tooth + brush | A brush used to clean teeth |
| **sunflower** | sun + flower | A type of flowering plant |

---

# 🧠 Key Takeaways

- **Closed compound nouns** combine two or more words into **one word**.
- They contain **no spaces or hyphens**.
- The combined words create a **specific meaning**.
- Common structures include:
  - **Noun + Noun**
  - **Adjective + Noun**
  - **Verb + Noun**
- The plural is generally formed by adding the plural marker to the **main word**.
- The primary stress often falls on the **first element**.

### Quick Comparison

| Compound Type | Example |
|---|---|
| **Closed / Solid** | **keyboard** |
| **Hyphenated** | **well-being** |
| **Open** | **coffee table** |

> 💡 **Simple rule:** If two or more words combine and are written as **one word without spaces or hyphens**, you have a **closed or solid compound noun**.
