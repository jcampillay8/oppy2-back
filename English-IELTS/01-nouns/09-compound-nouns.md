# 🧩 Compound Nouns — Sustantivos Compuestos

> **Compound nouns** are formed by combining **two or more words** to create a single noun. When these words come together, they function as a unit and convey a specific meaning.
>
> Compound nouns can be formed in different ways, and understanding their structure is essential for effective communication.

---

# 1. 📝 Types of Compound Nouns

Compound nouns can be divided into three main types based on how they are written.

## 1.1 Closed or Solid Compounds

These compound nouns are written as **a single word**, without spaces or hyphens.

### Examples

- **keyboard**
- **sunrise**
- **football**
- **bedroom**
- **airport**

### Example

> **I left my keyboard in the bedroom.**

---

## 1.2 Hyphenated Compounds

These compound nouns are formed by connecting two or more words with **hyphens**.

### Examples

- **well-being**
- **sister-in-law**
- **high-school**
- **up-to-date**

### Example

> **She has a strong sense of well-being.**

---

## 1.3 Open Compounds

These compound nouns consist of two or more words that are written as **separate words**.

### Examples

- **post office**
- **coffee table**
- **health care**
- **parking lot**

### Example

> **The post office is near the parking lot.**

---

# 2. 🏗️ Structure and Word Order

Compound nouns can also be categorized according to the relationship between the words that form them.

---

## 2.1 Noun + Noun

The first noun modifies or describes the second noun.

### Examples

- **coffee table**
- **car park**
- **bookshelf**
- **sunflower**

### Example

> **The coffee table is in the living room.**

Here, **coffee** describes the type or purpose of the **table**.

---

## 2.2 Adjective + Noun

The adjective provides additional information about the noun.

### Examples

- **blackboard**
- **greenhouse**
- **bigfoot**
- **loudspeaker**

### Example

> **The teacher wrote on the blackboard.**

Here, **black** describes the **board**.

---

## 2.3 Verb + Noun

The verb functions as a modifier for the noun.

### Examples

- **breakfast**
- **homework**
- **rainfall**
- **textbook**

### Example

> **I finished my homework before dinner.**

---

## 2.4 Preposition + Noun

The preposition introduces or describes the noun.

### Examples

- **outlook**
- **underground**
- **overcoat**
- **bypass**

### Example

> **The underground is an important form of transportation in London.**

---

# 3. 🔢 Singular and Plural Forms

In compound nouns, the plural is typically formed by adding the plural marker to the **main or primary word** of the compound.

### Examples

- **coffee table → coffee tables**
- **car park → car parks**
- **bookshelf → bookshelves**
- **sunflower → sunflowers**

### Example

> **There are three coffee tables in the room.**

> 💡 **Tip:** When a compound noun contains more than one noun, identify which word represents the main object. That is usually the word that becomes plural.

---

# 4. 🧠 Meaning and Semantic Relationship

Compound nouns combine words to convey a **specific meaning or concept**.

The relationship between the words can vary depending on the structure.

---

## 4.1 Noun + Noun

The first noun acts as a modifier and indicates the **type, purpose, or characteristics** of the second noun.

### Example

> **coffee table**

A **coffee table** is a table designed or commonly used for placing items such as coffee.

---

## 4.2 Adjective + Noun

The adjective provides a **quality, characteristic, or attribute** of the noun.

### Example

> **blackboard**

A **blackboard** is traditionally a board that is black in color.

---

## 4.3 Verb + Noun

The verb expresses an **action or process** related to the noun.

### Example

> **rainfall**

**Rainfall** refers to rain falling from the sky.

---

## 4.4 Preposition + Noun

The preposition can indicate a **location, direction, or relationship**.

### Example

> **outlook**

**Outlook** can refer to a person's view, perspective, or way of thinking.

---

# 5. 🔊 Stress and Pronunciation

In many compound nouns, the **primary stress tends to fall on the first element** of the compound.

### Examples

- **COFFEE table**
- **POST office**
- **HIGH school**
- **BLACKboard**

This stress pattern can help distinguish compound nouns from ordinary combinations of words.

> 💡 **Important:** Stress patterns can vary depending on the specific word, dialect, and context, so this is a useful general pattern rather than an absolute rule.

|---|---|---|
| **Closed / Solid** | One word | **keyboard, bedroom, airport** |
| **Hyphenated** | Connected with hyphens | **well-being, sister-in-law** |

# 📊 Compound Noun Structures

| Structure | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Noun + Noun** | **coffee table** | A table associated with coffee |
| **Adjective + Noun** | **blackboard** | A board traditionally associated with the color black |
| **Verb + Noun** | **rainfall** | The falling of rain |
| **Preposition + Noun** | **outlook** | A view or perspective |

---

# 🧠 Key Takeaways

- **Compound nouns** combine two or more words to create a noun with a specific meaning.
- They can be written in three main ways:
  - **Closed:** **bedroom**
  - **Hyphenated:** **well-being**
  - **Open:** **post office**
- Compound nouns can have different structures:
  - **Noun + Noun**
  - **Adjective + Noun**
  - **Verb + Noun**
  - **Preposition + Noun**
- The plural is generally formed on the **main word** of the compound.
- Many compound nouns place the main stress on the **first element**.

---

# 🎯 Why Are Compound Nouns Important?

Compound nouns allow us to express **complex ideas and specific concepts concisely**.

Instead of using several words to describe an object, activity, or concept, English can combine words into a single lexical unit.

### Example

> **coffee + table → coffee table**

> **bed + room → bedroom**

> **air + port → airport**

> **sun + flower → sunflower**

Understanding compound nouns helps you recognize vocabulary more easily and use English more **precisely, naturally, and efficiently**.
