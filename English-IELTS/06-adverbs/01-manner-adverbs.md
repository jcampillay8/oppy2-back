# Manner Adverbs

Manner adverbs describe **how an action is performed**. They provide information about the way something happens or is done.

## 1. Definition

A manner adverb modifies a **verb** by explaining the way an action is performed.

It usually answers the question:

> **How?**

Example:

- She sings **beautifully**.
- He writes **quickly**.
- They worked **carefully**.

In these sentences, the adverbs explain how the actions are performed.

## 2. Manner Adverbs and Verbs

The most common function of a manner adverb is to modify a verb.

Examples:

- He drives **carefully**.
- She speaks **quietly**.
- They walked **slowly**.
- The dog ran **quickly**.
- He answered the question **correctly**.

### How to identify the manner adverb

Ask:

> **How did the subject perform the action?**

Example:

- She danced **gracefully**.

Question:

- How did she dance?

Answer:

- **Gracefully**

Therefore, "gracefully" is a manner adverb.

## 3. Common Manner Adverbs

Some common manner adverbs include:

- slowly
- quickly
- carefully
- quietly
- loudly
- gently
- happily
- sadly
- gracefully
- skillfully
- easily
- correctly
- badly
- well
- politely
- angrily
- patiently
- seriously

Examples:

- He spoke **quietly**.
- She completed the task **carefully**.
- They worked **efficiently**.
- The student answered **correctly**.
- He plays the guitar **skillfully**.

## 4. Forming Manner Adverbs

Many manner adverbs are formed by adding **-ly** to an adjective.

| Adjective | Manner Adverb |
|---|---|
| quick | quickly |
| slow | slowly |
| careful | carefully |
| beautiful | beautifully |
| quiet | quietly |
| polite | politely |
| graceful | gracefully |
| skillful | skillfully |

Examples:

- He is a **careful** driver.
- He drives **carefully**.

- She is a **beautiful** singer.
- She sings **beautifully**.

## 5. Spelling Changes

Some adjectives require spelling changes when forming the adverb.

### Adjectives ending in -y

Change **-y** to **-i** and add **-ly**.

- happy → happily
- easy → easily
- angry → angrily

Examples:

- She smiled **happily**.
- He completed the exercise **easily**.

### Adjectives ending in -le

The ending often changes to **-ly**.

- gentle → gently
- simple → simply
- possible → possibly

Examples:

- He touched the object **gently**.
- She explained the problem **simply**.

## 6. Irregular Manner Adverbs

Not every manner adverb is formed with **-ly**.

Some important examples are:

- good → well
- fast → fast
- hard → hard
- late → late
- early → early

Examples:

- She sings **well**.
- He runs **fast**.
- They worked **hard**.
- He arrived **late**.
- She arrived **early**.

Be careful with **hard** and **hardly**:

- He works **hard**. → He works with a lot of effort.
- He **hardly** works. → He works almost not at all.

## 7. Position in a Sentence

Manner adverbs can appear in different positions, depending on the sentence and the emphasis.

### After the verb

- She sings **beautifully**.
- He drives **carefully**.

### After the object

- She opened the door **slowly**.
- He completed the assignment **carefully**.

### Before the verb

This is possible in some contexts, especially for emphasis:

- She **quietly** entered the room.
- He **carefully** examined the document.

The position can change the emphasis, but the meaning remains related to **how the action is performed**.

## 8. Manner Adverbs vs. Adjectives

A common mistake is confusing an adjective with an adverb.

### Adjective

Describes a **noun**.

- She is a **careful** driver.

"Careful" describes the noun "driver."

### Manner adverb

Describes a **verb**.

- She drives **carefully**.

"Carefully" describes how she drives.

Compare:

- He is a **slow** runner.
- He runs **slowly**.

- She is a **beautiful** singer.
- She sings **beautifully**.

## 9. Manner Adverbs Can Modify Adjectives or Other Adverbs

Some words traditionally grouped as adverbs can modify adjectives or other adverbs.

Examples:

- She is **extremely** talented.
- He is **unusually** calm.
- He runs **very** quickly.
- She speaks **quite** softly.

However, in these examples, **extremely**, **very**, and **quite** are more precisely classified as **degree adverbs**, not manner adverbs.

The distinction is important:

- **Manner adverb** → tells us **how** an action happens.
- **Degree adverb** → tells us **how much / to what degree**.

Example:

- She sings **beautifully**. → manner
- She sings **very** beautifully. → "very" expresses degree

## Quick Summary

**Manner adverbs describe how an action is performed.**

Main question:

> **How?**

Examples:

- drive **carefully**
- speak **quietly**
- run **quickly**
- work **hard**
- sing **beautifully**
- answer **correctly**

Basic structure:

**Subject + Verb + Manner Adverb**

- She sings **beautifully**.
- He drives **carefully**.
- They worked **efficiently**.

Remember:

**Adjective → describes a noun**

- a **careful** driver

**Manner adverb → describes how a verb is performed**

- He drives **carefully**.
