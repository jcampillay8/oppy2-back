# Comparison Adverbs

Comparison adverbs, also called **adverbs of comparison**, are used to compare the **degree, intensity, or manner** of an action or quality between two or more people, things, or situations.

They can express that something happens **more**, **less**, or **to the greatest/lowest degree** compared with something else.

## 1. Definition

Comparison adverbs modify:

- Verbs
- Adjectives
- Other adverbs

They are used to express differences in degree or intensity.

Examples:

- She runs **more quickly** than me.
- He speaks **more fluently** now.
- She works **less efficiently** than before.

## 2. Comparative Form

The **comparative form** is normally used when comparing **two** people, things, actions, or situations.

For many adverbs, the comparative is formed with:

**more + adverb**

Examples:

- quickly → **more quickly**
- slowly → **more slowly**
- carefully → **more carefully**
- fluently → **more fluently**
- efficiently → **more efficiently**

Examples:

- She runs **more quickly** than her brother.
- He speaks **more fluently** than before.
- They work **more efficiently** than the previous team.
- She completed the task **more carefully** than I did.

## 3. Comparative with "Than"

The word **than** is commonly used to introduce the second element of the comparison.

Structure:

**Subject + Verb + Comparative Adverb + than + comparison**

Examples:

- She runs **faster than** me.
- He works **more efficiently than** his colleague.
- She speaks **more clearly than** her teacher.
- They arrived **earlier than** us.

## 4. Superlative Form

The **superlative form** is used when comparing **three or more** people, things, actions, or situations.

For many adverbs, the superlative is formed with:

**the most + adverb**

Examples:

- quickly → **the most quickly**
- slowly → **the most slowly**
- carefully → **the most carefully**
- fluently → **the most fluently**
- efficiently → **the most efficiently**

Examples:

- She runs **the most quickly** of all the athletes.
- He speaks **the most fluently** in the group.
- She completed the task **the most carefully**.
- They worked **the most efficiently**.

## 5. Irregular Comparison

Some common adverbs have irregular comparative and superlative forms.

| Adverb | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| well | better | best |
| badly | worse | worst |
| little | less | least |
| much | more | most |
| far | farther/further | farthest/furthest |

Examples:

- She sings **well**.
- She sings **better** than me.
- She sings **the best** in the group.

Another example:

- He performed **badly**.
- He performed **worse** than yesterday.
- He performed **the worst** of everyone.

## 6. Short Adverbs

Some short adverbs use **-er** and **-est** instead of "more" and "most."

Examples:

- fast → faster → fastest
- hard → harder → hardest
- early → earlier → earliest
- soon → sooner → soonest

Examples:

- He runs **faster** than me.
- She arrived **earlier** than expected.
- He works **harder** than his colleague.
- They arrived **the earliest**.

## 7. Comparison Adverbs with Other Adverbs

A comparison adverb can modify another adverb.

Examples:

- He runs **more quickly** now.
- She speaks **more softly** than before.
- They worked **more efficiently** this year.
- He drives **less carefully** than his brother.

Here:

- more/less → expresses the comparison
- quickly/softly/efficiently/carefully → describes the manner

## 8. Comparison Adverbs with Adjectives

Be careful: when **more** or **most** modifies an adjective, it is functioning as a **degree/comparison modifier**, not as an adverb of manner.

Examples:

- She is **more beautiful** than her sister.
- This task is **more difficult** than the previous one.
- This is **the most interesting** book.

The comparison is between **qualities**, not between actions.

## 9. "Less" and "Least"

Comparison can also express a **lower degree**.

### Comparative

**less + adverb**

- He works **less efficiently** than before.
- She speaks **less clearly** than her colleague.

### Superlative

**the least + adverb**

- He works **the least efficiently** in the department.
- She speaks **the least clearly** of everyone.

## 10. "More" vs. "Better"

These are not interchangeable.

**More** is generally used with longer adverbs:

- She speaks **more fluently** than me.
- He works **more efficiently** than before.

**Better** is the irregular comparative of **well**:

- She sings **better** than me.
- He performed **better** this time.

Compare:

- She sings **more beautifully** than her sister.
- She sings **better** than her sister.

"More beautifully" compares the manner or quality of the singing, while "better" is the comparative form of "well."

## 12. Comparison Adverbs vs. Degree Adverbs

These two categories can overlap, but their functions are different.

### Degree

Indicates **how much or to what extent**.

- He runs **very** quickly.
- She speaks **extremely** softly.

### Comparison

Compares one action or quality with another.

- He runs **more quickly** than me.
- She speaks **more softly** than before.

Compare:

> He runs **very quickly**.

- very → degree
- quickly → manner

> He runs **more quickly than me**.

- more quickly → comparative expression
- than me → comparison

## Quick Summary

**Comparison adverbs express differences in the degree, intensity, or manner of actions or qualities.**

### Comparative

Used mainly for **two**:

**more + adverb + than**

- She runs **more quickly than** me.
- He works **more efficiently than** before.

Or with short/irregular adverbs:

- He runs **faster than** me.
- She sings **better than** me.

### Superlative

Used mainly for **three or more**:

**the most + adverb**

- She runs **the most quickly** in the group.
- He works **the most efficiently** of all.

Or with short/irregular adverbs:

- He runs **the fastest**.
- She sings **the best**.

Remember:

**Degree → How much?**

- He runs **very** quickly.

**Comparison → Compared with whom/what?**

- He runs **faster than** me.

**Superlative → Who/what has the highest or lowest degree?**

- He runs **the fastest** in the group.
