# Degree Adverbs

Degree adverbs, also called **adverbs of degree** or **intensifiers**, indicate the **extent, intensity, or degree** of an action, quality, or characteristic.

They help us express **how much**, **how strongly**, or **to what extent** something happens or is true.

## 1. Definition

Degree adverbs modify:

- Adjectives
- Verbs
- Other adverbs

They answer questions such as:

> **To what extent?**

> **How much?**

> **How strongly?**

Examples:

- She is **very** happy.
- He runs **extremely** fast.
- They are **quite** tired.
- I **almost** forgot.

## 2. Degree Adverbs Modifying Adjectives

Degree adverbs can indicate the intensity of an adjective.

Examples:

- She is **very** happy.
- It is **extremely** cold.
- He is **fairly** tall.
- The movie was **quite** interesting.
- The task is **incredibly** difficult.

Compare:

- She is **tired**.
- She is **very tired**.
- She is **extremely tired**.

The adjective remains "tired", while the degree adverb changes the **intensity**.

## 3. Degree Adverbs Modifying Other Adverbs

Degree adverbs can modify another adverb.

Examples:

- He runs **very** quickly.
- She speaks **quite** softly.
- He answered **extremely** carefully.
- They worked **remarkably** efficiently.

In:

> He runs **very quickly**.

- quickly → manner adverb
- very → degree adverb

"Very" tells us the **degree** of "quickly."

## 4. Degree Adverbs Modifying Verbs

Some degree adverbs can modify verbs by indicating the extent to which an action occurs.

Examples:

- I **almost** fell.
- She **completely** forgot.
- He **barely** passed.
- They **nearly** missed the train.
- I **really** like this book.

These adverbs provide information about the **extent or completeness** of the action.

## 6. Intensifying vs. Softening

Degree adverbs can either **increase** or **reduce** the intensity of a word.

### Intensifying

These make the meaning stronger:

- very
- extremely
- incredibly
- absolutely
- remarkably
- really
- completely

Examples:

- She is **very** talented.
- He is **extremely** intelligent.
- The movie was **incredibly** interesting.
- I **completely** agree.

### Softening

These make the meaning weaker or less absolute:

- slightly
- somewhat
- fairly
- rather
- quite

Examples:

- I am **slightly** tired.
- She is **somewhat** nervous.
- The test was **fairly** difficult.
- He is **quite** confident.

## 7. Degree Adverbs and Adjectives

A useful structure is:

**Degree Adverb + Adjective**

Examples:

- **very** happy
- **extremely** difficult
- **quite** interesting
- **fairly** easy
- **slightly** nervous

Sentence examples:

- She is **very happy**.
- The exam was **extremely difficult**.
- This book is **quite interesting**.
- The exercise is **fairly easy**.

## 8. Degree Adverbs and Other Adverbs

Another common structure is:

**Degree Adverb + Adverb**

Examples:

- **very** quickly
- **quite** slowly
- **extremely** carefully
- **remarkably** well

Examples:

- He runs **very quickly**.
- She speaks **quite slowly**.
- He completed the task **extremely carefully**.
- She performed **remarkably well**.

## 9. Important Difference: Degree vs. Manner

Degree adverbs and manner adverbs can appear together, but they have different functions.

### Manner adverb

Answers:

> **How?**

Example:

- He runs **quickly**.

"Quickly" describes **how** he runs.

### Degree adverb

Answers:

> **To what extent?**

Example:

- He runs **very** quickly.

"Very" indicates the **degree** of "quickly."

Therefore:

> He runs **very quickly**.

- very → degree
- quickly → manner

Another example:

> She speaks **extremely softly**.

- extremely → degree
- softly → manner

## 10. Important Difference: Very vs. Too

**Very** increases intensity but does not necessarily indicate a problem.

- The coffee is **very hot**.

**Too** means more than is desirable, necessary, or possible.

- The coffee is **too hot** to drink.

Compare:

- The bag is **very heavy**. → It has a high weight.
- The bag is **too heavy**. → Its weight is excessive for the situation.

## 11. Degree Adverbs and Comparatives

Some degree adverbs can modify comparative adjectives.

Examples:

- much better
- far better
- slightly better
- a little better
- considerably more expensive

Examples:

- This solution is **much better**.
- The new computer is **far more powerful**.
- This option is **slightly cheaper**.
- The second test was **considerably more difficult**.

## 12. Degree Adverbs and Superlatives

Degree adverbs can also emphasize superlative forms.

Examples:

- by far the best
- easily the most interesting
- quite the best

Example:

- This is **by far the best** option.

## Quick Summary

**Degree adverbs indicate the extent, intensity, or degree of something.**

Main questions:

> **How much?**

> **To what extent?**

> **How strongly?**

Common degree adverbs:

- very
- extremely
- incredibly
- really
- quite
- fairly
- rather
- somewhat
- slightly
- too
- so
- almost
- nearly
- completely
- absolutely
- barely

Basic structures:

**Degree Adverb + Adjective**

- **very** happy
- **extremely** difficult

**Degree Adverb + Manner Adverb**

- **very** quickly
- **extremely** carefully

**Degree Adverb + Comparative**

- **much** better
- **slightly** cheaper

Remember:

**Manner → How?**

- He runs **quickly**.

**Time → When?**

- He runs **today**.

**Place → Where?**

- He runs **outside**.

**Degree → To what extent?**

- He runs **very** quickly.
